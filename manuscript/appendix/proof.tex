\section{Proof Details}
\label{app:proofs}

Unless stated otherwise, all proofs use Assumption~\ref{ass:honest-separation}:
every nuisance, ratio, candidate function, and coefficient is fixed before the
independent RCT and OBS evaluation observations are used.

\subsection{Conditional validity of the trial pseudo-outcome}

Fix \(q=(q_0,q_1)\) without using the evaluation samples and condition on
\(X\). Under trial randomization, consistency, and honest sample separation,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_R\left[\frac{A}{e(X)}\{Y-q_1(X)\}\mid X\right]
&=\mathbb E_R\{Y(1)-q_1(X)\mid X\},
\\
\mathbb E_R\left[\frac{1-A}{1-e(X)}\{Y-q_0(X)\}\mid X\right]
&=\mathbb E_R\{Y(0)-q_0(X)\mid X\}.
\end{align}
Substituting these two equalities into the definition of \(\varphi\) cancels the
two fitted regressions and yields
\(\mathbb E_R\{\varphi(V;q)\mid X\}=\tau(X)\).  Applying the identity to
\(q=\widehat\mu_R\), \(q=\widehat\mu_O\), and their affine blend gives
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda\mid X)&=\tau(X),
&
\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)&=0.
\end{align}

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:rct-only-aipw}}

Apply Equation~\eqref{eq:pseudo-valid} with the nuisance-sample fitted pair
\(q=\widehat\mu_R\), average over \(D_R^{\mathrm{eval}}\), and use
iterated expectation. This gives
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E\{\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{AIPW},R}\}
&=\mathbb E_R\{\tau(X)\}=\theta.
\end{align*}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:ate-nuisance-sensitivity}}

For a pair direction \(h_q=(h_{q,0},h_{q,1})\), direct differentiation gives
\begin{align*}
D_qP_R\{\varphi(V;q)\}[h_q]
&=P_R\left[
h_{q,1}(X)-h_{q,0}(X)
-\frac{A}{e_0(X)}h_{q,1}(X)
+\frac{1-A}{1-e_0(X)}h_{q,0}(X)
\right]
=0,
\end{align*}
because \(P_R(A\mid X)=e_0(X)\). Thus perturbing the fixed outcome
regression does not change \(\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda\mid X)=\tau(X)\). For a fitted-prediction path
\(\widehat g_\varepsilon=\widehat g+\varepsilon u\), exact transport gives
\begin{align*}
\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}
\omega\{P_O(r_0\widehat g_\varepsilon)-P_R(\widehat g_\varepsilon)\}
\right|_{\varepsilon=0}
&=\omega\{P_O(r_0u)-P_R(u)\}=0.
\end{align*}
For an admissible ratio path \(r_\varepsilon=r_0+\varepsilon h\), differentiation under
the finite expectation gives
\begin{align*}
\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}
\omega P_O(r_\varepsilon\widehat g)\right|_{\varepsilon=0}
&=\omega P_O(h\widehat g),
\end{align*}
which proves Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-ratio-gateaux}. This derivative is generally
nonzero because exact transport imposes no general restriction forcing
\(P_O(h\widehat g)=0\); particular directions or coefficients can still make
it vanish.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-variance}}

Using \(\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda)=\theta\) and exact transport,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_O\{r(X)g(X)\}=\mathbb E_R\{g(X)\},
\end{align}
the evaluation-sample mean of Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-estimator} is \(\theta\). Rewrite
the centered estimator as
\begin{align}
\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)-\theta
&=
(\mathbb P_{R,n}-P_R)\{Z_\lambda-\omega g\}
+(\mathbb P_{O,N}-P_O)\{\omega rg\}.
\end{align}
This is Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-fixed-error-decomposition} with the fixed
coefficient \(\omega\) moved inside the second empirical process.
The two summands are independent because \(D_R^{\mathrm{eval}}\) and
\(D_O^{\mathrm{eval}}\) are
independent. The variance of
an empirical mean of \(m\) independent observations is the population variance
divided by \(m\), which proves Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-variance}.  Unbiased sample
variances on the two independent evaluation blocks therefore give the displayed
fixed-selected-coefficient variance estimator in Section~\ref{sec:ate}. Coverage
of its Wald interval additionally uses the stated evaluation-sample Lindeberg and
moment assumptions; variance unbiasedness alone is not a central limit theorem.

Because \(g(X)\) is fixed given \(X\),
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\omega g(X)\mid X\}
&=\tau(X)-\omega g(X),
&
\operatorname{Var}_R\{Z_\lambda-\omega g(X)\mid X\}
&=\operatorname{Var}_R(Z_\lambda\mid X).
\end{align*}
Applying the law of total variance proves Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-within-between}.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:ate-variance-quadratic}}

Expand the first variance in Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-variance} using
\(Z_\lambda=Z_0+\lambda\Delta\).  After collecting terms and multiplying by
\(n\), the coefficient-dependent part is
\begin{align}
2C\lambda-2D\omega+A\lambda^2-2E\lambda\omega+B\omega^2,
\end{align}
which is \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\boldsymbol\beta)\). This proves
Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-quadratic}. The representation
\begin{align}
\begin{pmatrix}u&v\end{pmatrix}\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix}
&=\operatorname{Var}_R(u\Delta-vg)
+\frac nN v^2\operatorname{Var}_O(rg)
\end{align}
proves positive semidefiniteness.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:ate-argmin-equivalence}}

Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-quadratic} is the positive affine transformation
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\boldsymbol\beta)\}
&=\frac1n\operatorname{Var}_R(Z_0)
+\frac1n\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\boldsymbol\beta),
\end{align*}
where \(n>0\). Adding a constant and multiplying by a positive scalar preserve
the complete argmin set over every feasible set.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:joint-oracle}}

If \(AB-E^2>0\), \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is positive definite. The
first-order condition for \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is
\(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\boldsymbol\beta=-\boldsymbol\ell\), which has the
unique
solution
\begin{align*}
\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star
&=-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell
=
\begin{pmatrix}
(ED-BC)/(AB-E^2)\\
(AD-EC)/(AB-E^2)
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align*}
For \(\rho>0\), the ridge objective has gradient
\begin{align*}
\nabla_{\boldsymbol\beta}
\left\{\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\boldsymbol\beta)
+\rho\lVert\boldsymbol\beta\rVert_2^2\right\}
&=2\boldsymbol\ell
+2(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}+\rho I_2)\boldsymbol\beta.
\end{align*}
Positive semidefiniteness of \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) makes
\(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}+\rho I_2\) positive definite. Setting the gradient
to zero proves Equation~\eqref{eq:ridge-oracle} and uniqueness.

\subsection{Singular and constrained extensions}

When \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is singular, nonnegativity of the variance quadratic implies
\(\boldsymbol\ell\in\operatorname{Range}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})\); otherwise a null-space direction
would make the expression arbitrarily negative.  The complete minimizer set is
therefore \(-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^+\boldsymbol\ell+\operatorname{Null}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})\).  Continuity of the
quadratic proves attainment on every prespecified compact feasible set.

For completeness, suppose \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is positive definite and write
\begin{align*}
\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\boldsymbol\beta)
&=2\boldsymbol\ell^\top\boldsymbol\beta
+\boldsymbol\beta^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\boldsymbol\beta,
&
\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star
&=-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell.
\end{align*}
Completing the square gives
\begin{align*}
\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\boldsymbol\beta)
-\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star)
&=(\boldsymbol\beta-\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star)^\top
\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}
(\boldsymbol\beta-\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star).
\end{align*}
The norm induced by a positive-definite matrix is equivalent to the Euclidean
norm.  Hence this distance is coercive and strictly convex.  On every nonempty
closed convex feasible set, it therefore attains a unique minimum, namely the
\(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\)-metric projection of
\(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star\).

If \(A=\operatorname{Var}_R(\Delta)=0\), then \(\Delta\) is constant almost
surely, and \(\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)=0\) forces that constant to be zero.
If
\begin{align*}
B
&=\operatorname{Var}_R(g)+\frac nN\operatorname{Var}_O(rg)=0,
\end{align*}
then \(g\) is constant \(P_R\)-almost surely and \(rg\) is constant
\(P_O\)-almost surely.  Exact transport gives
\(P_O(rg)=P_R(g)\), so the two constants agree and
\(\mathbb P_{O,N}(rg)-\mathbb P_{R,n}(g)=0\) for every evaluation sample.
\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:joint-oracle-variance}}

Write the evaluation-sample variance as
\begin{align*}
V(\boldsymbol\beta)
&=
\mathcal V_0+
\frac1n
\{2\boldsymbol\ell^\top\boldsymbol\beta
+\boldsymbol\beta^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\boldsymbol\beta\},
&
\mathcal V_0
&=
\frac1n\operatorname{Var}_R(Z_0).
\end{align*}
When \(AB-E^2>0\), the matrix \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is positive
definite and the unique minimizer is
\(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star
=-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell\). Substitution gives
\begin{align*}
V(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star)
&=
\mathcal V_0-
\frac1n\boldsymbol\ell^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell.
\end{align*}
For \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&-E\\-E&B\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
\begin{align*}
\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}
&=
\frac1{AB-E^2}
\begin{pmatrix}B&E\\E&A\end{pmatrix},
\\
\boldsymbol\ell^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell
&=
\frac{BC^2-2ECD+AD^2}{AB-E^2}.
\end{align*}
Positive definiteness makes this quadratic form nonnegative and makes it zero
exactly when \(\boldsymbol\ell=0\).

\paragraph{Singular variance reduction and compact constraints.}
If \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is singular, boundedness below of the variance objective implies \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\operatorname{Range}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})\). Evaluating at the minimum-norm solution \(-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^+\boldsymbol\ell\) gives
\begin{align*}
V_{\min}
&=
\mathcal V_0-
\frac1n\boldsymbol\ell^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^+\boldsymbol\ell.
\end{align*}
The pseudoinverse is positive definite on
\(\operatorname{Range}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})\), so the reduction is
strictly positive exactly when \(\boldsymbol\ell\neq0\). Finally, continuity
gives attainment on every nonempty compact feasible set \(\Theta\). If
\(0\in\Theta\), the constrained minimum is at most
\(V(0)=\mathcal V_0\), and it is strictly smaller exactly when some feasible
\(\boldsymbol\beta\) makes
\(2\boldsymbol\ell^\top\boldsymbol\beta
+\boldsymbol\beta^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\boldsymbol\beta<0\).

\paragraph{Separable specialization.}

Equation~\eqref{eq:pseudo-valid} gives
\(\mathbb E_R(Z_a\mid X)=\tau(X)\) for \(a\in\{0,1\}\). Subtracting the two
equalities yields \(\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)=0\). Since \(g\) is a fixed
function of \(X\), iterated expectation gives
\begin{align}
E
&=
\mathbb E_R\left[
\{g(X)-\mathbb E_Rg(X)\}\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)
\right]
=0,
\\
D
&=
\operatorname{Cov}_R\{\mathbb E_R(Z_0\mid X),g(X)\}
=
\operatorname{Cov}_R\{\tau(X),g(X)\}.
\label{eq:ate-population-orthogonality}
\end{align}
Substituting \(E=0\) into the coefficient objective and completing the two
scalar squares gives
\begin{align*}
\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\boldsymbol\beta)
&=
A\left(\lambda+\frac CA\right)^2
+B\left(\omega-\frac DB\right)^2
-\frac{C^2}{A}-\frac{D^2}{B}.
\end{align*}
The two minimizers are therefore \(-C/A\) and \(D/B\), proving
Equation~\eqref{eq:axis-oracle}. Substitution into the variance in
Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-quadratic} gives
\begin{align*}
V_{\min}^{\mathrm{sep}}
&=
\mathcal V_0-
\frac1n\left(\frac{C^2}{A}+\frac{D^2}{B}\right),
\end{align*}
which proves Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-separable-minimum}.

\subsection{Prediction-powered CATE losses and finite-sample theory}

Fix \(t,\lambda,\omega,g,r_0\) before using the fitting sample. Conditional
on \(X\), pseudo-outcome validity gives
\(\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda-\tau(X)\mid X)=0\), and hence
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-t(X)\}^2
&=
\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\tau(X)\}^2
+\mathbb E_R\{t(X)-\tau(X)\}^2.
\end{align}
Exact transport also gives
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_O\{r_0(X)(g(X)-t(X))^2\}
&=\mathbb E_R\{g(X)-t(X)\}^2.
\end{align}
The correction in Equation~\eqref{eq:dr-fusion-loss} therefore has mean zero,
which proves Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target}.

For the RF identity, expand conditionally on \(X\). Randomization
implies
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_R[(A-e_0)\{Y-m(X)\}\mid X]
&=e_0(X)\{1-e_0(X)\}\tau(X),\\
\mathbb E_R[\{A-e_0(X)\}^2\mid X]
&=e_0(X)\{1-e_0(X)\}.
\end{align*}
It follows that
\begin{align*}
\mathcal L_R(\zeta;m)
&=Q_R(m)+\mathcal R(\zeta).
\end{align*}
Because \(\mathbb E_R(\kappa\mid X)=1\) and exact transport holds, the RF
correction has mean zero. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:rf-target}.

\subsection{Proofs for general two-source learning}

For Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-general-erm}, exact transport and the target
identities in Section~\ref{sec:cate-loss} imply
\begin{align*}
L_j(\zeta)-L_j(\zeta_j^\circ)
&=\mathcal R(\zeta)-\mathcal R(\zeta_j^\circ).
\end{align*}
The global approximate-ERM inequality gives
\begin{align*}
\widehat L_j(\widehat\zeta_j)+\operatorname{Pen}(\widehat\zeta_j)
&\leq
\widehat L_j(\zeta_j^\circ)+\operatorname{Pen}(\zeta_j^\circ)
+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}.
\end{align*}
Add and subtract the two population losses.  By the definition of the
centered comparator process, the resulting stochastic term is exactly
\(-\mathbb Z_j(\widehat\zeta_j)\).  This proves
Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-general-erm-exact}, including its sign and without an
extra factor two.

For Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-general-localized}, work on the intersection of the
two sourcewise modulus events.  Independence and a union bound give probability
at least \(1-2\delta\).  The exact ERM reduction shows that any approximate
minimizer with excess risk in a peeling shell
\(2^m u<\mathcal R(\zeta)-\mathcal R(\zeta_j^\circ)\leq2^{m+1}u\)
must satisfy an upper bound by the sum of the two source moduli, the penalty at
the comparator, and \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}\).  Sub-root regularity and the
definition of \(u_{j,n_f,N_f}^\star\) make that upper bound strictly smaller
than the shell's lower endpoint once \(u\) is a sufficiently large universal
multiple of
\(u_{j,n_f,N_f}^\star+\operatorname{Pen}(\zeta_j^\circ)
+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}\). For each fixed \(\delta\), the full-modulus
sub-root condition gives
\(\Phi_{j,n_f,N_f}(tu;\delta)\leq
\sqrt{t}\,\Phi_{j,n_f,N_f}(u;\delta)\) for every \(t\geq1\).
Thus the stochastic upper bound grows only as the square root of the peeling
level, whereas the shell's excess-risk lower endpoint grows linearly. Summing the
geometric tail allocation over shells proves
Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-general-localized}.  Separability permits the displayed
suprema to be taken over countable dense subclasses; otherwise the same
argument is understood in outer probability.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-general-estimated-r}, condition on the
ratio-training sample.  The preceding argument now uses the actual
\(\widehat r\)-weighted source process, hence its fixed point is
\(u_{j,n_f,N_f}^\star(\widehat r;\delta)\).  Add and subtract the exact-ratio
population loss at \(\zeta\) and \(\zeta_j^\circ\).  Their increment is bounded
by \(\Delta_j^{\mathrm{fit}}\), after which the same localization argument
closes at \(\bar u\).  If the two stated \(L^2(P_O)\) bounds hold,
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{align*}
\Delta_j^{\mathrm{fit}}(u;\widehat r)
&\leq |\omega|\|\widehat r-r_0\|_{L^2(P_O)}
\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z_j(u)}
\|G_\zeta-G_{\zeta_j^\circ}\|_{L^2(P_O)},
\end{align*}
which proves the final claim.

\subsection{Proofs for coefficient selection}

For Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-noise-floor-lambda}, pseudo-outcome validity
gives \(\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)=0\).  Therefore
\begin{align*}
Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)
&=Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(0)
+2\lambda\mathbb E_R[(Z_0-\tau)\Delta]
+\lambda^2\mathbb E_R(\Delta^2),\\
\mathbb E_R[(Z_0-\tau)\Delta]
&=\mathbb E_R(Z_0\Delta).
\end{align*}
Completing the square gives Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-dr-noise-lambda} when
\(\mathbb E_R(\Delta^2)>0\).  If \(\mathbb E_R(\Delta^2)=0\), then
\(\Delta=0\) almost surely and the objective is flat.

For the R loss, write
\(s_0\triangleq e_0(1-e_0)\) and
\(U_R\triangleq Y-m_R-(A-e_0)\tau\).  Since
\(m_\lambda=m_R+\lambda d_m\),
\begin{align*}
Q_R(m_\lambda)
&=Q_R(m_R)
-2\lambda\mathbb E_R(U_Rd_m/s_0)
+\lambda^2\mathbb E_R(d_m^2/s_0),\\
\mathbb E_R(U_Rd_m/s_0)
&=\mathbb E_R[\{Y-m_R\}d_m/s_0].
\end{align*}
The second identity follows from
\(\mathbb E_R(A-e_0\mid X)=0\).  Completing the square proves
Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-r-noise-lambda}.  If
\(\mathbb E_R(d_m^2/s_0)=0\), strict overlap implies \(d_m=0\) almost surely
and the objective is flat.  A convex
quadratic minimized over \([0,1]\) is minimized by projecting its
unrestricted minimizer onto that interval.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-omega-flatness}, exact transport gives
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_O(r_0G_\zeta)-\mathbb E_R(G_\zeta)=0.
\end{align*}
Moreover, conditional randomization and strict overlap give
\(\mathbb E_R(\kappa\mid X)=1\), so
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_R(\kappa G_\zeta)
&=\mathbb E_R[\mathbb E_R(\kappa\mid X)G_\zeta]
=\mathbb E_R(G_\zeta),\\
\mathbb E_O(r_0G_\zeta)-\mathbb E_R(\kappa G_\zeta)
&=0.
\end{align*}
Substitution into Equations~\eqref{eq:dr-fusion-loss} and
\eqref{eq:rf-fusion-loss} leaves
\(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda)\) and
\(\mathcal L_R(\zeta;m_\lambda)\), respectively, for every
\(\omega\in\Omega\).  Each objective is therefore constant on the nonempty
set \(\Omega\), and its minimizer set is all of \(\Omega\).

For Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-validation-identity}, condition on the fitted
grid function at each \((\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j\).  Conditional
pseudo-outcome validity gives
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_R\{Z_0-\widehat\tau_j(X;\lambda,\omega)\}^2
&=Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(0)
+\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)\}.
\end{align*}
Exact transport gives
\(\mathbb E_O(r_0G_{j,\lambda,\omega})
=\mathbb E_R(G_{j,\lambda,\omega})\), so the term multiplied by \(\omega\)
has mean zero.  This proves Equation~\eqref{eq:common-reference-identity}.

\subsection{Proofs for the orthogonality audit}

Condition throughout on the auxiliary samples and algorithmic randomness, so
all nuisances, \(\lambda\), and \(\omega\) are fixed before the independent
loss observations are used. Assume strict trial overlap, exact transport for
claims at \(r_0\), and sufficient integrability and domination to interchange
Gateaux differentiation and expectation. Directions belong to the tangent
sets stated in the main text.

Differentiating Equations~\eqref{eq:dr-fusion-loss} and
\eqref{eq:rf-fusion-loss} in the target direction \(v\) gives
Equations~\eqref{eq:drf-learning-equation} and
\eqref{eq:rf-learning-equation}. For \(r=r_0\), exact transport gives
\(\mathbb E_O(r_0f)=\mathbb E_R(f)\) for each integrable \(f(X)\). Also,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_R(\kappa_0\mid X)
&=\mathbb E_R(\kappa\mid X)
=\frac{\mathbb E_R[\{A-e_0(X)\}^2\mid X]}{s_0(X)}=1.
\end{align*}
The two correction terms in each learning equation therefore cancel. This
proves Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-learning-equations}.

For the \(q\)-direction in Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-orthogonality-audit},
let \(q_\varepsilon=q+\varepsilon h\). Direct differentiation gives
\begin{align*}
\dot Z_q[h]
&=-\{A-e_0(X)\}\left\{\frac{h_1(X)}{e_0(X)}
+\frac{h_0(X)}{1-e_0(X)}\right\}.
\end{align*}
Because \(h\) and \(v\) are functions of \(X\) and
\(\mathbb E_R(A-e_0\mid X)=0\),
\(\mathbb E_R\{v(X)\dot Z_q[h]\}=0\).

For the \(m\)-direction, direct differentiation of the first term of
Equation~\eqref{eq:rf-learning-equation} gives
\begin{align*}
D_m\Psi_{\mathrm{RF}}[h,v]
&=-\mathbb E_R\left[v(X)\frac{A-e_0(X)}{s_0(X)}h(X)\right]=0.
\end{align*}
The last equality again uses conditional randomization.

For a prediction path \(g_\varepsilon=g+\varepsilon u\), only the transported
correction changes. Exact transport, and \(\mathbb E_R(\kappa_0\mid X)=1\)
for RF, give
\begin{align*}
D_g\Psi_j[u,v]
&=\omega\mathbb E_O[\{r-r_0\}uv],\\
D_g\mathbb E(\widehat L_j)[u]
&=2\omega\mathbb E_O[\{r-r_0\}(g-\zeta)u],
\end{align*}
which proves Equations~\eqref{eq:cate-g-score-derivative} and
\eqref{eq:cate-g-loss-derivative}. A ratio path
\(r_\varepsilon=r+\varepsilon h_r\) affects only the positive OBS term, so
\begin{align*}
D_r\Psi_j[h_r,v]
&=\omega\mathbb E_O[h_rv(g-\zeta)],\\
D_r\mathbb E(\widehat L_j)[h_r]
&=\omega\mathbb E_O[h_r(g-\zeta)^2].
\end{align*}
This proves Equations~\eqref{eq:cate-ratio-score-derivative} and
\eqref{eq:drf-ratio-derivative}.

To prove Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-ratio-nonorthogonality}, take any
\(f=v(g-\zeta)\in L^2(P_O^X)\). Its inner product with every element of
\(L_0^2(P_O^X)\) is zero if and only if \(f\) lies in the orthogonal
complement of that space, which consists of the almost-sure constants.
For example, the centered direction \(h=f-\mathbb E_O(f)\) has pairing
\(\mathbb E_O(hf)=\operatorname{Var}_O(f)\). Multiplication by nonzero
\(\omega\) does not change the zero set.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-sieve-normal-equations}, expand each quadratic
objective in \(\beta\).  The DRF curvature and right-hand side are
\((1-\omega)\widehat\Gamma_R+\omega\widehat\Gamma_{O,r}+\rho P\) and
\(\widehat h_\lambda+\omega\widehat d_g\).  The RF counterparts are
\((1-\omega)\widehat\Gamma_{R,\kappa}
+\omega\widehat\Gamma_{O,r}+\rho P\) and
\(\widehat h_{m_\lambda}+\omega\widehat d_{g,\kappa}\).  Setting the
gradients to zero proves Equations~\eqref{eq:linear-sieve} and
\eqref{eq:linear-sieve-rf}.  For \(0\leq\omega\leq1\), each unpenalized
Hessian is a convex combination of positive-semidefinite weighted Gram
matrices; adding \(\rho P\succeq0\) preserves positive semidefiniteness.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-sieve-lambda-profile}, fixed \(\omega\) makes
the Hessian independent of \(\lambda\), while
\(Z_\lambda=Z_0+\lambda\Delta\) and
\(m_\lambda=m_R+\lambda d_m\) make the right-hand side affine. Multiplication
by the fixed inverse Hessian proves
Equation~\eqref{eq:linear-sieve-lambda-profile}. Substitution of the affine
fitted function into the separate tuning criterion and expansion of its
squares proves Equation~\eqref{eq:linear-sieve-validation-profile}. The
coefficients are the constant, linear, and quadratic terms of that expansion.

For Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-finite-validation}, Hoeffding's inequality and a union
bound over the \(M=|\mathcal G_j|\) grid pairs gives the uniform deviation
\(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{val}}\). Comparing the selected score with the
grid-oracle score and cancelling the common \(Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(0)\) gives the
factor two.

For Corollary~\ref{cor:cate-estimated-r-validation}, direct expansion with
the evaluation-independent approximate ratio perturbs the expectation at
each grid pair by
\(\omega\mathbb E_O[(\widehat r-r_0)G_{j,\lambda,\omega}]\).
The main-text quantity \(\Delta_r\) bounds these perturbations uniformly, so
the same selector comparison adds \(2\Delta_r\).  Hoeffding's inequality
applied to the actual scores gives
\(2\widehat\varepsilon_{\mathrm{val}}(\delta)\), proving both displayed
bounds.

For Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-general-selected-risk}, assign fitting failure
probability \(\delta_f/M\) to each of the \(M\) fixed candidates.  A union
bound makes all candidate-specific fitting inequalities hold simultaneously
with probability at least \(1-\delta_f\).  On that event, the ideal risk of
candidate \(k\) is at most \(B_k\).  Independently, the validation event in
Corollary~\ref{cor:cate-estimated-r-validation} holds with probability at
least \(1-\delta_v\) and compares the selected fitted candidate with the
best fitted candidate, adding
\(2\widehat\varepsilon_{\mathrm{val}}(\delta_v)
+2\Delta_r^{\mathrm{val}}\).  Intersecting the fitting and validation events
and then minimizing the simultaneous upper bounds over \(k\) proves
Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-general-selected-risk}.  No independence among the
candidate fits is required; only their joint independence from final
validation is used.

For Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-sieve-identity}, the first-order condition is
\((\widehat H_j+\rho P)\widehat\beta_j=\widehat h_j\). Subtracting its value at
\(\beta_p\) proves Equation~\eqref{eq:sieve-coefficient-identity}. Projection
orthogonality, \(\mathbb E_R[b(\zeta_p-\tau)]=0\), removes the risk cross term and
proves Equation~\eqref{eq:sieve-risk-identity}.

For Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-fixed-candidate-bound}, let \(\psi_{R,j}\) and
\(\psi_{O,j}\) be the explicit raw contributions in the main text. Their
source means need not vanish separately, but exact transport gives
\(P_R\psi_{R,j}+P_O\psi_{O,j}=0\). Center each source by its own mean. Since
the centered RCT and OBS sample means are independent, their covariance
proxies add:
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Cov}\left(
\mathbb P_R\psi_{R,j}+\mathbb P_O\psi_{O,j}\right)
&=\frac{\Sigma_{R,j}}{n_f}+\frac{\Sigma_{O,j}}{N_f}.
\end{align*}
The stated sub-Gaussian vector concentration inequality bounds the squared
\(\Gamma_R^{-1/2}\)-norm by the sum of the two trace terms and their operator
norm tail terms, without applying a triangle inequality between sources. On
\(\mathcal E_H\), the whitened inverse penalized Hessian has norm at most
\((1-\eta)^{-1}\). Combining this fact with
Equation~\eqref{eq:sieve-coefficient-identity} and bounding the ridge vector
separately proves Equation~\eqref{eq:finite-sieve-bound}.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-fixed-p-leading-risk}, write the leading term
as
\(A_{n_f,N_f}\triangleq\Gamma_R^{-1}\{(\mathbb P_R-P_R)\psi_{R,j}
+(\mathbb P_O-P_O)\psi_{O,j}\}\), so the remainder defined in the
proposition gives
\(\widehat\beta_j-\beta_p=A_{n_f,N_f}+R_{n_f,N_f}\).
Insert this identity into Equation~\eqref{eq:sieve-risk-identity}.
Independence removes the cross covariance between the RCT and OBS components
of \(A_{n_f,N_f}\), and hence
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E\|A_{n_f,N_f}\|_{\Gamma_R}^2
&=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_R^{-1}\Sigma_{R,j})}{n_f}
+\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_R^{-1}\Sigma_{O,j})}{N_f}.
\end{align*}
Moreover, Cauchy--Schwarz and the stated mean-square remainder condition give
\begin{align*}
\left|\mathbb E\langle A_{n_f,N_f},R_{n_f,N_f}\rangle_{\Gamma_R}\right|
&\leq
\{\mathbb E\|A_{n_f,N_f}\|_{\Gamma_R}^2\}^{1/2}
\{\mathbb E\|R_{n_f,N_f}\|_{\Gamma_R}^2\}^{1/2}
=o(n_f^{-1}+N_f^{-1}),
\end{align*}
and the expected squared remainder has the same smaller order. The identities
\(\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\operatorname{tr}(BA)\) then give
Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-fixed-p-leading-risk}. The comparison in
Corollary~\ref{cor:cate-rct-fusion-leading-risk} follows by subtracting the two
leading expansions; it is not a finite-sample comparison.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-sieve-ratio-perturbation}, write the estimated-
ratio normal equation as
\((\widehat H_j^{r_0}+\widehat\Delta_{H,j}+\rho P)
\widehat\beta_j^{\widehat r}
=\widehat h_j^{r_0}+\widehat\Delta_{h,j}\). Subtract the exact-ratio equation
and rearrange. Invertibility gives the displayed resolvent identity.

Finally, substitute \(a_K^2=O(K^{-2s})\) and the covariance scales
\(Kc_{R,j}(K;\lambda,\omega)/n_f\) and
\(Kc_{O,j}(K;\lambda,\omega)/N_f\) into the finite-sample result. Adding
the stated ridge and fitting-ratio terms proves
Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-spline-rate}. Minimizing the resulting criterion over
\(\mathcal K_n\) gives the displayed definition of \(K_j^\star\). Under the
stated uniform positive finite limits, that criterion is asymptotically
equivalent, uniformly over the coefficient candidates, to
\(K^{-2s}+K v_j^\infty\). Balancing its two terms gives
\(K\asymp(v_j^\infty)^{-1/(2s+1)}\) and rate
\((v_j^\infty)^{2s/(2s+1)}\), proving
Corollary~\ref{cor:cate-spline-rate}.

\subsection{A two-stratum oracle calculation}

Let \(P_R(X=0)=P_R(X=1)=1/2\), \(\tau(0)=0\), \(\tau(1)=2\), and \(g=\tau\). With \(n=100\), \(N=900\), and a common covariate marginal,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_R\{\tau(X)\}&=1,
&
\operatorname{Var}_R\{\tau(X)\}&=1,
&
\omega_{\mathrm{ATE}}^\star&=\frac{1}{1+100/900}=0.9.
\end{align*}
The baseline between-\(X\) variance is \(0.01\), whereas the oracle component is
\begin{align*}
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_R\{0.1\tau(X)\}}{100}
+\frac{0.9^2\operatorname{Var}_R\{\tau(X)\}}{900}
&=0.0001+0.0009=0.001.
\end{align*}
The reduction \(0.009\) concerns only between-\(X\) variation; total variance also contains the within-\(X\) pseudo-outcome noise from Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-within-between}.

\subsection{Honest variance estimation and evaluation-sample inference}
\label{app:ate-evaluation-regularity}

For Proposition~\ref{prop:ate-honest-variance-active}, fix the coefficients and
scores before using the evaluation observations. Ordinary unbiased sample-variance identities
give
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E\{\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}_{R,\mathrm{eval}}(\psi_R)\}
&=\operatorname{Var}_R(\psi_R),
&
\mathbb E\{\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}_{O,\mathrm{eval}}(\psi_O)\}
&=\operatorname{Var}_O(\psi_O).
\end{align*}
Dividing by \(n\) and \(N\), then adding, proves unbiasedness of
\(\widehat V\) by independence of \(D_R^{\mathrm{eval}}\) and
\(D_O^{\mathrm{eval}}\).

For Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-honest-clt-active}, impose the following evaluation
regularity conditions after fixing the learned objects:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(R1)] the RCT and OBS evaluation observations are conditionally
independent and retain their respective product laws;
\item[(R2)] \(n,N\to\infty\), \(n/N\to\kappa\in[0,\infty)\), and for some
\(\delta>0\) the centered RCT and OBS evaluation-score arrays have uniformly
bounded conditional \((2+\delta)\) moments;
\item[(R3)]
\(n\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\widehat\lambda,
\widehat\omega)\}\to\sigma^2\in(0,\infty)\) in probability, and the combined
sample-variance estimator is ratio-consistent for this variance.
\end{enumerate}
Center the two evaluation-score arrays at their population means. Their
weighted sum is \(\widehat\theta_r-\theta\). Condition (R2) supplies the
two-sample triangular-array central limit theorem: the RCT Lyapunov remainder
is of order \(n^{-\delta/2}\), and the OBS remainder also vanishes when
\(n/N\to\kappa<\infty\). Condition (R3) and Slutsky's theorem yield the
studentized limit and asymptotic coverage of the normal-approximation interval.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-three-way-comparison}}
\label{app:ate-three-way-comparison}

Under either exact \(r=r_0\) transport or its common-marginal special case,
Equation~\eqref{eq:pseudo-valid} gives
\(\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)=0\). Hence
\(E=\operatorname{Cov}_R(\Delta,g)=0\), as shown in
Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-population-orthogonality}. Multiplying
Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-quadratic} by \(n\) therefore gives
\begin{align*}
\widetilde{\mathcal V}(\lambda,\omega)
&=
\sigma_0^2+2C\lambda-2D\omega+A\lambda^2+B\omega^2.
\end{align*}
At \((\lambda,\omega)=(0,0)\), this equals \(\sigma_0^2\). Holding
\(\lambda=0\) and completing the square in \(\omega\) gives the unique
PPI++-only oracle \(D/B\) and variance \(\sigma_0^2-D^2/B\). Completing the
two separate squares gives the joint oracle \((-C/A,D/B)\) and variance
\(\sigma_0^2-C^2/A-D^2/B\). The two variance gaps are \(D^2/B\) and
\(C^2/A\). Because \(A,B>0\), they are nonnegative and vanish exactly when
\(D=0\) and \(C=0\), respectively.

\subsection{Density-ratio characterizations and estimated-ratio inheritance}
\label{app:estimated-ratio-inheritance}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:ratio-characterizations}.}
The defining property of the Radon--Nikodym derivative gives
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_O\{r_0(X)h(X)\}
&=
\mathbb E_R\{h(X)\}
\end{align*}
for every integrable \(h\). If a nonnegative normalized function
\(\widetilde r\) gives
the same equality for every bounded measurable \(h\), then
\(\int_A \widetilde r\,dP_O=P_R(A)=\int_A r_0\,dP_O\) for every measurable
set \(A\). Uniqueness of the Radon--Nikodym derivative gives
\(\widetilde r=r_0\)
\(P_O^X\)-almost surely.

If \(r_0\in L_2(P_O^X)\), then for every \(a\in L_2(P_O^X)\),
\begin{align*}
\mathcal R(a)
&=
\mathbb E_O\{a(X)^2\}-2\mathbb E_R\{a(X)\}
\\
&=
\mathbb E_O\{(a(X)-r_0(X))^2\}
-\mathbb E_O\{r_0(X)^2\}.
\end{align*}
The squared term is uniquely minimized at \(a=r_0\) in
\(L_2(P_O^X)\). Restricting the balance equations to the coordinates of
\(\phi\) tests equality only against functions in their linear span, which
proves the finite-feature qualification.

\paragraph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-riesz-ratio}.}
For \(a_\gamma=\phi^\top\gamma\), the gradient of the objective in
Equation~\eqref{eq:ratio-riesz-empirical} is twice
\begin{align*}
\left[
\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{nuis}}\{\phi(X)\phi(X)^\top\}+\rho I_p
\right]\gamma
-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{nuis}}\{\phi(X)\}.
\end{align*}
The stated invertibility condition makes its unique zero
Equation~\eqref{eq:ratio-riesz-linear}.

For Equation~\eqref{eq:ratio-targeted-population}, transport identity
\eqref{eq:ratio-transport-characterization} gives
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_O(g^2a^2)-2\mathbb E_R(g^2a)
&=
\mathbb E_O\{g^2(a-r_0)^2\}-\mathbb E_O(g^2r_0^2),
\end{align*}
which proves the claimed weighted excess-risk identity.

\paragraph{Classifier odds.}
Let \(p_R\) and \(p_O\) be densities with respect to a common dominating
measure. In the pooled nuisance population,
\begin{align*}
s_0(x)
&=
\frac{\pi p_R(x)}{\pi p_R(x)+(1-\pi)p_O(x)}.
\end{align*}
Solving for \(p_R(x)/p_O(x)\) gives
\begin{align*}
r_0(x)
&=
\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}\frac{s_0(x)}{1-s_0(x)},
\end{align*}
which proves Equation~\eqref{eq:ratio-classifier-bayes} in
Definition~\ref{def:classifier-density-ratio}. The same algebra with a fitted
domain probability and the effective training prior yields
\(\widehat r_{\mathrm{CLS}}\).

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:automatic-ratio-balance}.}
The log of a positive weighted exponential sum is convex in \(\xi\). At an
interior minimizer, differentiating the objective in
Definition~\ref{def:classifier-calibrated-balance} gives
\begin{align*}
0
&=
\frac{
\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{nuis}}
\{\widehat r_{\mathrm{base}}(X)e^{\widehat\xi^\top\phi(X)}\phi(X)\}
}{
\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{nuis}}
\{\widehat r_{\mathrm{base}}(X)e^{\widehat\xi^\top\phi(X)}\}
}
-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{nuis}}\{\phi(X)\}
\\
&=
\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{nuis}}
\{\widehat r_{\mathrm{BAL}}(X)\phi(X)\}
-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{nuis}}\{\phi(X)\}.
\end{align*}
The denominator normalizes the OBS weights to one, and positivity follows from
the positive base ratio and exponential tilt. A finite interior optimizer can
exist only when the target feature mean lies in the relative interior of the
convex hull of the weighted OBS feature vectors. Boundary or infeasible cases require a separate
approximate-balance rule and are not covered by the exact first-order
condition.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{lem:ratio-error-coefficients}.}
On the tuning OBS sample, \(\widehat r g=r_0g+\delta_{\widehat r}\). The
sample-variance identity
\(\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(U+V)=
\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(U)+2\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(U,V)+
\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(V)\) proves
Equation~\eqref{eq:ratio-B-error}. When the axis thresholds are inactive,
\begin{align*}
\widehat\omega_{\widehat r}-\widehat\omega_{r_0}
&=
\frac{\widehat D}{\widehat B_{\widehat r}}
-\frac{\widehat D}{\widehat B_{r_0}},
\end{align*}
which is Equation~\eqref{eq:ratio-omega-error} after taking a common
denominator.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:estimated-ratio-decomposition}.}
Condition on the nuisance and tuning samples. At the same selected
coefficients, the only direct difference between
\(\widehat\theta_{\widehat r}\) and \(\widehat\theta_{r_0}\) in
Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-estimator} is
\(\widehat\omega\mathbb P_{O,N}\delta_{\widehat r}\). Adding and subtracting
\(\widehat\omega P_O\delta_{\widehat r}\) identifies the scalar drift
\(b_{\widehat r}\) and centered fluctuation \(e_{\widehat r}\), proving both
equalities in Equations~\eqref{eq:estimated-ratio-exact-decomposition} and
\eqref{eq:estimated-ratio-mean-fluctuation}.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{cor:estimated-ratio-mean-preservation}.}
Taking the conditional expectation of
Equation~\eqref{eq:estimated-ratio-mean-fluctuation} removes its centered
empirical term. The remaining added mean is
\(\widehat\omega P_O\delta_{\widehat r}\), and
transport identity \eqref{eq:ratio-transport-characterization} gives
\begin{align*}
P_O\delta_{\widehat r}
&=
\mathbb E_O(\widehat r g)-\mathbb E_R(g),
\end{align*}
which proves Equation~\eqref{eq:estimated-ratio-mean-preservation}.

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{cor:estimated-ratio-inheritance}.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:estimated-ratio-mean-rate} makes the scaled population
drift in Equation~\eqref{eq:estimated-ratio-mean-fluctuation} negligible. For
the centered OBS evaluation perturbation,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E
\left[
\left\{
\sqrt n\,\widehat\omega
(\mathbb P_{O,N}-P_O)\delta_{\widehat r}
\right\}^2
\right]
&=
\widehat\omega^2\frac nN
\left\|
\delta_{\widehat r}-P_O\delta_{\widehat r}
\right\|_{L_2(P_O)}^2,
\end{align*}
so Equation~\eqref{eq:estimated-ratio-fluctuation-rate} makes this perturbation
\(o_p(1)\). Tuning-moment consistency and asymptotically inactive thresholds
make the selected
coefficients converge to their exact-ratio limits. Slutsky's theorem then
transfers the normal limit. More explicitly, for the deterministic limit
\((\lambda^\circ,\omega^\circ)\),
\begin{align*}
&\widehat\theta_{r_0}(\widehat\lambda,\widehat\omega)
-\widehat\theta_{r_0}(\lambda^\circ,\omega^\circ)
\\
&\qquad=
(\widehat\lambda-\lambda^\circ)\mathbb P_{R,n}\Delta
+(\widehat\omega-\omega^\circ)
\left\{\mathbb P_{O,N}(r_0g)-\mathbb P_{R,n}g\right\}.
\end{align*}
Coefficient consistency makes both coefficient differences \(o_p(1)\).
The RCT score satisfies \(\mathbb P_{R,n}\Delta=O_p(n^{-1/2})\), and exact
transport gives
\(\mathbb P_{O,N}(r_0g)-\mathbb P_{R,n}g
=O_p(N^{-1/2}+n^{-1/2})\). Because \(n/N\) is bounded, the entire display is
\(o_p(n^{-1/2})\). If each deterministic limit is the corresponding
exact-ratio population-oracle pair in
Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-three-way-comparison}, this replacement argument transfers
its first-order variance ordering.
If either ratio-error condition fails, the ratio learner's influence term,
the OBS empirical-process term, and their covariances must enter the limiting
variance. None of these arguments compares realized finite-sample variances.

\subsection{Counterexamples to adaptive reuse}

The fixed-coefficient theorem cannot be extended by selecting a coefficient on
the same evaluation outcomes without a separate analysis.

\paragraph{Reuse through \(\lambda\).}
Let \(X\) be degenerate, \(\theta=0\), \(Z_0=0\), and let the \(\Delta_i\) be i.i.d. Rademacher variables. Every fixed \(\lambda\) satisfies \(\mathbb E\{\lambda\mathbb P_{R,n}\Delta\}=0\). If the same outcomes select \(\widehat\lambda=\operatorname{sign}(\mathbb P_{R,n}\Delta)\), then
\begin{align*}
\mathbb P_{R,n}(Z_0+\widehat\lambda\Delta)
&=|\mathbb P_{R,n}\Delta|,
\end{align*}
whose expectation is strictly positive.

\paragraph{Reuse through \(\omega\).}
Under the common covariate marginal, let \(g(X)\) be a nondegenerate mean-zero Rademacher variable and let the RCT-valid score be identically zero. Every fixed \(\omega\) centers \(\omega\{\mathbb P_{O,N}g-\mathbb P_{R,n}g\}\). Choosing \(\widehat\omega\) as the sign of that same bracket produces its absolute value, which again has strictly positive expectation. The bias in both examples comes solely from adaptive reuse.
