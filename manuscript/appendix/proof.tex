\section{Proof Details}
\label{app:proofs}

Unless stated otherwise, all proofs fix blocks \(D_R^1,D_O^1,D_R^2,D_O^2\)
and every nuisance, ratio, candidate function, and coefficient learned from
them. The remaining expectations are over the independent RCT and OBS block-0
observations.

\subsection{Conditional validity of the trial pseudo-outcome}

Fix \(q=(q_0,q_1)\) after block 1 and condition on \(X\). Under trial
randomization, consistency, and independence of block 0,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_R\left[\frac{A}{e(X)}\{Y-q_1(X)\}\mid X\right]
&=\mathbb E_R\{Y(1)-q_1(X)\mid X\},
\\
\mathbb E_R\left[\frac{1-A}{1-e(X)}\{Y-q_0(X)\}\mid X\right]
&=\mathbb E_R\{Y(0)-q_0(X)\mid X\}.
\end{align}
Substituting these two equalities into the definition of \(\varphi\) cancels the
two fitted regressions and yields
\(\mathbb E_R\{\varphi(V;q)\mid X\}=\tau(X)\).  Applying the identity to
\(q=\widehat\mu_R\), \(q=\widehat\mu_O\), and their affine blend gives
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda\mid X)&=\tau(X),
&
\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)&=0.
\end{align}

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:rct-only-aipw}}

Apply Equation~\eqref{eq:pseudo-valid} with the block-1 fitted pair
\(q=\widehat\mu_R\), average over the independent block \(D_R^0\), and use
iterated expectation. This gives
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E\{\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{AIPW},R}\}
&=\mathbb E_R\{\tau(X)\}=\theta.
\end{align*}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-variance}}

Using \(\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda)=\theta\) and exact transport,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_O\{r(X)g(X)\}=\mathbb E_R\{g(X)\},
\end{align}
the block-0 mean of Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-estimator} is \(\theta\). Rewrite
the centered estimator as
\begin{align}
\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)-\theta
&=
(\mathbb P_{R,n}-P_R)\{Z_\lambda-\omega g\}
+(\mathbb P_{O,N}-P_O)\{\omega rg\}.
\end{align}
The two summands are independent because \(D_R^0\) and \(D_O^0\) are
independent. The variance of
an empirical mean of \(m\) independent observations is the population variance
divided by \(m\), which proves Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-variance}.  Unbiased sample
variances on the two independent evaluation blocks therefore give the displayed
fixed-selected-coefficient variance estimator in Section~\ref{sec:ate}. Coverage
of its Wald interval additionally uses the stated block-0 Lindeberg and
moment assumptions; variance unbiasedness alone is not a central limit theorem.

Because \(g(X)\) is fixed given \(X\),
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\omega g(X)\mid X\}
&=\tau(X)-\omega g(X),
&
\operatorname{Var}_R\{Z_\lambda-\omega g(X)\mid X\}
&=\operatorname{Var}_R(Z_\lambda\mid X).
\end{align*}
Applying the law of total variance proves Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-within-between}.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:ate-variance-quadratic}}

Expand the first variance in Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-variance} using
\(Z_\lambda=Z_0+\lambda\Delta\).  After collecting terms and multiplying by
\(n\), the coefficient-dependent part is
\begin{align}
2C\lambda-2D\omega+A\lambda^2-2E\lambda\omega+B\omega^2,
\end{align}
which is \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\beta)\). This proves
Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-quadratic}. The representation
\begin{align}
\begin{pmatrix}u&v\end{pmatrix}\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix}
&=\operatorname{Var}_R(u\Delta-vg)
+\frac nN v^2\operatorname{Var}_O(rg)
\end{align}
proves positive semidefiniteness.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:ate-argmin-equivalence}}

Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-quadratic} is the positive affine transformation
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\beta)\}
&=\frac1n\operatorname{Var}_R(Z_0)
+\frac1n\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\beta),
\end{align*}
where \(n>0\). Adding a constant and multiplying by a positive scalar preserve
the complete argmin set over every feasible set.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:joint-oracle}}

If \(AB-E^2>0\), \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is positive definite. The
first-order condition for \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is
\(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\beta=-\boldsymbol\ell\), which has the unique
solution
\begin{align*}
\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star
&=-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell
=
\begin{pmatrix}
(ED-BC)/(AB-E^2)\\
(AD-EC)/(AB-E^2)
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align*}
For \(\rho>0\), the ridge objective has gradient
\begin{align*}
\nabla_\beta
\left\{\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\beta)+\rho\lVert\beta\rVert_2^2\right\}
&=2\boldsymbol\ell
+2(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}+\rho I_2)\beta.
\end{align*}
Positive semidefiniteness of \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) makes
\(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}+\rho I_2\) positive definite. Setting the gradient
to zero proves Equation~\eqref{eq:ridge-oracle} and uniqueness.

\subsection{Singular and constrained extensions}

When \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is singular, nonnegativity of the variance quadratic implies
\(\boldsymbol\ell\in\operatorname{Range}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})\); otherwise a null-space direction
would make the expression arbitrarily negative.  The complete minimizer set is
therefore \(-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^+\boldsymbol\ell+\operatorname{Null}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})\).  Continuity of the
quadratic proves attainment on every prespecified compact feasible set.

For completeness, suppose \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is positive definite and write
\begin{align*}
\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\beta)
&=2\boldsymbol\ell^\top\beta
+\beta^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\beta,
&
\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star
&=-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell.
\end{align*}
Completing the square gives
\begin{align*}
\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\beta)
-\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star)
&=(\beta-\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star)^\top
\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\beta-\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star).
\end{align*}
The norm induced by a positive-definite matrix is equivalent to the Euclidean
norm.  Hence this distance is coercive and strictly convex.  On every nonempty
closed convex feasible set, it therefore attains a unique minimum, namely the
\(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\)-metric projection of
\(\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star\).

Finally, because \(g\) is \(X\)-measurable,
\begin{align}
E
&=\mathbb E_R[\{g(X)-P_Rg\}\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)]=0,
\\
D
&=\operatorname{Cov}_R\{\mathbb E_R(Z_0\mid X),g(X)\}
=\operatorname{Cov}_R\{\tau(X),g(X)\}.
\end{align}
If \(A=\operatorname{Var}_R(\Delta)=0\), then \(\Delta\) is constant almost
surely, and \(\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)=0\) forces that constant to be zero.
If
\begin{align*}
B
&=\operatorname{Var}_R(g)+\frac nN\operatorname{Var}_O(rg)=0,
\end{align*}
then \(g\) is constant \(P_R\)-almost surely and \(rg\) is constant
\(P_O\)-almost surely.  Exact transport gives
\(P_O(rg)=P_R(g)\), so the two constants agree and
\(\mathbb P_{O,N}(rg)-\mathbb P_{R,n}(g)=0\) for every evaluation sample.
Finally, when \(E=0\) and \(A,B>0\), completing the two scalar squares gives
\begin{align*}
\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ATE}}(\beta)
&=
A\left(\lambda+\frac CA\right)^2
+B\left(\omega-\frac DB\right)^2
-\frac{C^2}{A}-\frac{D^2}{B}.
\end{align*}
Therefore minimization over a rectangular feasible set is exactly coordinatewise
clipping of \(-C/A\) and \(D/B\).  This argument does not apply to coupled
constraints or to a nonzero interaction \(E\).
Substitution yields Equation~\eqref{eq:axis-oracle}.  Since the population
objective is minimized over a set containing \((0,0)\), its oracle value cannot
exceed the trial-only value.  This comparison concerns the population objective,
not coefficients estimated from a finite tuning sample.

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:joint-oracle-variance}}

Write the block-0 variance as
\begin{align*}
V(\beta)
&=
\mathcal V_0+
\frac1n
\{2\boldsymbol\ell^\top\beta+\beta^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\beta\},
&
\mathcal V_0
&=
\frac1n\operatorname{Var}_R(Z_0).
\end{align*}
When \(AB-E^2>0\), the matrix \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is positive definite and the unique minimizer is \(\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star=-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell\). Substitution gives
\begin{align*}
V(\beta_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star)
&=
\mathcal V_0-
\frac1n\boldsymbol\ell^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell.
\end{align*}
For \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&-E\\-E&B\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
\begin{align*}
\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}
&=
\frac1{AB-E^2}
\begin{pmatrix}B&E\\E&A\end{pmatrix},
\\
\boldsymbol\ell^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell
&=
\frac{BC^2-2ECD+AD^2}{AB-E^2}.
\end{align*}
Positive definiteness makes this quadratic form nonnegative and makes it zero exactly when \(\boldsymbol\ell=0\). Setting \(E=0\) yields \(C^2/A+D^2/B\).

\paragraph{Singular variance reduction and compact constraints.}
If \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is singular, boundedness below of the variance objective implies \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\operatorname{Range}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})\). Evaluating at the minimum-norm solution \(-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^+\boldsymbol\ell\) gives
\begin{align*}
V_{\min}
&=
\mathcal V_0-
\frac1n\boldsymbol\ell^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^+\boldsymbol\ell.
\end{align*}
The pseudoinverse is positive definite on \(\operatorname{Range}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})\), so the reduction is strictly positive exactly when \(\boldsymbol\ell\neq0\). Finally, continuity gives attainment on every nonempty compact feasible set \(\mathcal B_\beta\). If \(0\in\mathcal B_\beta\), the constrained minimum is at most \(V(0)=\mathcal V_0\), and it is strictly smaller exactly when some feasible \(\beta\) makes \(2\boldsymbol\ell^\top\beta+\beta^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\beta<0\).

\subsection{Prediction-powered CATE risk identity}

Fix \(t,\lambda,\omega,g,r\) after blocks 1 and 2, and then condition on \(X\).
Since \(\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda-\tau(X)\mid X)=0\),
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-t(X)\}^2
&=
\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\tau(X)\}^2
+\mathbb E_R\{t(X)-\tau(X)\}^2.
\end{align}
Exact transport also gives
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_O\{r(X)(g(X)-t(X))^2\}
&=\mathbb E_R\{g(X)-t(X)\}^2.
\end{align}
The correction in Equation~\eqref{eq:dr-fusion-loss} therefore has mean zero,
which proves Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-target}.  In particular, population expected
loss is flat in \(\omega\).  The part depending on \(\lambda\) is
\begin{align}
Q(\lambda)
&=Q(0)+2C\lambda+A\lambda^2,
\end{align}
so when \(A>0\) its minimizer is \(-C/A\), the same integrated-noise oracle as
in the population-orthogonal ATE problem.  If \(A=0\), then \(\Delta=0\) almost
surely by conditional mean zero, and \(Q\) is flat in \(\lambda\).

After fixing \(t\), \(\lambda\), \(g\), and \(r\) using blocks 1 and 2, the
block-0 empirical loss is the sum of independent empirical means of
\(U_{t,\lambda}-\omega G_t\) over \(D_R^0\) and \(\omega rG_t\) over
\(D_O^0\). Expanding its block-0 variance, with sample-size ratio \(n/N\), as a
quadratic in \(\omega\) and differentiating gives
Equation~\eqref{eq:loss-variance-omega}.  Because \(t\) is fixed in this
calculation, the formula does not identify the coefficient minimizing the risk
of a learner chosen after seeing the same loss.

For the linear sieve, expand Equation~\eqref{eq:dr-fusion-loss} in \(\beta\), add
\(\rho\beta^\top P\beta\), and set the gradient to zero.  The resulting normal
equation is
\begin{align}
\{(1-\omega)G_R+\omega G_{O,r}+\rho P\}\beta
&=h_\lambda+\omega d_g,
\end{align}
which proves Equation~\eqref{eq:linear-sieve} whenever the matrix is invertible.

\subsection{Pointwise noise coefficients and square completion}

For Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-pointwise-lambda-active}, measurability and conditional mean zero give
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_R\{Z_0+\lambda(X)\Delta\mid X\}
&=\tau(X)+\lambda(X)\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)
=\tau(X).
\end{align*}
At a fixed \(x\), expand the conditional noise as a quadratic in a scalar \(u\):
\begin{align*}
Q_x(u)
&=Q_x(0)
+2u\operatorname{Cov}_R(Z_0,\Delta\mid X=x)
+u^2\operatorname{Var}_R(\Delta\mid X=x).
\end{align*}
A positive quadratic coefficient yields the displayed pointwise ratio. If it is zero, \(\Delta=0\) conditionally almost surely and every \(u\)-dependent term vanishes.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-square-completion-active}, expand the trial contribution as
\begin{align*}
\{Z_\lambda-t\}^2-\omega\{g-t\}^2
&=(1-\omega)t^2-2(Z_\lambda-\omega g)t+Z_\lambda^2-\omega g^2.
\end{align*}
Since \((1-\omega)\widetilde Z_{\lambda,\omega}=Z_\lambda-\omega g\), completing the square leaves
\begin{align*}
Z_\lambda^2-\omega g^2-(1-\omega)\widetilde Z_{\lambda,\omega}^2
&=-\frac{\omega}{1-\omega}(Z_\lambda-g)^2.
\end{align*}
Averaging and adding the transported OBS term proves the proposition. For a linear sieve, the Hessian of the original \(t\)-dependent quadratic is a nonnegative weighted sum of \(G_R\) and \(G_{O,r}\) for \(0\leq\omega\leq1\), which proves positive semidefiniteness in that range. The claim does not extend automatically to nonlinear parameterizations.

\subsection{Loss-targeted balance and approximate drift}

The two inequalities in Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-candidate-dominance} follow directly from the prespecified inclusion \(\mathfrak C_R\subseteq\mathfrak C\). Strict inclusion alone does not imply a strict risk inequality.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-selector-inequality}, fix the candidates fitted
on block 1 and the fitting portion of block 2, then take expectation over the
disjoint validation portion of block 2. Expanding the
shared-reference score gives
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_2\{\widehat{\mathcal R}^{\mathrm{ref}}_c\}
&=Q(\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}})
+\mathcal R_{12}(c)
+\delta_{r,c}+\delta_{\mathrm{nuis},c}.
\end{align*}
Compare the empirical score of \(\widehat c\) with that of an arbitrary \(c\in\mathfrak C\). Two validation deviations contribute at most \(2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{val}}\), and the difference between two ratio and nuisance drifts contributes at most \(2\Delta_r+2\Delta_{\mathrm{nuis}}\). Taking the infimum gives the stated oracle inequality.

Suppose a nonnegative out-of-sample-evaluable weight \(w\) satisfies
\begin{align}
P_O\{w(g-t)^2\}=P_R\{(g-t)^2\}
\quad\text{for every }t\in\mathcal T.
\end{align}
Replacing \(r\) by \(w\) then leaves the population correction exactly zero for
every candidate, so the target identity remains valid.  If
\(t_\beta=b^\top\beta\), expanding
\begin{align}
(g-b^\top\beta)^2
&=g^2-2\beta^\top bg+\beta^\top bb^\top\beta
\end{align}
shows that balancing \(bg\) and \(bb^\top\) preserves the minimizer.  Balancing
\(g^2\) is needed only to preserve the absolute loss value.

For approximate weights, the difference between the weighted and target
population losses is bounded uniformly over \(\mathcal T\) by
\(|\omega|\Delta_\mathcal T(w)\).  Let \(t_w\) minimize the weighted population
loss and \(t_0\) minimize the target loss.  Applying the uniform bound at both
minimizers gives
\begin{align}
L_0(t_w)
&\leq L_w(t_w)+|\omega|\Delta_\mathcal T(w)
\\
&\leq L_w(t_0)+|\omega|\Delta_\mathcal T(w)
\\
&\leq L_0(t_0)+2|\omega|\Delta_\mathcal T(w),
\end{align}
which is the claimed excess-risk bound.  If the same finite sample is used both
to impose exact sieve balance and to fit \(\beta\), the balanced linear and
quadratic coefficient terms cancel from the empirical correction (and the
constant cancels as well if \(g^2\) is balanced).  This algebra explains why
weight learning and candidate fitting require honest separation or a separate
outer-fold argument.

\subsection{A two-stratum oracle calculation}

Let \(P_R(X=0)=P_R(X=1)=1/2\), \(\tau(0)=0\), \(\tau(1)=2\), and \(g=\tau\). With \(n=100\), \(N=900\), and a common covariate marginal,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_R\{\tau(X)\}&=1,
&
\operatorname{Var}_R\{\tau(X)\}&=1,
&
\omega_{\mathrm{ATE}}^\star&=\frac{1}{1+100/900}=0.9.
\end{align*}
The baseline between-\(X\) variance is \(0.01\), whereas the oracle component is
\begin{align*}
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_R\{0.1\tau(X)\}}{100}
+\frac{0.9^2\operatorname{Var}_R\{\tau(X)\}}{900}
&=0.0001+0.0009=0.001.
\end{align*}
The reduction \(0.009\) concerns only between-\(X\) variation; total variance also contains the within-\(X\) pseudo-outcome noise from Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-within-between}.

\subsection{Honest variance estimation and block-0 Wald inference}

For Proposition~\ref{prop:ate-honest-variance-active}, fix the coefficients and
scores selected on blocks 1 and 2. Ordinary unbiased block-0 variance identities
give
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E\{s_{R,0}^2(\psi_R)\}
&=\operatorname{Var}_R(\psi_R),
&
\mathbb E\{s_{O,0}^2(\psi_O)\}
&=\operatorname{Var}_O(\psi_O).
\end{align*}
Dividing by \(n\) and \(N\), then adding, proves block-0 unbiasedness of
\(\widehat V\) by independence of \(D_R^0\) and \(D_O^0\).

For Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-honest-clt-active}, center the two block-0 score arrays
at their population means after fixing blocks 1 and 2. Their weighted sum is
\(\widehat\theta_r-\theta\). The stated Lindeberg condition and nondegenerate
variance limit give the two-sample triangular-array central limit theorem. Under
a uniform \((2+\delta)\)-moment bound, the RCT Lyapunov remainder is of order
\(n^{-\delta/2}\), and the OBS remainder also vanishes when
\(n/N\to\kappa<\infty\). The same moment control gives ratio consistency of the
combined sample-variance estimator. Slutsky's theorem yields the studentized
limit and Wald coverage.

\subsection{Estimated density ratios and outer-fold honesty}

For \(\widehat r\) trained on block 1 and then held fixed, subtract and add
\(P_O(rg)=P_R(g)\) to obtain the exact block-0 drift
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E\{\widehat\theta_{\widehat r}(\lambda,\omega)\}-\theta
&=\omega P_O[\{\widehat r(X)-r(X)\}g(X)].
\end{align*}
With \(h_n=(\widehat r-r)g\), the block-0 second moment of the OBS evaluation
perturbation is
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E
\left[
\left\{\sqrt n\,\omega(\mathbb P_{O,N}-P_O)h_n\right\}^2
\right]
&=\omega^2\frac nN
\|h_n-P_Oh_n\|_{L_2(P_O)}^2.
\end{align*}
This proves the two negligible-drift requirements in Section~\ref{sec:ate}; a nonnegligible ratio-training influence function or empirical remainder must instead enter the limiting influence function and its covariance.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:ate-outer-unbiased-active}, condition on the prespecified or independently randomized split assignments and on one outer complement. Every fitted object is fixed, and the held-out paired folds retain the required RCT and OBS laws. The exact centering argument applies within that fold. Iterated expectation proves aggregate unbiasedness. It does not make different fold statistics independent: in general,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_k a_k\widehat\theta_k\right)
&=\sum_k a_k^2\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta_k)
+2\sum_{k<\ell}a_ka_\ell
\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat\theta_k,\widehat\theta_\ell).
\end{align*}
The second sum need not vanish because outer training complements overlap.

\subsection{Counterexamples to adaptive reuse}

The fixed-coefficient theorem cannot be extended by selecting a coefficient on
the same block-0 outcomes without a separate analysis.

\paragraph{Reuse through \(\lambda\).}
Let \(X\) be degenerate, \(\theta=0\), \(Z_0=0\), and let the \(\Delta_i\) be i.i.d. Rademacher variables. Every fixed \(\lambda\) satisfies \(\mathbb E\{\lambda\mathbb P_{R,n}\Delta\}=0\). If the same outcomes select \(\widehat\lambda=\operatorname{sign}(\mathbb P_{R,n}\Delta)\), then
\begin{align*}
\mathbb P_{R,n}(Z_0+\widehat\lambda\Delta)
&=|\mathbb P_{R,n}\Delta|,
\end{align*}
whose expectation is strictly positive.

\paragraph{Reuse through \(\omega\).}
Under the common covariate marginal, let \(g(X)\) be a nondegenerate mean-zero Rademacher variable and let the RCT-valid score be identically zero. Every fixed \(\omega\) centers \(\omega\{\mathbb P_{O,N}g-\mathbb P_{R,n}g\}\). Choosing \(\widehat\omega\) as the sign of that same bracket produces its absolute value, which again has strictly positive expectation. The bias in both examples comes solely from adaptive reuse.
