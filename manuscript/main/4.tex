\section{Data Fusion for CATE Estimation}
\label{sec:cate}

In this section, we develop two estimators of the CATE $\tau$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:cate} that combine  RCT data with  potentially confounded OBS data. The RCT identifies the causal target. The OBS outcome regressions and their treatment-effect prediction may improve statistical efficiency, but they need not be causally valid due to unmeasured confounders. The first estimator is the Doubly Robust Fusion (DRF) Learner, based on the AIPW pseudo-outcome of the DR-learner \citep{kennedy2022drlearner}. The second is the R-Fusion (RF) Learner, based on the R-learner loss \citep{nie2020rlearner} scaled by the known trial propensity variance.


\subsection{CATE targets and RCT-valid learning losses}
\label{sec:cate-loss}


The CATE estimators of this section minimize empirical versions of the following risk.
\begin{definition}[CATE risk]
\label{def:cate-class-risk}
Let $\mathcal Z\subseteq L_2(P_R^X)$ be a prespecified nonempty class of candidate CATE functions, such as a fixed linear sieve. For $\zeta\in\mathcal Z$, define the CATE risk
\begin{align}
\mathcal R(\zeta)&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{\zeta(X)-\tau(X)\}^2.
\label{eq:cate-ideal-risk}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

Recall the fused score $Z_\lambda=\varphi(V;\widehat\mu_\lambda)$ of Definition~\ref{def:fusion-score}. Throughout this section $\lambda\in[0,1]$, so that $\widehat\mu_\lambda$ in Definition~\ref{def:fusion-score} is a convex combination of the two fitted regressions $\widehat\mu_R$ and $\widehat\mu_O$. Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:honest-separation} and~\ref{ass:trial}, recall that $\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda\mid X)=\tau(X)$ for every fixed $\lambda$. Recall also the OBS prediction $\widehat g\triangleq\widehat\mu_{O,1}-\widehat\mu_{O,0}$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:obs-effect-prediction}, and write $G_\zeta(X)\triangleq\{\widehat g(X)-\zeta(X)\}^2$ for the squared distance between the OBS prediction and the candidate CATE function $\zeta$.

We first define the two population risks that the DRF and RF estimators target, and then their empirical versions.

\begin{definition}[RCT population risks]
\label{def:rct-risks}
For $\lambda\in[0,1]$ and a candidate CATE function $\zeta\in\mathcal Z$, define the doubly robust (DR) risk by
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda)&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\zeta(X)\}^2.
\label{eq:dr-risk}
\end{align}
Let $m_\lambda$ be the estimate of the RCT marginal outcome regression $\mathbb E_R[Y\mid X]$ built from the same fitted regressions as $\widehat\mu_\lambda$,
\begin{align*}
	m_R(x)&\triangleq e(x)\widehat\mu_{R,1}(x)+\{1-e(x)\}\widehat\mu_{R,0}(x), \\ 
	m_O(x)&\triangleq e(x)\widehat\mu_{O,1}(x)+\{1-e(x)\}\widehat\mu_{O,0}(x), \\
	m_\lambda&\triangleq(1-\lambda)m_R+\lambda m_O,
\end{align*}
and define the R risk by
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_R(\zeta;\lambda)&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\left[\frac{[\,Y-m_\lambda(X)-\{A-e(X)\}\zeta(X)\,]^2}{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}\right].
\label{eq:r-risk}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

The DR risk is the mean squared error of regressing the RCT-valid pseudo-outcome $Z_\lambda$ on $X$, as in the DR-learner \citep{kennedy2022drlearner}. It is motivated by the conditional validity $\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda\mid X)=\tau(X)$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:pseudo-valid}, which makes $\tau$ the population regression of $Z_\lambda$ on $X$. The R risk is the R-learner loss \citep{nie2020rlearner} with the known trial propensity $e$, divided by $e(X)\{1-e(X)\}$, the conditional variance of $A$ given $X$. Under the unrestricted function class, the minimizer of $\mathcal L_R(\zeta;\lambda)$ is again $\tau$, and over $\mathcal Z$ it is the $L_2(P_R^X)$ projection of $\tau$; Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target} states both, so no separate lemma is needed. Both risks are computable from the RCT alone. To use the large OBS covariate sample, the empirical versions below add the same transported correction as the ATE estimator in Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-estimator}, now applied to the squared distance $G_\zeta$.

\begin{definition}[Doubly Robust Fusion and R-Fusion losses]
\label{def:fusion-losses}
For $S\in\{R,O\}$, let $\mathbb P_S^{\mathrm{tune}}$ denote the empirical average over the tuning sample $D_S^{\mathrm{tune}}$. For $\lambda\in[0,1]$, $\omega\in\mathbb R$, a candidate transport weight $r$, and a candidate CATE function $\zeta\in\mathcal Z$, define the Doubly Robust Fusion (DRF) loss by
\begin{align}
\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)
&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\{Z_\lambda-\zeta(X)\}^2
+\omega\left[\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\{r(X)G_\zeta(X)\}-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\{G_\zeta(X)\}\right].
\label{eq:dr-fusion-loss}
\end{align}
Define the weight $\chi(A,X)\triangleq\{A-e(X)\}^2/[e(X)\{1-e(X)\}]$, which satisfies $\mathbb E_R\{\chi(A,X)\mid X\}=1$ under Assumption~\ref{ass:trial}, and the R-Fusion (RF) loss by
\begin{align}
\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)
&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\left[\frac{[\,Y-m_\lambda(X)-\{A-e(X)\}\zeta(X)\,]^2}{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}\right]
\notag\\
&\quad+\omega\left[\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\{r(X)G_\zeta(X)\}-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\{\chi(A,X)G_\zeta(X)\}\right].
\label{eq:rf-fusion-loss}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

The first term of each loss is the empirical version of the corresponding risk in Definition~\ref{def:rct-risks}, and the bracket is the OBS correction. Both losses also depend on the fitted objects $\widehat\mu_R$ and $\widehat\mu_O$, through $Z_\lambda$ and $m_\lambda$, and on $\widehat g$, through $G_\zeta$; these objects are fixed before the tuning samples are used, so the notation displays only the tuning coefficients, and later sections write $\widehat L_j(\zeta;\lambda,\omega)$ for $j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}$ with $r$ suppressed as well.  Any fixed function $m$ may replace $m_\lambda$; we then write $\mathcal L_R(\zeta;m)$, $Q_R(m)$, and $\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;m,\omega,r)$, and Section~\ref{sec:cate-orthogonality} uses this form to differentiate in $m$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact targets of the two losses]
\label{prop:cate-target}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:trial} and~\ref{ass:honest-separation} hold, and either Assumption~\ref{ass:common-marginal} holds with $r_0=1$ or Assumption~\ref{ass:exact-transport} holds with $r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$. Fix $\lambda\in[0,1]$, $\omega\in\mathbb R$, $\zeta\in\mathcal Z$, and suppose $Z_\lambda\in L_2(P_R)$, $\tau\in L_2(P_R^X)$, $Y-m_\lambda(X)\in L_2(P_R)$, $G_\zeta\in L_1(P_R)$, and $r_0G_\zeta\in L_1(P_O)$. Define the noise floors
\begin{align*}
Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\tau(X)\}^2,
&
Q_R(\lambda)&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\left[\frac{[\,Y-m_\lambda(X)-\{A-e(X)\}\tau(X)\,]^2}{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}\right].
\end{align*}
Then, with $r=r_0$,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}&=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda)=Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)+\mathcal R(\zeta),
\notag\\
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}&=\mathcal L_R(\zeta;\lambda)=Q_R(\lambda)+\mathcal R(\zeta).
\label{eq:cate-target}
\end{align}
Consequently, whenever the minimum is attained, the population minimizers of either expected loss over $\mathcal Z$ are the $L_2(P_R^X)$ projections of $\tau$ onto $\mathcal Z$, for every $\omega$. The same identities hold with any fixed $m$ in place of $m_\lambda$.
\end{proposition}
Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target} shows that both fusion losses target the CATE: minimizing over $\zeta$ is unaffected by the noise floors $Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)$ and $Q_R(\lambda)$, which do not depend on $\zeta$.  Also, the expected loss is flat in $\omega$ for the same reason as in Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-variance}: under exact transport the OBS correction has mean zero. In the later sections, we show what $\omega$ and an estimated ratio change (Section~\ref{sec:cate-orthogonality}) and what $\omega$ buys in finite samples (Section~\ref{sec:cate-general-learning}).

\subsection{Orthogonality of learning equations and nuisance sensitivity}
\label{sec:cate-orthogonality}

Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target} establishes the exact population targets. We now ask a different question: how does each
fitted object enter the first-order equation that learns $\zeta$?

\begin{definition}[Derivative directions and learning roles]
For a functional $F$ of a function-valued argument $\eta$, let $h$ be an
admissible direction such that $\eta+\varepsilon h$ remains in the domain of
$F$ for all sufficiently small $|\varepsilon|$. Whenever the derivative
exists, define the Gateaux derivative by
\begin{align}
D_\eta F(\eta)[h]
&\triangleq
\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}F(\eta+\varepsilon h)\right|_{\varepsilon=0}.
\label{eq:gateaux-derivative}
\end{align}
The target argument is $\zeta$, and $v$ denotes an admissible target
direction. The learned objects $q,m,\widehat{g},r$
are nuisance arguments. The coefficients $\lambda$ and $\omega$ are held fixed during
this audit: $\lambda$ selects $\widehat\mu_\lambda$ or $m_\lambda$, while
$\omega$ scales the OBS correction. The trial propensity $e$ is known
by design.
\end{definition}

Following \citet[Assumption~1]{foster2023orthogonal}, for a population loss $L(\zeta;\eta)$ with target argument $\zeta$ and nuisance argument $\eta$, loss orthogonality means
that the mixed derivative $D_\eta D_\zeta L$ vanishes at the population target
and nuisance truth. Equivalently, up to scaling, it means $D_\eta\Psi=0$ for
$\Psi\triangleq-D_\zeta L/2$. This definition alone does not establish an
oracle convergence rate.

Write $Z_q\triangleq\varphi(V;q)$ for a generic outcome-regression pair $q$ as in Definition~\ref{def:rct-aipw-score}, so that $Z_\lambda=Z_{\widehat\mu_\lambda}$.
\begin{definition}[Full fusion learning equations]
For fixed $(\lambda,\omega)$, define the DRF and RF population learning
equations by
\begin{align}
\Psi_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;q,\widehat{g},r)[v]
&\triangleq
-\frac12D_\zeta\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}\}[v]
\nonumber\\
&=\mathbb E_R[v(X)\{Z_q-\zeta(X)\}]
\nonumber\\
&\quad+\omega\left[
\mathbb E_O\{r(X)v(X)[\widehat{g}(X)-\zeta(X)]\}
-\mathbb E_R\{v(X)[\widehat{g}(X)-\zeta(X)]\}
\right],
\label{eq:drf-learning-equation}\\
\Psi_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;m,\widehat{g},r)[v]
&\triangleq
-\frac12D_\zeta\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}\}[v]
\nonumber\\
&=\mathbb E_R\left[
v(X)\frac{A-e(X)}{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}
\{Y-m(X)-[A-e(X)]\zeta(X)\}\right]
\nonumber\\
&\quad+\omega\left[
\mathbb E_O\{r(X)v(X)[\widehat{g}(X)-\zeta(X)]\}
-\mathbb E_R\{\chi(A,X)v(X)[\widehat{g}(X)-\zeta(X)]\}
\right].
\label{eq:rf-learning-equation}
\end{align}
All derivatives hold the remaining displayed arguments fixed.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact-transport learning equations]
\label{prop:cate-learning-equations}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known propensity
$e$ satisfying strict overlap, and nuisance functions fitted honestly on
data independent of the loss-evaluation observations.  Suppose either that
the RCT and OBS have a common covariate marginal, in which case $r_0=1$,
or that exact covariate transport holds with
$r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$.  Use $r=r_0$, and suppose all displayed
expectations below are finite.
For every integrable function $f(X)$, exact transport gives
$\mathbb E_O(r_0f)=\mathbb E_R(f)$, and randomization gives
$\mathbb E_R(\chi\mid X)=1$. Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\Psi_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;q,\widehat{g},r_0)[v]
&=\mathbb E_R[v(X)\{Z_q-\zeta(X)\}],\\
\Psi_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;m,\widehat{g},r_0)[v]
&=\mathbb E_R\left[
v(X)\frac{A-e(X)}{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}
\{Y-m(X)-[A-e(X)]\zeta(X)\}\right].
\end{align*}
Thus exact transport removes the OBS correction from the population learning
equation without removing it from the empirical optimization problem.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Direction-by-direction learning-equation orthogonality]
\label{prop:cate-orthogonality-audit}
Assume honest fitting, strict trial overlap, the integrability needed to
interchange differentiation and expectation, and exact fixed $r_0$ where
stated. Then the following partial-path statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For an outcome-regression direction $h=(h_0,h_1)$,
\begin{align*}
\dot Z_q[h]
&\triangleq
-\{A-e(X)\}\left\{
\frac{h_1(X)}{e(X)}+\frac{h_0(X)}{1-e(X)}\right\},\\
D_q\Psi_{\mathrm{DRF}}[h,v]
&=\mathbb E_R\{v(X)\dot Z_q[h]\}=0.
\end{align*}
This exact score orthogonality is the main protection supplied by the known
trial propensity.

\item For a marginal-outcome direction $h$,
\begin{align*}
D_m\Psi_{\mathrm{RF}}[h,v]
&=-\mathbb E_R\left[
v(X)\frac{A-e(X)}{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}h(X)\right]=0.
\end{align*}

\item For a prediction direction $u$, define the path
$\widehat{g}_\varepsilon\triangleq\widehat{g}+\varepsilon u$. For either
$j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}$, write $\Psi_j(\widehat{g}_\varepsilon)[v]$
and $\widehat L_j(\widehat{g}_\varepsilon)$ with all other displayed arguments
held fixed. By Equation~\eqref{eq:gateaux-derivative}, the two derivatives below are $D_{\widehat g}\Psi_j[u,v]$ and $D_{\widehat g}\mathbb E\{\widehat L_j\}[u]$, in the same notation as items 1 to 3 and 5. Then
\begin{align}
\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}
\Psi_j(\widehat{g}_\varepsilon)[v]\right|_{\varepsilon=0}
&=\omega\mathbb E_O[\{r(X)-r_0(X)\}u(X)v(X)],
\label{eq:cate-g-score-derivative}\\
\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_j(\widehat{g}_\varepsilon)\}
\right|_{\varepsilon=0}
&=2\omega\mathbb E_O[\{r(X)-r_0(X)\}
\{\widehat{g}(X)-\zeta(X)\}u(X)].
\label{eq:cate-g-loss-derivative}
\end{align}
Both derivatives vanish when $r=r_0$, but not generally for an approximate
ratio.

\item For a ratio direction $h_r$,
\begin{align}
D_r\Psi_j[h_r,v]
&=\omega\mathbb E_O[h_r(X)v(X)\{\widehat{g}(X)-\zeta(X)\}],
\label{eq:cate-ratio-score-derivative}\\
D_r\mathbb E\{\widehat L_j\}[h_r]
&=\omega\mathbb E_O[h_r(X)\{\widehat{g}(X)-\zeta(X)\}^2].
\label{eq:drf-ratio-derivative}
\end{align}
These derivatives are generally nonzero.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The normalization constraint on a density ratio restricts its regular tangent
directions. Define
\begin{align*}
\mathcal T_r
&\triangleq L_0^2(P_O^X)
=\{h\in L_2(P_O^X):\mathbb E_O(h)=0\}.
\end{align*}
This linear space describes first-order normalization; admissible paths must
also respect support and nonnegativity, and a ratio on the positivity boundary
may have only a tangent cone.

\begin{proposition}[Density-ratio nonorthogonality]
\label{prop:cate-ratio-nonorthogonality}
Suppose $\omega\neq0$ and
$v(\widehat{g}-\zeta)\in L_2(P_O^X)$.
Over the full normalized tangent space $\mathcal T_r$, the ratio-score
derivative in Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-ratio-score-derivative} vanishes for
every $h_r\in\mathcal T_r$ if and only if
$v(X)\{\widehat{g}(X)-\zeta(X)\}$ is constant $P_O^X$-almost surely. Therefore,
the learning equations are generally not orthogonal to the density ratio.
Balancing selected empirical moments does not generally produce orthogonality
over the full normalized tangent space.
\end{proposition}

As a direct chain-rule consequence of items 1--3 in
Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-orthogonality-audit}, if the same OBS outcome
regression generates $(\widehat\mu_O,\widehat{g})$ for DRF or $(m_O,\widehat{g})$ for RF, then its
total learning-equation derivative is zero when $e$ is known and exact
$r_0$ is held fixed. This conclusion does not cover paths that change
$r$, or the use of an approximate ratio.

These results establish the target and its first-order nuisance sensitivity,
but they do not control the error of the fitted CATE function. We first derive
that error for a general function class with explicit RCT and OBS fitting
sample sizes. We then select $(\lambda,\omega)$ by honest validation.

\subsection{General two-source learning theory}
\label{sec:cate-general-learning}

Fix a method $j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}$, coefficients
$(\lambda,\omega)$, and honestly fitted nuisances.  Let $n_f$ and $N_f$
denote the independent RCT and OBS fitting-sample sizes.  The method-specific
losses in Equations~\eqref{eq:dr-fusion-loss} and
\eqref{eq:rf-fusion-loss} can be written as an RCT contribution
$\ell_{R,j}(\zeta)$ and an OBS contribution $\ell_{O,j}(\zeta)$.

\begin{definition}[General fusion empirical learner]
For $\zeta\in\mathcal Z$, define
\begin{align*}
L_j(\zeta)
&\triangleq P_R\ell_{R,j}(\zeta)+P_O\ell_{O,j}(\zeta),\\
\widehat L_j(\zeta)
&\triangleq
\mathbb P_{R,n_f}\ell_{R,j}(\zeta)
+\mathbb P_{O,N_f}\ell_{O,j}(\zeta).
\end{align*}
Let
\begin{align*}
\zeta_j^\circ
&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}\mathcal R(\zeta)
\end{align*}
be a prespecified, deterministically selected best-in-class function.  Let
$\operatorname{Pen}:\mathcal Z\to[0,\infty]$ be the full penalty functional
and define
\begin{align*}
\widehat J_j(\zeta)
&\triangleq\widehat L_j(\zeta)+\operatorname{Pen}(\zeta).
\end{align*}
Assume $\widehat J_j$ is bounded below and has a measurable global
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$-approximate minimizer $\widehat\zeta_j$, meaning
\begin{align*}
\widehat J_j(\widehat\zeta_j)
&\leq\inf_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}\widehat J_j(\zeta)
+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}.
\end{align*}
Finally, define the centered comparator process
\begin{align*}
\mathbb Z_j(\zeta)
&\triangleq
\{\widehat L_j-L_j\}(\zeta)
-\{\widehat L_j-L_j\}(\zeta_j^\circ).
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

The next theorem is deterministic once the two tuning samples and fitted
nuisances are fixed.  It is the master reduction from fusion-loss fluctuation
to CATE excess risk.
\begin{theorem}[Exact general fusion ERM reduction]
\label{thm:cate-general-erm}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known propensity
$e$ satisfying strict overlap, and honest nuisance fitting.  Suppose either
$P_R^X=P_O^X$ and $r_0=1$, or $P_R^X\ll P_O^X$ and the exact ratio
$r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$ is used.  Suppose the displayed losses are finite and
$\widehat J_j$ is bounded below and has a measurable global
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$-approximate minimizer $\widehat\zeta_j$, meaning
$\widehat J_j(\widehat\zeta_j)\leq
\inf_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}\widehat J_j(\zeta)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$. Then
\begin{align}
\mathcal R(\widehat\zeta_j)-\mathcal R(\zeta_j^\circ)
&\leq-\mathbb Z_j(\widehat\zeta_j)
+\operatorname{Pen}(\zeta_j^\circ)
-\operatorname{Pen}(\widehat\zeta_j)
+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}.
\label{eq:cate-general-erm-exact}
\end{align}
Consequently, the right-hand side is at most
$\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}\{-\mathbb Z_j(\zeta)\}
+\operatorname{Pen}(\zeta_j^\circ)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$.
\end{theorem}

To obtain a sample-size-dependent result, localize by excess risk rather than
by an unnamed norm.  For $u\geq0$, define
\begin{align*}
\mathcal Z_j(u)
&\triangleq\{\zeta\in\mathcal Z:
\mathcal R(\zeta)-\mathcal R(\zeta_j^\circ)\leq u\}.
\end{align*}
Let $\mathbb Z_{R,j}$ and $\mathbb Z_{O,j}$ be the sourcewise centered
increments whose sum is $\mathbb Z_j$.  Suppose measurable functions
$\psi_{R,j},\psi_{O,j}$ and $\operatorname{Tail}_{R,j},
\operatorname{Tail}_{O,j}$ satisfy, sourcewise and uniformly over the
localized class, the high-probability upper bounds stated in the theorem
below. Define
\begin{align*}
\Phi_{j,n_f,N_f}(u;\delta)
&\triangleq
\psi_{R,j}(u;n_f)+\psi_{O,j}(u;N_f)
+\operatorname{Tail}_{R,j}(u,\delta)
+\operatorname{Tail}_{O,j}(u,\delta),\\
u_{j,n_f,N_f}^\star(\delta)
&\triangleq
\inf\{u>0:\Phi_{j,n_f,N_f}(u;\delta)\leq c_0u\},
\qquad 0<c_0<1.
\end{align*}
For every fixed $\delta$, assume that the full function
$u\mapsto\Phi_{j,n_f,N_f}(u;\delta)$ is nondecreasing and that
$u\mapsto\Phi_{j,n_f,N_f}(u;\delta)/\sqrt{u}$ is nonincreasing on
$(0,\infty)$. Thus the full RCT-plus-OBS modulus, including its tail
terms, has the sub-root scaling used below.
Thus $u_{j,n_f,N_f}^\star$ is an excess-risk level, not an
$L_2$-distance radius.

\begin{theorem}[Localized two-source excess risk]
\label{thm:cate-general-localized}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known strict-overlap
propensity $e$, honest fixed nuisances, and either a common covariate
marginal with $r_0=1$ or exact transport with
$r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$. Assume the displayed losses are finite, the penalized
objective is bounded below, and a measurable global
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$-approximate minimizer exists. Assume the RCT and
OBS tuning samples are independent; the function class and
localized loss-increment classes are measurable or separable; their envelopes
are finite. For every fixed $\delta$, assume the full modulus
$u\mapsto\Phi_{j,n_f,N_f}(u;\delta)$ is nondecreasing and
$u\mapsto\Phi_{j,n_f,N_f}(u;\delta)/\sqrt{u}$ is nonincreasing for
$u>0$. Assume also that, with probability at least
$1-\delta$ for each source,
\begin{align*}
\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z_j(u)}\{-\mathbb Z_{R,j}(\zeta)\}
&\leq\psi_{R,j}(u;n_f)+\operatorname{Tail}_{R,j}(u,\delta),\\
\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z_j(u)}\{-\mathbb Z_{O,j}(\zeta)\}
&\leq\psi_{O,j}(u;N_f)+\operatorname{Tail}_{O,j}(u,\delta)
\end{align*}
simultaneously on the peeling levels used in the proof.  Then, with
probability at least $1-2\delta$,
\begin{align}
\mathcal R(\widehat\zeta_j)-\mathcal R(\zeta_j^\circ)
&\leq C_0\left\{
u_{j,n_f,N_f}^\star(\delta)
+\operatorname{Pen}(\zeta_j^\circ)
+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}\right\},
\label{eq:cate-general-localized}
\end{align}
where $C_0$ depends only on the stated sub-root and fixed-point constants.
The theorem does not assert empirical convexity; the global approximate-ERM
condition remains substantive when the source correction is signed.
\end{theorem}

An estimated density ratio changes both the population loss and the empirical
process.  Condition on the ratio-training sample and let
$u_{j,n_f,N_f}^\star(\widehat r;\delta)$ denote the preceding fixed point
for the $\widehat r$-weighted empirical loss.  Define the fitting drift
\begin{align*}
\Delta_j^{\mathrm{tune}}(u;\widehat r)
&\triangleq
\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z_j(u)}
\left|\{L_j^{\widehat r}-L_j^{r_0}\}(\zeta)
-\{L_j^{\widehat r}-L_j^{r_0}\}(\zeta_j^\circ)\right|.
\end{align*}

\begin{proposition}[General estimated-ratio fitting drift]
\label{prop:cate-general-estimated-r}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known strict-overlap
propensity, honest nuisance fitting, independent RCT and OBS tuning samples,
and an independent ratio-training sample. Suppose either
$P_R^X=P_O^X$ with $r_0=1$, or $P_R^X\ll P_O^X$ with
$r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$. Condition on the ratio-training sample. Assume the
actual $\widehat r$-weighted penalized objective is
bounded below and has a measurable global approximate minimizer, and that its
measurable localized classes have finite envelopes and sourcewise
high-probability modulus bounds. For every fixed $\delta$, assume their full
RCT-plus-OBS modulus $\Phi(u;\delta)$ is nondecreasing and
$\Phi(u;\delta)/\sqrt u$ is nonincreasing for $u>0$, including the stated
tail terms. Form the fixed point from the actual $\widehat r$-weighted loss.
Assume the localization closes at $\bar u$ in the explicit sense that
\begin{align*}
C_1\{u_{j,n_f,N_f}^\star(\widehat r;\delta)
+\Delta_j^{\mathrm{tune}}(\bar u;\widehat r)
+\operatorname{Pen}(\zeta_j^\circ)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}\}
&\leq\bar u.
\end{align*}
Then, with probability at least $1-2\delta$ conditional on the
ratio-training sample,
\begin{align*}
\mathcal R(\widehat\zeta_j^{\widehat r})-
\mathcal R(\zeta_j^\circ)
&\leq C_1\left\{
u_{j,n_f,N_f}^\star(\widehat r;\delta)
+\Delta_j^{\mathrm{tune}}(\bar u;\widehat r)
+\operatorname{Pen}(\zeta_j^\circ)
+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}\right\}.
\end{align*}
If $\|\widehat r-r_0\|_{L_2(P_O)}\leq a_{n_r,N_r}$ and
\begin{align*}
\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z_j(u)}
\|G_\zeta-G_{\zeta_j^\circ}\|_{L_2(P_O)}
&\leq B_{G,j}(u),
\end{align*}
then
$\Delta_j^{\mathrm{tune}}(u;\widehat r)
\leq|\omega|a_{n_r,N_r}B_{G,j}(u)$.
This is a fitting-drift result, not density-ratio orthogonality.
\end{proposition}

The preceding results control each fixed candidate.  We now select the fusion
coefficients while keeping the final validation observations separate.

\subsection{Choosing the fusion coefficients}
\label{sec:cate-risk-selection}

\begin{definition}[Fitted fusion learner and final-risk oracle]
Let $j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}$ label the Doubly Robust Fusion
Learner or the R-Fusion Learner, and let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R$ be a
prespecified compact set containing zero.  For fixed
$(\lambda,\omega)\in[0,1]\times\Omega$, define
$\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal Z$ as the output of a
prespecified empirical minimization rule, with deterministic tie-breaking,
applied to the empirical DRF loss in
Equation~\eqref{eq:dr-fusion-loss} or the empirical RF loss in
Equation~\eqref{eq:rf-fusion-loss}, respectively, on the tuning sample.
After the nuisance fits, tuning sample, and fitting-stage algorithmic
randomness are fixed, define its final fitted risk over a fresh
$X\sim P_R^X$ by
\begin{align*}
\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)\}
&\triangleq
\mathbb E_R\!\left[
\{\widehat\tau_j(X;\lambda,\omega)-\tau(X)\}^2
\,\middle|\,\text{fitted objects}\right].
\end{align*}
Whenever the minimum is attained, define the infeasible final-risk oracle by
\begin{align}
(\lambda_j^\star,\omega_j^\star)
&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\lambda\in[0,1],\,\omega\in\Omega}
\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)\}.
\label{eq:final-risk-oracle}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

In DRF, $\lambda$ changes $Z_\lambda$; in RF, it changes
$m_\lambda$.  In both methods, $\omega$ changes the empirical transported
OBS correction.  Hence the fitted function depends on both coefficients,
even though exact transport cancels the correction in conditional expectation
for each fixed candidate function.  Recall from
Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-ideal-risk} that for any fixed function $f$,
$\mathcal R(f)=\mathbb E_R[\{f(X)-\tau(X)\}^2]$.
Throughout this section, an efficiency gain means a reduction in the
integrated squared CATE error $\mathcal R$ of the fitted learner.  This
operational learning criterion is distinct from attaining a semiparametric
efficiency bound.  OBS information may reduce this error without changing
the RCT-population target.
This oracle is infeasible because $\tau$ is unknown, and its value changes
across fitting-sample realizations.  For both methods,
$(\lambda,\omega)=(0,0)$ gives the corresponding RCT-only learner.  Since
this pair is feasible, the oracle obeys
the conditional comparison
\begin{align}
\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda_j^\star,\omega_j^\star)\}
&\leq\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;0,0)\}.
\label{eq:conditional-rct-oracle-comparison}
\end{align}
This is an oracle comparison, not a guarantee for estimated coefficients.
There is no universal closed form for Equation~\eqref{eq:final-risk-oracle}.

Two simpler closed forms minimize the base-loss noise floors rather than the
fitted risk.  Conditional on honestly fitted and now fixed nuisances, the
fixed-function decompositions from Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target} are
\begin{align*}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda)
&=Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)+\mathcal R(\zeta),
&\mathcal L_R(\zeta;\lambda)
&=Q_R(\lambda)+\mathcal R(\zeta).
\end{align*}
Recall that $Z_0=\varphi(V;\widehat\mu_R)$ and
$Z_1=\varphi(V;\widehat\mu_O)$, while $m_R$ and $m_O$ are the corresponding
RCT and OBS marginal-outcome nuisances.  Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target} defines $Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)$ and
$Q_R(\lambda)$, respectively.  These terms do not equal the ideal risk
of a fitted CATE function.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-form noise-floor coefficients]
\label{prop:cate-noise-floor-lambda}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known propensity
$e$ satisfying strict overlap, and nuisance functions fitted honestly on
data independent of the observations used below.  Condition on these fixed
nuisance fits and suppose the displayed expectations are finite.  Let
$\widehat\mu_R=(\widehat\mu_{R,0},\widehat\mu_{R,1})$ and $\widehat\mu_O=(\widehat\mu_{O,0},\widehat\mu_{O,1})$ be the fixed RCT and
OBS outcome-regression pairs, and define
\begin{align*}
Z_0&\triangleq\varphi(V;\widehat\mu_R),
&Z_1&\triangleq\varphi(V;\widehat\mu_O),\\
m_R(X)&\triangleq e(X)\widehat\mu_{R,1}(X)+\{1-e(X)\}\widehat\mu_{R,0}(X),\\
m_O(X)&\triangleq e(X)\widehat\mu_{O,1}(X)+\{1-e(X)\}\widehat\mu_{O,0}(X),\\
\Delta&\triangleq Z_1-Z_0,
&d_m(X)&\triangleq m_O(X)-m_R(X).
\end{align*}
For $Z_\lambda\triangleq Z_0+\lambda\Delta$ and
$m_\lambda\triangleq m_R+\lambda d_m$, define
\begin{align*}
Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)
&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\tau(X)\}^2,\\
Q_R(\lambda)
&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\left[
\frac{[Y-m_\lambda(X)-\{A-e(X)\}\tau(X)]^2}
{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}\right].
\end{align*}
For an interval, the projection used below is
$\operatorname{Proj}_{[0,1]}(u)\triangleq\min\{1,\max(0,u)\}$.
If $\mathbb E_R(\Delta^2)>0$, the constrained DR noise-floor minimizer is
\begin{align}
\lambda_{\mathrm{DR},Q}^{\star}
&\triangleq\operatorname{Proj}_{[0,1]}
\left\{-\frac{\mathbb E_R(Z_0\Delta)}{\mathbb E_R(\Delta^2)}\right\}.
\label{eq:cate-dr-noise-lambda}
\end{align}
If
$\mathbb E_R[d_m(X)^2/\{e(X)(1-e(X))\}]>0$, the constrained R
noise-floor minimizer is
\begin{align}
\lambda_{R,Q}^{\star}
&\triangleq\operatorname{Proj}_{[0,1]}\left[
\frac{
\mathbb E_R[\{Y-m_R(X)\}d_m(X)/\{e(X)(1-e(X))\}]
}{
\mathbb E_R[d_m(X)^2/\{e(X)(1-e(X))\}]
}\right].
\label{eq:cate-r-noise-lambda}
\end{align}
If either denominator is zero, its corresponding noise floor is flat in
$\lambda$, so every $\lambda\in[0,1]$ is a minimizer.  The displayed
equivalent moments do not involve $\tau$.
\end{proposition}

The two coefficients in Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-noise-floor-lambda} are
population noise-floor oracles.  They are neither fitted-risk optimizers in
Equation~\eqref{eq:final-risk-oracle} nor data-adaptive coefficient estimates.

\begin{proposition}[Population-loss flatness in the fusion scale]
\label{prop:cate-omega-flatness}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known trial
propensity $e$ satisfying strict overlap, and nuisance functions fitted
honestly on data independent of the loss-evaluation observations.  Suppose
either that the RCT and OBS have a common covariate marginal with $r_0=1$,
or that exact covariate transport holds with
$r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$.  Use $r=r_0$, let
$\Omega\subset\mathbb R$ be nonempty and compact, and fix
$\zeta\in\mathcal Z$ and $\lambda\in[0,1]$.  Condition on the fitted
nuisances and suppose the expectations below are finite.  For every
$\omega\in\Omega$, the method-specific corrections satisfy
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_O\{r_0(X)G_\zeta(X)\}-\mathbb E_R\{G_\zeta(X)\}
&=0,\\
\mathbb E_O\{r_0(X)G_\zeta(X)\}
-\mathbb E_R\{\chi(A,X)G_\zeta(X)\}
&=0.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}
(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}
&=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda),\\
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}
(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}
&=\mathcal L_R(\zeta;\lambda),
\end{align*}
and both right-hand sides are constant in $\omega$.  Therefore,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\omega\in\Omega}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}
(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}
&=\Omega,\\
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\omega\in\Omega}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}
(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}
&=\Omega.
\end{align*}
Thus population loss at a fixed function and fixed $\lambda$ cannot select
a unique fusion scale.
\end{proposition}

Although the conditional expected full-fusion loss at fixed $\zeta$ and
fixed $\lambda$ is flat in $\omega$ under exact $r_0$, the fitted
function depends on $\omega$; we therefore compare fully fitted
coefficient-grid functions by honest validation, after all fitted objects are
fixed.

\begin{definition}[Honest finite-grid candidates]
Fix $j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}$, its function class, and its
penalty.  Let $\mathcal G_j\subseteq[0,1]\times\Omega$ be a prespecified
nonempty finite grid containing $(0,0)$, and fit
$\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)$ before validation for every
$(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j$.  Define the infeasible grid oracle by
\begin{align*}
(\lambda_j^{\mathcal G},\omega_j^{\mathcal G})
&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j}
\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)\}.
\end{align*}
The continuous oracle in Equation~\eqref{eq:final-risk-oracle} searches all of
$[0,1]\times\Omega$; this grid oracle searches only $\mathcal G_j$; the
empirical selector below minimizes an observed validation criterion.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Common-reference validation score]
\label{def:cate-finite-grid-validation}
Set
$Z_0\triangleq\varphi(V;\widehat\mu_R)$, where $\widehat\mu_R$ is fixed before validation,
and let $\widehat{g}$ be the fitted OBS treatment-effect prediction in
Equation~\eqref{eq:obs-effect-prediction}.  Under a common covariate marginal
use $r_0=1$; under covariate shift use the exact ratio
$r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$.  Let $\mathbb P_{R,n}$ and
$\mathbb P_{O,N}$ denote empirical averages over the RCT and
OBS evaluation samples.  Define
\begin{align*}
G_{j,\lambda,\omega}(X)
&\triangleq\{\widehat{g}(X)-\widehat\tau_j(X;\lambda,\omega)\}^2,\\
S_{R,j,\lambda,\omega}
&\triangleq\{Z_0-\widehat\tau_j(X;\lambda,\omega)\}^2
-\omega G_{j,\lambda,\omega}(X),\\
S_{O,j,\lambda,\omega}
&\triangleq\omega r_0(X)G_{j,\lambda,\omega}(X).
\end{align*}
The validation criterion is
\begin{align}
\widehat C_j(\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq
\mathbb P_{R,n}S_{R,j,\lambda,\omega}
+\mathbb P_{O,N}S_{O,j,\lambda,\omega}.
\label{eq:common-reference-validation}
\end{align}
Equivalently, this sample quantity is the RCT pseudo-outcome loss plus
$\omega\{\mathbb P_{O,N}(r_0G_{j,\lambda,\omega})-
\mathbb P_{R,n}(G_{j,\lambda,\omega})\}$.  The minus sign creates a transported
OBS-minus-RCT control variate: under exact transport its conditional expected
correction is zero.  A plus sign would not have this property.
Using deterministic tie-breaking, define
\begin{align*}
(\widehat\lambda_j,\widehat\omega_j)
&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j}
\widehat C_j(\lambda,\omega).
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Common-noise validation identity]
\label{prop:cate-validation-identity}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known trial
propensity $e$ satisfying strict overlap, and honest nuisance fitting.
Condition on the fitted grid functions and fitting randomness, and suppose
the validation observations are independent of these objects.  Suppose
either $P_R^X=P_O^X$ and $r_0=1$, or $P_R^X\ll P_O^X$ and
$r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$.  Fix $\widehat\mu_R$ before validation, so
Equation~\eqref{eq:pseudo-valid} gives
$\mathbb E_R(Z_0\mid X)=\tau(X)$, and suppose the displayed expectations
are finite.  Then, for every $(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j$,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{eval}}\!\left[
\widehat C_j(\lambda,\omega)\mid\text{fitted objects}\right]
&=Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(0)
+\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)\},
\label{eq:common-reference-identity}
\end{align}
where $Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(0)\triangleq
\mathbb E_R\{Z_0-\tau(X)\}^2$.
This is a conditional-expectation identity over fresh validation observations,
not an equality for each realized evaluation sample and not an unbiased
estimate of $\mathcal R$ itself.  It identifies candidate risk differences
because the additive term $Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(0)$ is common to every candidate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Honest selection of CATE fusion coefficients}
\label{alg:cate-fusion-selection}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{The objects in Definition~\ref{def:cate-finite-grid-validation} and
independent nuisance, tuning, and evaluation samples.}
Fit the nuisances on the nuisance sample.\;
For every $(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j$, fit
$\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)$ on the tuning sample.\;
Compute $\widehat C_j(\lambda,\omega)$ in
Equation~\eqref{eq:common-reference-validation} for every grid pair on the
common evaluation samples.\;
Select $(\widehat\lambda_j,\widehat\omega_j)$ by minimizing
$\widehat C_j$ over $\mathcal G_j$.\;
\KwOut{$(\widehat\lambda_j,\widehat\omega_j)$ and
$\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\widehat\lambda_j,\widehat\omega_j)$.}
\end{algorithm}

Let $M\triangleq|\mathcal G_j|$, let $n_v,N_v$ be the RCT and OBS
validation sizes, and suppose the RCT and OBS source-score ranges have lengths
at most $B_R$ and $B_O$.  Define
\begin{align*}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta)
&\triangleq
B_R\sqrt{\frac{\log(4M/\delta)}{2n_v}}
+B_O\sqrt{\frac{\log(4M/\delta)}{2N_v}}.
\end{align*}
This is a uniform finite-grid validation-error radius obtained from Hoeffding
concentration and a union bound over the $M$ candidates.  It is not a
Brownian or Gaussian-process radius.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-grid honest validation]
\label{thm:cate-finite-validation}
Use the objects in Definition~\ref{def:cate-finite-grid-validation}.  Assume
RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known propensity $e$
satisfying strict overlap, and honest nuisance fitting.  Condition on the
finite grid, all fitted functions, and fitting randomness, all of which must
be independent of final validation.  Let the RCT and OBS evaluation samples
be mutually independent.  Suppose either $P_R^X=P_O^X$ and $r_0=1$, or
$P_R^X\ll P_O^X$ and $r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$.  Fix $\widehat\mu_R$ before
validation so that $\mathbb E_R(Z_0\mid X)=\tau(X)$.  Suppose the source
scores $S_{R,j,\lambda,\omega}$ and $S_{O,j,\lambda,\omega}$ have
uniform range lengths at most $B_R$ and $B_O$, respectively, over
$\mathcal G_j$.
Then, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{align}
\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j
(\cdot;\widehat\lambda_j,\widehat\omega_j)\}
&\leq\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j
(\cdot;\lambda_j^{\mathcal G},\omega_j^{\mathcal G})\}
+2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta)
\leq\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;0,0)\}
+2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta).
\label{eq:cate-selector-inequality}
\end{align}
This is not realized-sample universal dominance.
It also does not establish coefficient consistency, approximation to the
continuous oracle, strict improvement, or unconditional dominance.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Estimated-ratio validation]
\label{cor:cate-estimated-r-validation}
Use the objects in Definition~\ref{def:cate-finite-grid-validation}.  Assume
RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known propensity $e$
satisfying strict overlap, and honest nuisance fitting.  Condition on the
finite grid, all fitted functions, and fitting randomness, all independent
of mutually independent RCT and OBS final-evaluation samples.  Suppose
either $P_R^X=P_O^X$ and $r_0=1$, or $P_R^X\ll P_O^X$ and
$r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$.  Fix $\widehat\mu_R$ before validation so that
$\mathbb E_R(Z_0\mid X)=\tau(X)$.  Let an evaluation-independent
$\widehat r$ replace $r_0$ in the OBS validation score, and condition on
$\widehat r$.  Define
\begin{align*}
S_{O,j,\lambda,\omega}^{\widehat r}
&\triangleq\omega\widehat r(X)G_{j,\lambda,\omega}(X),\\
\widehat C_j^{\widehat r}(\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq\mathbb P_{R,n}S_{R,j,\lambda,\omega}
+\mathbb P_{O,N}S_{O,j,\lambda,\omega}^{\widehat r}.
\end{align*}
Suppose the actual RCT and OBS scores have uniform range lengths at most
$\widehat B_R$ and $\widehat B_O$, and define
\begin{align}
\widehat\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta)
&\triangleq
\widehat B_R\sqrt{\frac{\log(4M/\delta)}{2n_v}}
+\widehat B_O\sqrt{\frac{\log(4M/\delta)}{2N_v}},\notag\\
\Delta_r
&\triangleq
\max_{(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j}
\left|\omega\mathbb E_O[
(\widehat r-r_0)G_{j,\lambda,\omega}]\right|.
\label{eq:validation-drift-bounds}
\end{align}
Let $(\widehat\lambda_j^{\widehat r},
\widehat\omega_j^{\widehat r})$ be the deterministically tie-broken
minimizer of $\widehat C_j^{\widehat r}$ over $\mathcal G_j$.  Then,
with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{align*}
\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;
\widehat\lambda_j^{\widehat r},\widehat\omega_j^{\widehat r})\}
&\leq
\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;
\lambda_j^{\mathcal G},\omega_j^{\mathcal G})\}
+2\widehat\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta)+2\Delta_r\\
&\leq
\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;0,0)\}
+2\widehat\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta)+2\Delta_r.
\end{align*}
This does not give realized-sample universal dominance.
Section~\ref{sec:ratio-estimation} constructs candidate ratio estimators,
including classifier, Riesz-representer, and balancing estimators.  This
corollary only propagates the drift of a supplied validation-independent
$\widehat r$; it neither constructs $\widehat r$ nor establishes
orthogonality with respect to the density-ratio direction.
\end{corollary}

The general fitting and validation results combine only after the fitting
events hold simultaneously over the finite candidate set.  The next theorem
makes that requirement explicit.
\begin{theorem}[Risk of the honestly selected general learner]
\label{thm:cate-general-selected-risk}
Let $\widehat\tau_1,\ldots,\widehat\tau_M$ be $M<\infty$ fitted
candidates fixed before final validation and assume
RCT consistency and conditional randomization, known strict-overlap trial
propensity, honest nuisance fitting, independent RCT and OBS tuning samples,
and final RCT and OBS evaluation samples independent of all candidate fitting.
Suppose either the covariate marginals agree and $r_0=1$, or exact transport
holds with $r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$. Allow independently trained ratios in fitting
and validation and condition on them. For candidate $k$, let
$\zeta_k^\circ$ be its best-in-class target, let
$u_k^\star\triangleq
u_{k,n_f,N_f}^\star(\widehat r_k;\delta_f/M)$, and define
\begin{align*}
B_k
&\triangleq\mathcal R(\zeta_k^\circ)
+C_k\{u_k^\star+\Delta_k^{\mathrm{tune}}
+\operatorname{Pen}_k(\zeta_k^\circ)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt},k}\}.
\end{align*}
Assume directly that
$\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_k)\leq B_k$ for every
$k=1,\ldots,M$ simultaneously with probability at least
$1-\delta_f$.
Let $\widehat k$ be the deterministically tie-broken index selected by the
common-reference criterion in Equation~\eqref{eq:common-reference-validation}.
Let $\widehat\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta_v)$ and
$\Delta_r^{\mathrm{eval}}\triangleq\Delta_r$ be the validation concentration
radius and validation ratio drift in
Equation~\eqref{eq:validation-drift-bounds}, respectively. Assume the
validation-selection event
\begin{align*}
\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_{\widehat k})
&\leq \min_{1\leq k\leq M}\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_k)
+2\widehat\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta_v)
+2\Delta_r^{\mathrm{eval}}
\end{align*}
holds with probability at least $1-\delta_v$. Then, with probability at least
$1-\delta_f-\delta_v$,
\begin{align}
\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_{\widehat k})
&\leq\min_{1\leq k\leq M}B_k
+2\widehat\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\delta_v)
+2\Delta_r^{\mathrm{eval}}.
\label{eq:cate-general-selected-risk}
\end{align}
Here $\Delta_k^{\mathrm{tune}}$ changes a fitted candidate, whereas
$\Delta_r^{\mathrm{eval}}$ changes how fixed candidates are ranked.  The
result does not imply coefficient consistency, strict improvement, or
approximation of the continuous coefficient oracle.
\end{theorem}

We next instantiate the abstract fitting bound and candidate construction in
a class for which the estimator, covariance constants, and approximation rate
are explicit.

\subsection{Linear-sieve and additive-spline specialization}
\label{sec:cate-sieve}

This specialization has two roles. It computes every fixed
$(\lambda,\omega)$ candidate used in Section~\ref{sec:cate-risk-selection},
and it turns the abstract two-source modulus into explicit covariance and
approximation terms.

\begin{definition}[Linear-sieve fusion estimator]
Let $b(x)\in\mathbb R^p$ be fixed or honestly trained and define
$\zeta_\beta(x)\triangleq b(x)^\top\beta$.  Let $P=P^\top\succeq0$ and
$\rho\geq0$.  For $j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}$, define
\begin{align*}
\widehat J_j(\beta;\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq
\widehat L_j(\zeta_\beta;\lambda,\omega)+\rho\beta^\top P\beta,\\
\widehat\beta_{\lambda,\omega}^j
&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\beta\in\mathbb R^p}
\widehat J_j(\beta;\lambda,\omega),\\
\widehat\tau_j(x;\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq b(x)^\top\widehat\beta_{\lambda,\omega}^j,
\end{align*}
using deterministic tie-breaking whenever needed.
\end{definition}

For DRF, define
\begin{align*}
\widehat\Gamma_R&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}(bb^\top),
&\widehat\Gamma_{O,r}&\triangleq\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}(r_0bb^\top),\\
\widehat h_\lambda&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}(bZ_\lambda),
&\widehat d_{\mathrm{OBS}}&\triangleq
\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}(r_0b\widehat{g})
-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}(b\widehat{g}).
\end{align*}
For RF, let
\begin{align*}
s_{m_\lambda}&\triangleq
\frac{(A-e)(Y-m_\lambda)}{e(1-e)},
&\widehat\Gamma_{R,\chi}
&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}(\chi bb^\top),\\
\widehat h_{m_\lambda}&\triangleq
\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}(bs_{m_\lambda}),
&\widehat d_{\mathrm{OBS},\chi}&\triangleq
\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}(r_0b\widehat{g})
-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}(\chi b\widehat{g}).
\end{align*}

\begin{proposition}[Normal equations and positive semidefiniteness]
\label{prop:cate-sieve-normal-equations}
For DRF and RF, respectively, define
\begin{align*}
\widehat H_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\omega)
&\triangleq(1-\omega)\widehat\Gamma_R
+\omega\widehat\Gamma_{O,r},
&\widehat h_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq\widehat h_\lambda+\omega\widehat d_{\mathrm{OBS}},\\
\widehat H_{\mathrm{RF}}(\omega)
&\triangleq(1-\omega)\widehat\Gamma_{R,\chi}
+\omega\widehat\Gamma_{O,r},
&\widehat h_{\mathrm{RF}}(\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq\widehat h_{m_\lambda}+\omega\widehat d_{\mathrm{OBS},\chi}.
\end{align*}
The normal equation is
\begin{align*}
\{\widehat H_j(\omega)+\rho P\}
\widehat\beta_{\lambda,\omega}^j
&=\widehat h_j(\lambda,\omega).
\end{align*}
When the matrix is invertible,
\begin{align}
\widehat\beta_{\lambda,\omega}^{\mathrm{DRF}}
&=\{\widehat H_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\omega)+\rho P\}^{-1}
\widehat h_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\lambda,\omega),
\label{eq:linear-sieve}\\
\widehat\beta_{\lambda,\omega}^{\mathrm{RF}}
&=\{\widehat H_{\mathrm{RF}}(\omega)+\rho P\}^{-1}
\widehat h_{\mathrm{RF}}(\lambda,\omega).
\label{eq:linear-sieve-rf}
\end{align}
If $0\leq\omega\leq1$, the ratio weights are nonnegative, and
$P\succeq0$, both penalized Hessians are positive semidefinite.  The signed
source representation alone does not imply invertibility.  A fit using
$\widehat r$ requires nonnegative weights, a direct Hessian check, or a
prespecified positive-semidefinite projection.
\end{proposition}

For fixed $\omega$, the Hessian does not depend on $\lambda$, while the
right-hand side is affine.  This yields an efficient candidate-generation
device, not a replacement for final honest validation.
\begin{proposition}[Fixed-scale profiling for candidate generation]
\label{prop:cate-sieve-lambda-profile}
Suppose $\widehat H_j(\omega)+\rho P$ is invertible and write
\begin{align*}
\widehat h_j(\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq\widehat h_{j,0}(\omega)
+\lambda\widehat h_{j,1}(\omega).
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{align}
\widehat\beta_{\lambda,\omega}^j
&=\widehat\beta_{0,\omega}^j+\lambda\dot\beta_\omega^j,
&\dot\beta_\omega^j
&\triangleq\{\widehat H_j(\omega)+\rho P\}^{-1}
\widehat h_{j,1}(\omega).
\label{eq:linear-sieve-lambda-profile}
\end{align}
On a separate tuning sample, the corresponding quadratic criterion has the
form
\begin{align}
\widehat C_j^{\mathrm{tune}}(\lambda,\omega)
&=c_0(\omega)+2c_1(\omega)\lambda+c_2(\omega)\lambda^2.
\label{eq:linear-sieve-validation-profile}
\end{align}
For a prespecified finite $\omega$-grid, checking the admissible stationary
point and the endpoints of $[0,1]$ generates finitely many candidates.
Those candidates must be fixed before the final validation used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-finite-validation}.  Continuous profiling on the final
evaluation sample requires a separate uniform-convergence argument.
\end{proposition}

We now separate sieve approximation from estimation. Define
\begin{align*}
\Gamma_R&\triangleq\mathbb E_R(bb^\top),
&\gamma_R&\triangleq\mathbb E_R(b\tau),\\
\beta_p&\triangleq\Gamma_R^{-1}\gamma_R,
&\zeta_p&\triangleq b^\top\beta_p,
&a_p^2&\triangleq\mathbb E_R(\tau-\zeta_p)^2,
\end{align*}
and assume $\Gamma_R\succ0$.  For a fixed candidate, let
$\widehat H_j\triangleq\widehat H_j(\omega)$,
$\widehat h_j\triangleq\widehat h_j(\lambda,\omega)$, and define
\begin{align*}
\widehat u_j&\triangleq\widehat h_j-\widehat H_j\beta_p.
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}[Exact random-Hessian sieve identity]
\label{thm:cate-sieve-identity}
For any realized tuning samples and fixed fitted nuisances, if
$\widehat H_j+\rho P$ is invertible, then
\begin{align}
\widehat\beta_j-\beta_p
&=(\widehat H_j+\rho P)^{-1}
\{\widehat u_j-\rho P\beta_p\},
\label{eq:sieve-coefficient-identity}\\
\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_j)
&=a_p^2+(\widehat\beta_j-\beta_p)^\top
\Gamma_R(\widehat\beta_j-\beta_p).
\label{eq:sieve-risk-identity}
\end{align}
The identity is exact, but it is not a same-sample bias-variance
decomposition because the random inverse Hessian and score are dependent.
\end{theorem}

For the concentration result, define the raw source contributions explicitly:
\begin{align*}
\psi_{R,\mathrm{DRF}}
&\triangleq b\{Z_\lambda-\omega\widehat{g}-(1-\omega)\zeta_p\},
&\psi_{O,\mathrm{DRF}}
&\triangleq\omega r_0b(\widehat{g}-\zeta_p),\\
\psi_{R,\mathrm{RF}}
&\triangleq b[s_{m_\lambda}
-\chi\{\omega \widehat{g}+(1-\omega)\zeta_p\}],
&\psi_{O,\mathrm{RF}}
&\triangleq\omega r_0b(\widehat{g}-\zeta_p).
\end{align*}
Under the exact-target conditions,
$\mathbb E_R\psi_{R,j}+\mathbb E_O\psi_{O,j}=0$; the two source means need
not vanish separately.  Let
\begin{align*}
\Sigma_{R,j}&\triangleq\operatorname{Var}_R(\psi_{R,j}),
&\Sigma_{O,j}&\triangleq\operatorname{Var}_O(\psi_{O,j}).
\end{align*}
For $S\in\{R,O\}$, define
\begin{align*}
\nu_{S,j}
&\triangleq
\|\Gamma_R^{-1/2}\Sigma_{S,j}\Gamma_R^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-candidate covariance-sensitive bound]
\label{thm:cate-fixed-candidate-bound}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, known strict-overlap
$e$, exact $r_0$, honestly fixed nuisances and basis, fixed
$(j,\lambda,\omega,p,\rho)$, and independent tuning samples of sizes
$n_f,N_f$.  Assume the centered source vectors are sub-Gaussian with the
displayed covariance proxies and the weighted designs satisfy, with failure
probability at most $\delta_H$,
\begin{align*}
\mathcal E_H
&\triangleq\{\|\Gamma_R^{-1/2}
(\widehat H_j-\Gamma_R)\Gamma_R^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq\eta\},\qquad 0\leq\eta<1.
\end{align*}
Then, with probability at least $1-\delta_H-\delta_S$,
\begin{align}
\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_j)
&\leq a_p^2+\frac{C_{\mathrm{sg}}}{(1-\eta)^2}
\left[
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_R^{-1}\Sigma_{R,j})
+\nu_{R,j}\log(4/\delta_S)}{n_f}
\right.\nonumber\\
&\left.\qquad+
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_R^{-1}\Sigma_{O,j})
+\nu_{O,j}\log(4/\delta_S)}{N_f}
\right]
+\frac{C_\rho\rho^2}{(1-\eta)^2}
\|\Gamma_R^{-1/2}P\beta_p\|_2^2,
\label{eq:finite-sieve-bound}
\end{align}
where $\nu_{S,j}$ bounds the corresponding whitened covariance operator
norm and $C_{\mathrm{sg}},C_\rho$ are universal constants under the stated
sub-Gaussian convention. Independence makes the source covariance proxies add;
the bound has no artificial $1/\sqrt{n_fN_f}$ cross term.
\end{theorem}

The preceding theorem is a safe finite-sample upper bound.  A trace expression
for the leading risk requires a separate fixed-dimensional asymptotic result.
\begin{proposition}[Fixed-dimensional leading expected risk]
\label{prop:cate-fixed-p-leading-risk}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, known strict-overlap
$e$, honest fixed nuisances and basis, and either a common covariate
marginal with $r_0=1$ or $P_R^X\ll P_O^X$ with exact
$r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$. Fix $(j,\lambda,\omega,p)$, and suppose the RCT and OBS tuning samples are independent with
$n_f,N_f\to\infty$ at a stable sample-size ratio. Assume finite
$(2+\delta)$-moments, $\Gamma_R\succ0$, Gram convergence, and first-order
negligible ridge. Define the linearization remainder
\begin{align*}
R_{n_f,N_f}
&\triangleq \widehat\beta_j-\beta_p
-\Gamma_R^{-1}\left[
(\mathbb P_R-P_R)\psi_{R,j}
+(\mathbb P_O-P_O)\psi_{O,j}\right],
\end{align*}
and assume explicitly that
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E\|R_{n_f,N_f}\|_{\Gamma_R}^2
&=o(n_f^{-1}+N_f^{-1}),
\qquad \|a\|_{\Gamma_R}^2\triangleq a^\top\Gamma_Ra.
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{align*}
\widehat\beta_j-\beta_p
&=\Gamma_R^{-1}\left[
(\mathbb P_R-P_R)\psi_{R,j}
+(\mathbb P_O-P_O)\psi_{O,j}\right]
+R_{n_f,N_f},
\end{align*}
and
\begin{align}
\mathbb E\{\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_j)\}-a_p^2
&=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_R^{-1}\Sigma_{R,j})}{n_f}
+\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_R^{-1}\Sigma_{O,j})}{N_f}
+o(n_f^{-1}+N_f^{-1}).
\label{eq:cate-fixed-p-leading-risk}
\end{align}
This is an asymptotic expected leading-risk expansion, not an exact
finite-sample identity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[First-order RCT versus fusion comparison]
\label{cor:cate-rct-fusion-leading-risk}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, known strict-overlap
$e$, honest fixed nuisances and basis, and either a common covariate
marginal with $r_0=1$ or $P_R^X\ll P_O^X$ with exact
$r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$. Assume independent RCT
and OBS tuning samples with $n_f,N_f\to\infty$ at a stable sample-size
ratio. Consider a fixed fusion rule $(\lambda,\omega)$ and the strict
RCT-only comparator $(\lambda,\omega)=(0,0)$. The two rules use the same basis, penalty, RCT
nuisance, and sample split. Assume finite $(2+\delta)$-moments,
$\Gamma_R\succ0$, Gram convergence, first-order negligible ridge, and for
each rule the mean-square linearization remainder is
$o(n_f^{-1}+N_f^{-1})$. Their difference in first-order expected excess
risk is
\begin{align}
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{FUS}}^{(1)}-\mathcal V_{\mathrm{RCT}}^{(1)}
&\triangleq
\frac{\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
\Gamma_R^{-1}\{\Sigma_{R,j}(\lambda,\omega)
-\Sigma_{R,j}(0,0)\}\right]}{n_f}
+\frac{\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
\Gamma_R^{-1}\Sigma_{O,j}(\lambda,\omega)\right]}{N_f}.
\label{eq:cate-rct-fusion-leading-gap}
\end{align}
The fixed fusion rule improves on the strict RCT-only comparator at first
order if and only if the right-hand side of
Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-rct-fusion-leading-gap} is negative. Equality does
not order the higher-order remainders, and this comparison gives no universal
finite-sample dominance.
\end{corollary}

An estimated ratio can perturb both the normal-equation right-hand side and
its Hessian. Define
\begin{align}
\widehat\Delta_{h,j}
&\triangleq\omega\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}
[(\widehat r-r_0)b\widehat{g}],
\label{eq:cate-ratio-score-drift}\\
\widehat\Delta_{H,j}
&\triangleq\omega\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}
[(\widehat r-r_0)bb^\top].
\label{eq:cate-ratio-hessian-drift}
\end{align}
\begin{proposition}[Estimated-ratio perturbation of a fitted sieve]
\label{prop:cate-sieve-ratio-perturbation}
Condition on an independent ratio-training sample and use the same candidate
specification with $r_0$ and $\widehat r$. If both penalized Hessians are
invertible, then
\begin{align*}
\widehat\beta_j^{\widehat r}-\widehat\beta_j^{r_0}
&=\{\widehat H_j^{\widehat r}+\rho P\}^{-1}
\{\widehat\Delta_{h,j}
-\widehat\Delta_{H,j}\widehat\beta_j^{r_0}\}.
\end{align*}
Thus ratio normalization alone controls neither the score nor Hessian
perturbation. This proposition instantiates the fitting drift in
Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-general-estimated-r}; it is not an orthogonality
result.
\end{proposition}

Finally, the additive specialization exposes how the two sample sizes enter
the rate through method-specific variance constants rather than a generic
effective sample size.
\begin{corollary}[Operational additive-spline rate]
\label{cor:cate-spline-rate}
Suppose $\tau(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^d\tau_\ell(x_\ell)$, with fixed $d$ and
smoothness $s>0$.  Let $K$ denote the spline dimension and suppose
$a_K^2=O(K^{-2s})$. Assume RCT consistency and randomization, known
strict-overlap $e$, exact $r_0$ unless an explicit fitting-drift term is
retained, honest fixed nuisances and basis, independent tuning samples, and
uniformly bounded eigenvalues of $\Gamma_R$. Assume the centered source
scores and weighted designs have uniformly bounded sub-Gaussian norms,
$K+\log(1/\delta)=o\{\min(n_f,N_f)\}$, and the Gram event holds with
probability tending to one. Define the per-dimension
variance scale
\begin{align*}
c_{S,j}(K;\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq K^{-1}\operatorname{tr}
\{\Gamma_R(K)^{-1}\Sigma_{S,j}^{(K)}(\lambda,\omega)\},
\qquad S\in\{R,O\},\\
v_j(K;\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq
\frac{c_{R,j}(K;\lambda,\omega)}{n_f}
+\frac{c_{O,j}(K;\lambda,\omega)}{N_f},
\end{align*}
and suppress $\lambda$ from $c_{O,j}$ only when the OBS score contribution
is invariant to it.
If the fitting ratio drift and ridge bias are $B_{r,j}^{\mathrm{tune}}$ and
$B_{\rho,j}$, then
\begin{align}
\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_j)
&=O_p\{K^{-2s}+Kv_j(K;\lambda,\omega)
+B_{r,j}^{\mathrm{tune}}+B_{\rho,j}\}.
\label{eq:cate-spline-rate}
\end{align}
For a prespecified admissible dimension set $\mathcal K_n$, define the
noncircular oracle dimension by
\begin{align*}
K_j^\star(\lambda,\omega)
&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{K\in\mathcal K_n}
\{K^{-2s}+K v_j(K;\lambda,\omega)\}.
\end{align*}
If, along the admissible sequence and uniformly over the coefficient
candidates under consideration,
$c_{S,j}(K;\lambda,\omega)\to
c_{S,j}^\infty(\lambda,\omega)$, where the limiting constants are uniformly
bounded away from zero and infinity, define
\begin{align*}
v_j^\infty(\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq
\frac{c_{R,j}^\infty(\lambda,\omega)}{n_f}
+\frac{c_{O,j}^\infty(\lambda,\omega)}{N_f}.
\end{align*}
Then a rate-optimal admissible choice satisfies
$K\asymp\{v_j^\infty(\lambda,\omega)\}^{-1/(2s+1)}$, and the first two
terms are of order
$\{v_j^\infty(\lambda,\omega)\}^{2s/(2s+1)}$. For a validation-selected learner, the
validation radius and validation ratio drift from
Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-general-selected-risk} must also be added.  The harmonic
size $(1/n_f+1/N_f)^{-1}$ is only a rough simplification when the two
variance constants have comparable, uniformly bounded order.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Implementation boundaries and open theory}
\label{sec:cate-scope}

A fixed or honestly trained frozen neural representation can replace $b$,
conditional on that representation. End-to-end neural fitting is nonconvex and
is not covered. Estimated-ratio guarantees require uniform score, Hessian, and
validation-loss control. Cross-fitting can recover data efficiency, but
overlapping outer training sets do not justify a naive sum of fold variances.
Neither target preservation nor candidate inclusion gives universal
finite-sample improvement for estimated $(\lambda,\omega)$.
