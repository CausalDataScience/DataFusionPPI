\section{Data Fusion for CATE Estimation}
\label{sec:cate}

In this section, we develop two estimators of the CATE $\tau$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:cate} that combine  RCT data with  potentially confounded OBS data. The RCT identifies the causal target. The OBS outcome regressions and their treatment-effect prediction may improve statistical efficiency, but they need not be causally valid due to unmeasured confounders. The first estimator is the Doubly Robust Fusion (DRF) Learner, based on the AIPW pseudo-outcome of the DR-learner \citep{kennedy2022drlearner}. The second is the R-Fusion (RF) Learner, based on the R-learner loss \citep{nie2020rlearner} scaled by the known trial propensity variance.


\subsection{CATE targets and RCT-valid learning losses}
\label{sec:cate-loss}


The CATE estimators of this section minimize empirical versions of the following risk.
\begin{definition}[CATE risk]
\label{def:cate-class-risk}
Let $\mathcal Z\subseteq L_2(P_R^X)$ be a prespecified nonempty class of candidate CATE functions, such as a fixed linear sieve. For $\zeta\in\mathcal Z$, define the CATE risk
\begin{align}
\mathcal R(\zeta)&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{\zeta(X)-\tau(X)\}^2.
\label{eq:cate-ideal-risk}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

Recall the fused score $Z_\lambda=\varphi(V;\widehat\mu_\lambda)$ of Definition~\ref{def:fusion-score}. Throughout this section $\lambda\in[0,1]$, so that $\widehat\mu_\lambda$ in Definition~\ref{def:fusion-score} is a convex combination of the two fitted regressions $\widehat\mu_R$ and $\widehat\mu_O$. Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:honest-separation} and~\ref{ass:trial}, recall that $\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda\mid X)=\tau(X)$ for every fixed $\lambda$. Recall also the OBS prediction $\widehat g\triangleq\widehat\mu_{O,1}-\widehat\mu_{O,0}$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:obs-effect-prediction}, and write $G_\zeta(X)\triangleq\{\widehat g(X)-\zeta(X)\}^2$ for the squared distance between the OBS prediction and the candidate CATE function $\zeta$.

We first define the two population risks that the DRF and RF estimators target, and then their empirical versions.

\begin{definition}[RCT population risks]
\label{def:rct-risks}
For $\lambda\in[0,1]$ and a candidate CATE function $\zeta\in\mathcal Z$, define the doubly robust (DR) risk by
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda)&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\zeta(X)\}^2.
\label{eq:dr-risk}
\end{align}
Let $m_\lambda$ be the estimate of the RCT marginal outcome regression $\mathbb E_R[Y\mid X]$ built from the same fitted regressions as $\widehat\mu_\lambda$,
\begin{align*}
	m_R(x)&\triangleq e(x)\widehat\mu_{R,1}(x)+\{1-e(x)\}\widehat\mu_{R,0}(x), \\ 
	m_O(x)&\triangleq e(x)\widehat\mu_{O,1}(x)+\{1-e(x)\}\widehat\mu_{O,0}(x), \\
	m_\lambda&\triangleq(1-\lambda)m_R+\lambda m_O,
\end{align*}
and define the R risk by
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_R(\zeta;\lambda)&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\left[\frac{[\,Y-m_\lambda(X)-\{A-e(X)\}\zeta(X)\,]^2}{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}\right].
\label{eq:r-risk}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

The DR risk is the mean squared error of regressing the RCT-valid pseudo-outcome $Z_\lambda$ on $X$, as in the DR-learner \citep{kennedy2022drlearner}. It is motivated by the conditional validity $\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda\mid X)=\tau(X)$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:pseudo-valid}, which makes $\tau$ the population regression of $Z_\lambda$ on $X$. The R risk is the R-learner loss \citep{nie2020rlearner} with the known trial propensity $e$, divided by $e(X)\{1-e(X)\}$, the conditional variance of $A$ given $X$. Under the unrestricted function class, the minimizer of $\mathcal L_R(\zeta;\lambda)$ is again $\tau$, and over $\mathcal Z$ it is the $L_2(P_R^X)$ projection of $\tau$; Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target} states both, so no separate lemma is needed. Both risks are computable from the RCT alone. To use the large OBS covariate sample, the empirical versions below add the same transported correction as the ATE estimator in Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-estimator}, now applied to the squared distance $G_\zeta$.

\begin{definition}[Doubly Robust Fusion and R-Fusion losses]
\label{def:fusion-losses}
For $S\in\{R,O\}$, let $\mathbb P_S^{\mathrm{tune}}$ denote the empirical average over the tuning sample $D_S^{\mathrm{tune}}$. For $\lambda\in[0,1]$, $\omega\in\mathbb R$, a candidate transport weight $r$, and a candidate CATE function $\zeta\in\mathcal Z$, define the Doubly Robust Fusion (DRF) loss by
\begin{align}
\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)
&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\{Z_\lambda-\zeta(X)\}^2
+\omega\left[\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\{r(X)G_\zeta(X)\}-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\{G_\zeta(X)\}\right].
\label{eq:dr-fusion-loss}
\end{align}
Define the weight $\chi(A,X)\triangleq\{A-e(X)\}^2/[e(X)\{1-e(X)\}]$, which satisfies $\mathbb E_R\{\chi(A,X)\mid X\}=1$ under Assumption~\ref{ass:trial}, and the R-Fusion (RF) loss by
\begin{align}
\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)
&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\left[\frac{[\,Y-m_\lambda(X)-\{A-e(X)\}\zeta(X)\,]^2}{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}\right]
\notag\\
&\quad+\omega\left[\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\{r(X)G_\zeta(X)\}-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\{\chi(A,X)G_\zeta(X)\}\right].
\label{eq:rf-fusion-loss}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

The first term of each loss is the empirical version of the corresponding risk in Definition~\ref{def:rct-risks}, and the bracket is the OBS correction. Both losses also depend on the fitted objects $\widehat\mu_R$ and $\widehat\mu_O$, through $Z_\lambda$ and $m_\lambda$, and on $\widehat g$, through $G_\zeta$; these objects are fixed before the tuning samples are used, so the notation displays only the tuning coefficients, and later sections write $\widehat L_j(\zeta;\lambda,\omega)$ for $j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}$ with $r$ suppressed as well.  Any fixed function $m$ may replace $m_\lambda$; we then write $\mathcal L_R(\zeta;m)$, $Q_R(m)$, and $\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;m,\omega,r)$, and Section~\ref{sec:cate-orthogonality} uses this form to differentiate in $m$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact targets of the two losses]
\label{prop:cate-target}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:trial} and~\ref{ass:honest-separation} hold, and either Assumption~\ref{ass:common-marginal} holds with $r_0=1$ or Assumption~\ref{ass:exact-transport} holds with $r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$. Fix $\lambda\in[0,1]$, $\omega\in\mathbb R$, $\zeta\in\mathcal Z$, and suppose $Z_\lambda\in L_2(P_R)$, $\tau\in L_2(P_R^X)$, $Y-m_\lambda(X)\in L_2(P_R)$, $G_\zeta\in L_1(P_R)$, and $r_0G_\zeta\in L_1(P_O)$. Define the noise floors
\begin{align*}
Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\tau(X)\}^2,
&
Q_R(\lambda)&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\left[\frac{[\,Y-m_\lambda(X)-\{A-e(X)\}\tau(X)\,]^2}{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}\right].
\end{align*}
Then, with $r=r_0$,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}&=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda)=Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)+\mathcal R(\zeta),
\notag\\
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}&=\mathcal L_R(\zeta;\lambda)=Q_R(\lambda)+\mathcal R(\zeta).
\label{eq:cate-target}
\end{align}
Consequently, whenever the minimum is attained, the population minimizers of either expected loss over $\mathcal Z$ are the $L_2(P_R^X)$ projections of $\tau$ onto $\mathcal Z$, for every $\omega$. The same identities hold with any fixed $m$ in place of $m_\lambda$.
\end{proposition}
Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target} shows that both fusion losses target the CATE: minimizing over $\zeta$ is unaffected by the noise floors $Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)$ and $Q_R(\lambda)$, which do not depend on $\zeta$.  Also, the expected loss is flat in $\omega$ for the same reason as in Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-variance}: under exact transport the OBS correction has mean zero. 
\subsection{Orthogonality of learning equations and nuisance sensitivity}
\label{sec:cate-orthogonality}


Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target} establishes the population targets. We now ask how each fitted object enters the first-order condition that learns $\zeta$, that is, whether the learning equations are orthogonal to the nuisances in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:orthogonality}.

Throughout this subsection $\lambda$ and $\omega$ are fixed. The nuisances are the fitted regressions $\widehat\mu_R$ and $\widehat\mu_O$ of Section~\ref{sec:rct-obs-setting}, which enter the losses through $\widehat\mu_\lambda$, $m_\lambda$, and $\widehat g=\widehat\mu_{O,1}-\widehat\mu_{O,0}$, and the candidate ratio $r$.  Derivatives are the Gateaux derivatives $D_\eta F(\eta)[h]$ of Definition~\ref{def:gateaux}.

\begin{definition}[Population learning equations]
\label{def:learning-equations}
For $j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}$, $\lambda\in[0,1]$, $\omega\in\mathbb R$, a candidate ratio $r$, $\zeta\in\mathcal Z$, and a direction $v\in\mathcal Z$, define the learning equation of the expected loss $\mathbb E\{\widehat L_j(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)\}$ of Definition~\ref{def:fusion-losses}, as in Definition~\ref{def:orthogonality}, by
\begin{align}
\Psi_j(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)[v]&\triangleq-\frac12D_\zeta\mathbb E\{\widehat L_j(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)\}[v].
\label{eq:learning-equation}
\end{align}
Explicitly,
\begin{align}
\Psi_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)[v]
&=\mathbb E_R[v(X)\{Z_\lambda-\zeta(X)\}]
\nonumber\\
&\quad+\omega\left[\mathbb E_O\{r(X)v(X)[\widehat g(X)-\zeta(X)]\}-\mathbb E_R\{v(X)[\widehat g(X)-\zeta(X)]\}\right],
\label{eq:drf-learning-equation}\\
\Psi_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)[v]
&=\mathbb E_R\left[v(X)\frac{A-e(X)}{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}\{Y-m_\lambda(X)-[A-e(X)]\zeta(X)\}\right]
\nonumber\\
&\quad+\omega\left[\mathbb E_O\{r(X)v(X)[\widehat g(X)-\zeta(X)]\}-\mathbb E_R\{\chi(A,X)v(X)[\widehat g(X)-\zeta(X)]\}\right].
\label{eq:rf-learning-equation}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

 The expected loss is quadratic in $\zeta$ and, at $r=r_0$, convex by Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target}, so Definition~\ref{def:orthogonality} applies: the population minimizer over $\mathcal Z$ solves $\Psi_j(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)[v]=0$ for every $v\in\mathcal Z$, and the tuning-sample optimization solves the empirical version of the same equation. The factor $-\tfrac12$ removes the constant produced by differentiating a square, so that $\Psi_j$ is the cross-moment of the direction $v$ with the residual of the loss, plus the corresponding OBS correction. A nuisance error moves the fitted function only through its effect on $\Psi_j$, which is what the next proposition measures.

At $r=r_0$, the OBS bracket of each loss has expectation zero for every $\zeta$, which is the last step in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target}, so it contributes nothing to the population learning equations:
\begin{align*}
\Psi_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)[v]
&=\mathbb E_R[v(X)\{Z_\lambda-\zeta(X)\}],
\\
\Psi_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)[v]
&=\mathbb E_R\left[v(X)\frac{A-e(X)}{e(X)\{1-e(X)\}}\{Y-m_\lambda(X)-[A-e(X)]\zeta(X)\}\right].
\end{align*}
Exact transport thus removes the OBS correction from the population learning equations, while the correction remains in the empirical optimization problem.

\begin{proposition}[Directional derivatives of the learning equations]
\label{prop:cate-orthogonality-audit}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:trial} and~\ref{ass:honest-separation} hold, either Assumption~\ref{ass:common-marginal} or Assumption~\ref{ass:exact-transport} holds, and differentiation and expectation can be interchanged along the displayed paths. Fix $j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}$ and $v\in\mathcal Z$. For a pair of directions $h=(h_0,h_1)$, let $D_{\widehat\mu_R}\Psi_j[h,v]$ and $D_{\widehat\mu_O}\Psi_j[h,v]$ denote the derivatives of $\Psi_j(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)[v]$ along $\widehat\mu_R+\varepsilon h$ and along $\widehat\mu_O+\varepsilon h$, and for a direction $h_r$ let $D_r\Psi_j[h_r,v]$ denote the derivative along $r+\varepsilon h_r$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (RCT regression.) $D_{\widehat\mu_R}\Psi_j[h,v]=0$.
\item (OBS regression.) With $u\triangleq h_1-h_0$ the induced direction of $\widehat g$,
\begin{align}
D_{\widehat\mu_O}\Psi_j[h,v]
&=\omega\mathbb E_O[\{r(X)-r_0(X)\}u(X)v(X)],
\label{eq:cate-g-score-derivative}
\end{align}
which vanishes when $r=r_0$ or $\omega=0$, but not for an approximate ratio.
\item (Density ratio.)
\begin{align}
D_r\Psi_j[h_r,v]
&=\omega\mathbb E_O[h_r(X)v(X)\{\widehat g(X)-\zeta(X)\}].
\label{eq:cate-ratio-score-derivative}
\end{align}
The directions that preserve the normalization $\mathbb E_O(r)=1$ are those with $\mathbb E_O(h_r)=0$. If $\omega\neq0$ and $v(\widehat g-\zeta)\in L_2(P_O^X)$, then $D_r\Psi_j[h_r,v]=0$ for every such $h_r\in L_2(P_O^X)$ if and only if $v(X)\{\widehat g(X)-\zeta(X)\}$ is $P_O^X$-almost surely constant.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Item 1 is the classical orthogonality of the AIPW score and of the R loss to their RCT outcome nuisances under a known propensity \citep{chernozhukov2018dml,kennedy2022drlearner,nie2020rlearner}. Item 2 says that the OBS regression is a first-order nuisance only through the transported correction: its effect through $\widehat\mu_\lambda$ and $m_\lambda$ vanishes for the same reason as in item 1, and its effect through $\widehat g$ vanishes exactly when the ratio is exact. Item 3 says that the learning equations are not orthogonal to the density ratio: a first-order error in $\widehat r$ moves the learning equation by Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-ratio-score-derivative}, and calibrating $\widehat r$ to finitely many moments, as in Definition~\ref{def:classifier-calibrated-balance}, constrains $h_r$ only on a finite-dimensional subspace and does not change this conclusion.


\subsection{Fitting the learners and choosing the fusion coefficients}
\label{sec:cate-risk-selection}

The DRF and RF learners have three inputs that the data must supply: the fitted CATE function for given coefficients, the coefficients $(\lambda,\omega)$, and the ratio $r$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:cate-fusion-selection} states the procedure; the rest of this subsection defines its objects and analyzes its output, treating the three inputs in turn.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Honest fitting and selection of the CATE fusion learner}
\label{alg:cate-fusion-selection}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{Nuisance, tuning, and evaluation samples; the class $\mathcal Z$; a prespecified finite grid $\mathcal G_j\subseteq[0,1]^2$ containing $(0,0)$; a density-ratio estimator.}
Fit $\widehat\mu_R$, $\widehat\mu_O$, $\widehat g$, and $r$ on the nuisance samples.\;
For every $(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j$, fit $\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega,r)$ on the tuning samples by minimizing $\widehat L_j(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)$ of Definition~\ref{def:fusion-losses} over $\zeta\in\mathcal Z$.\;
For every $(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j$, compute on the evaluation samples, with $G\triangleq\{\widehat g(X)-\widehat\zeta_j(X;\lambda,\omega,r)\}^2$, the validation score $\widehat C_j(\lambda,\omega)\triangleq\mathbb P_{R,n}[\{Z_0-\widehat\zeta_j(X;\lambda,\omega,r)\}^2-\omega G]+\mathbb P_{O,N}\{\omega r(X)G\}$.\;
Select $(\widehat\lambda_j,\widehat\omega_j)$ by minimizing $\widehat C_j$ over $\mathcal G_j$, with deterministic tie-breaking.\;
\KwOut{$(\widehat\lambda_j,\widehat\omega_j)$ and $\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;\widehat\lambda_j,\widehat\omega_j,r)$.}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{definition}[Fitted fusion learner]
\label{def:fitted-fusion-learner}
Fix $j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}$, a prespecified compact set $\Omega\subseteq[0,1]$ containing $0$, coefficients $(\lambda,\omega)\in[0,1]\times\Omega$, and a candidate ratio $r$. The fitted fusion learner is
\begin{align}
\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega,r)
&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}\widehat L_j(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r),
\label{eq:fitted-fusion-learner}
\end{align}
with ties broken by a prespecified deterministic rule, and its CATE risk is $\mathcal R\{\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega,r)\}$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-ideal-risk}, evaluated conditionally on all fitted objects.
\end{definition}
Two abbreviations are used below: $\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)$ is the learner fitted with the ratio $r$ in use, and $\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;0,0)$ is the RCT-only learner, which uses neither $\omega$ nor $r$.

Step 2 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:cate-fusion-selection} is a convex least-squares problem, because the losses of Definition~\ref{def:fusion-losses} are quadratic in $\zeta$ with a nonnegative second-order part; the expansion is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:fusion-convexity}.

In DRF, $\lambda$ changes $Z_\lambda$; in RF, it changes $m_\lambda$; in both, $\omega$ scales the OBS correction and $r$ weights it. The fitted function therefore depends on $(\lambda,\omega,r)$, even though by Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target} the expected loss at a fixed $\zeta$ does not depend on $\omega$ at $r=r_0$.
The coefficients that minimize the CATE risk of the fitted function cannot be computed, because $\tau$ is unknown, so the coefficients are chosen by comparing the fitted candidates on the evaluation samples, as in steps 3 and 4 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:cate-fusion-selection}.

\begin{definition}[Validation score on a finite grid]
\label{def:cate-finite-grid-validation}
Let $\mathcal G_j\subseteq[0,1]\times\Omega$ be a prespecified finite grid containing $(0,0)$, and let $\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)$, $(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j$, be the candidates fitted in step 2 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:cate-fusion-selection} with the ratio $r$. For each grid pair, define on the evaluation samples $G_{j,\lambda,\omega}(X)\triangleq\{\widehat g(X)-\widehat\zeta_j(X;\lambda,\omega)\}^2$ and the validation score of step 3,
\begin{align}
\widehat C_j(\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq\mathbb P_{R,n}\left[\{Z_0-\widehat\zeta_j(X;\lambda,\omega)\}^2-\omega G_{j,\lambda,\omega}(X)\right]+\mathbb P_{O,N}\left\{\omega r(X)G_{j,\lambda,\omega}(X)\right\}.
\label{eq:common-reference-validation}
\end{align}
Step 4 selects $(\widehat\lambda_j,\widehat\omega_j)\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j}\widehat C_j(\lambda,\omega)$, with deterministic tie-breaking.
\end{definition}

The score uses the same pseudo-outcome $Z_0$ for every candidate, so that candidates differ only through their fitted functions, and it adds the transported correction $\omega\{\mathbb P_{O,N}(rG_{j,\lambda,\omega})-\mathbb P_{R,n}(G_{j,\lambda,\omega})\}$, whose conditional mean is zero when $r=r_0$, exactly as the control variate in Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-estimator}.

\begin{theorem}[Honest validation of the fusion candidates]
\label{thm:cate-finite-validation}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:honest-separation} and~\ref{ass:trial} hold, and either Assumption~\ref{ass:common-marginal} holds with $r_0=1$ or Assumption~\ref{ass:exact-transport} holds with $r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$. Let $r$ and the candidates be as in Definition~\ref{def:cate-finite-grid-validation}, suppose $|Z_0|$, $|\widehat g|$, and $|\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)|$ are bounded by $B$ and $0\leq r\leq\bar r$, let $M\triangleq|\mathcal G_j|$, and for $\alpha\in(0,1)$ define
\begin{align}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\alpha)
&\triangleq2B^2\sqrt{2\log(4M/\alpha)}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt n}+\frac{\bar r}{\sqrt N}\right).
\label{eq:eval-radius}
\end{align}
Conditional on all fitted objects, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (What the score measures.) For every $(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j$,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E\{\widehat C_j(\lambda,\omega)\}
&=Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(0)+\mathcal R\{\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)\}+\omega\,\mathbb E_O[\{r(X)-r_0(X)\}G_{j,\lambda,\omega}(X)].
\label{eq:common-reference-identity}
\end{align}
The first term is the same for every candidate, and the last term, the transport error of $r$, is zero when $r=r_0$ and for the RCT-only candidate.
\item (How accurate the score is.) With probability at least $1-\alpha$ over the evaluation samples, $|\widehat C_j(\lambda,\omega)-\mathbb E\{\widehat C_j(\lambda,\omega)\}|\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\alpha)$ for every $(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j$.
\item (What the selection achieves.) On the same event,
\begin{align}
\mathcal R\{\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;\widehat\lambda_j,\widehat\omega_j)\}
&\leq\min_{(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j}\mathcal R\{\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)\}+2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\alpha)\nonumber\\
&\quad+2\max_{(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G_j}\left|\omega\,\mathbb E_O[\{r(X)-r_0(X)\}G_{j,\lambda,\omega}(X)]\right|,
\label{eq:cate-selector-inequality}
\end{align}
and the minimum is at most $\mathcal R\{\widehat\zeta_j(\cdot;0,0)\}$ because $(0,0)\in\mathcal G_j$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
In words, the score orders the candidates by their CATE risk up to a constant that is common to all of them; the order is correct up to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\alpha)$, which decreases like $n^{-1/2}$ and $N^{-1/2}$ and grows only like $\sqrt{\log M}$ in the number of candidates; so the selected learner is within $2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eval}}(\alpha)$ of the best candidate, and in particular of the RCT-only learner. An estimated ratio from Section~\ref{sec:ratio-estimation} adds twice its largest transport error on the functions $G_{j,\lambda,\omega}$; under Assumption~\ref{ass:common-marginal}, $r_0=1$ and no ratio is estimated.

\subsection{Linear sieves: computation and when fusion helps}
\label{sec:cate-sieve}


Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-finite-validation} holds for any class $\mathcal Z$ but does not say when the OBS data help. This subsection answers that question for linear sieves, where every candidate of Algorithm~\ref{alg:cate-fusion-selection} has a closed form and the risk of a fixed candidate has the same quadratic structure in $(\lambda,\omega)$ as the ATE variance in Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-variance}.

\begin{definition}[Linear-sieve fusion learner]
\label{def:linear-sieve-fusion-learner}
Let $b:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^p$ be a feature map fixed before the tuning samples are used, for instance splines, random features, or a neural representation trained on the nuisance samples and then frozen, and let $\mathcal Z_b\triangleq\{b(\cdot)^\top\beta:\beta\in\mathbb R^p\}$. For $\rho\geq0$ and $(j,\lambda,\omega,r)$ as in Definition~\ref{def:fitted-fusion-learner}, the sieve fusion learner is $\widehat\zeta_j(x;\lambda,\omega,r)\triangleq b(x)^\top\widehat\beta_j(\lambda,\omega)$ with
\begin{align}
\widehat\beta_j(\lambda,\omega)
&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\beta\in\mathbb R^p}\left\{\widehat L_j(b^\top\beta;\lambda,\omega,r)+\rho\|\beta\|_2^2\right\},
\label{eq:sieve-learner}
\end{align}
with ties broken by a prespecified deterministic rule; $\rho=0$ recovers Definition~\ref{def:fitted-fusion-learner} on $\mathcal Z_b$.
\end{definition}
End-to-end neural fitting is not a sieve: it is covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-finite-validation} as a candidate, but not by this subsection.

Both losses are weighted least-squares problems in $\beta$. Write
\begin{align}
w_{\mathrm{DRF}}&\triangleq1,
&
\widetilde Z_{\mathrm{DRF},\lambda}&\triangleq Z_\lambda,
&
w_{\mathrm{RF}}&\triangleq\chi(A,X),
&
\widetilde Z_{\mathrm{RF},\lambda}&\triangleq\frac{Y-m_\lambda(X)}{A-e(X)},
\label{eq:sieve-weights}
\end{align}
so that $\widetilde Z_{j,\lambda}=\widetilde Z_{j,0}+\lambda\widetilde\Delta_j$ with $\widetilde\Delta_{\mathrm{DRF}}\triangleq\Delta$ and $\widetilde\Delta_{\mathrm{RF}}\triangleq-\{m_O(X)-m_R(X)\}/\{A-e(X)\}$.

\begin{proposition}[Weighted least squares, closed form, and profiling]
\label{prop:sieve-closed-form}
Fix $j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}$, $(\lambda,\omega)\in[0,1]\times\Omega$, a ratio $r\geq0$, and $\rho\geq0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Weighted least-squares form.) The loss of Definition~\ref{def:fusion-losses} equals
\begin{align}
\widehat L_j(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)
&=\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\left[w_j\{\widetilde Z_{j,\lambda}-\zeta(X)\}^2\right]
+\omega\left[\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\{r(X)G_\zeta(X)\}-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\{w_jG_\zeta(X)\}\right],
\label{eq:sieve-wls-form}
\end{align}
and under Assumption~\ref{ass:trial}, $\mathbb E_R(w_j\mid X)=1$, $\mathbb E_R(w_j\widetilde Z_{j,\lambda}\mid X)=\tau(X)$, and $\mathbb E_R(w_j\widetilde\Delta_j\mid X)=0$.
\item (Closed form.) With $\widehat H_j(\omega)\triangleq(1-\omega)\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}(w_jbb^\top)+\omega\,\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}(r\,bb^\top)$, every minimizer in Equation~\eqref{eq:sieve-learner} solves
\begin{align}
\{\widehat H_j(\omega)+\rho I\}\beta
&=\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\left[w_jb\{\widetilde Z_{j,\lambda}-\omega\widehat g(X)\}\right]+\omega\,\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\{r(X)\,b\,\widehat g(X)\},
\label{eq:sieve-normal-equation}
\end{align}
and $\widehat H_j(\omega)$ is positive semidefinite, so the minimizer is unique whenever $\widehat H_j(\omega)+\rho I$ is invertible.
\item (Profiling in $\lambda$.) The matrix $\widehat H_j(\omega)$ does not depend on $\lambda$ and the right-hand side of Equation~\eqref{eq:sieve-normal-equation} is affine in $\lambda$, so when $\widehat H_j(\omega)+\rho I$ is invertible,
\begin{align}
\widehat\beta_j(\lambda,\omega)
&=\widehat\beta_j(0,\omega)+\lambda\{\widehat H_j(\omega)+\rho I\}^{-1}\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}(w_jb\,\widetilde\Delta_j).
\label{eq:sieve-lambda-path}
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
Item 3 makes the grid of Definition~\ref{def:cate-finite-grid-validation} cheap: for each $\omega$ in the grid, all $\lambda$ candidates cost one linear solve.

The risk of a fixed candidate separates into approximation and estimation. Let $\Gamma_R\triangleq\mathbb E_R(bb^\top)\succ0$, let $\beta_p\triangleq\Gamma_R^{-1}\mathbb E_R\{b\,\tau(X)\}$ and $\zeta_p\triangleq b^\top\beta_p$ be the $L_2(P_R^X)$ projection of $\tau$ onto $\mathcal Z_b$, and let $a_p^2\triangleq\mathcal R(\zeta_p)$. For $r=r_0$ define the per-observation contributions
\begin{align}
\psi_{R,j}(\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq w_jb\left\{\widetilde Z_{j,\lambda}-\omega\widehat g(X)-(1-\omega)\zeta_p(X)\right\},
&
\psi_O(\omega)
&\triangleq\omega\,r_0(X)\,b\left\{\widehat g(X)-\zeta_p(X)\right\},
\label{eq:sieve-contributions}
\end{align}
the sieve versions of $\psi_R$ and $\psi_O$ in Algorithm~\ref{alg:honest-ate}, and the first-order risk
\begin{align}
\mathcal J_j(\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq\frac{\operatorname{tr}[\Gamma_R^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_R\{\psi_{R,j}(\lambda,\omega)\}]}{n_R^{\mathrm{tune}}}
+\frac{\operatorname{tr}[\Gamma_R^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_O\{\psi_O(\omega)\}]}{n_O^{\mathrm{tune}}}.
\label{eq:sieve-first-order-risk}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Risk of a sieve candidate]
\label{thm:sieve-risk}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:honest-separation} and~\ref{ass:trial} hold, either Assumption~\ref{ass:common-marginal} holds with $r_0=1$ or Assumption~\ref{ass:exact-transport} holds with $r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X$, the candidate uses $r=r_0$, and the displayed moments are finite. Fix $(j,\lambda,\omega,\rho)$ and condition on the fitted objects and on $b$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Exact decomposition.) For every $\beta\in\mathbb R^p$, $\mathcal R(b^\top\beta)=a_p^2+(\beta-\beta_p)^\top\Gamma_R(\beta-\beta_p)$, and whenever $\widehat H_j(\omega)+\rho I$ is invertible,
\begin{align}
\widehat\beta_j(\lambda,\omega)-\beta_p
&=\{\widehat H_j(\omega)+\rho I\}^{-1}\left\{\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\psi_{R,j}(\lambda,\omega)+\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\psi_O(\omega)-\rho\beta_p\right\}.
\label{eq:sieve-coefficient-identity}
\end{align}
\item (Exact target and variance.) $\mathbb E\{\widehat H_j(\omega)\}=\Gamma_R$, $\mathbb E\{\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\psi_{R,j}(\lambda,\omega)+\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\psi_O(\omega)\}=0$, and
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Var}\{\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\psi_{R,j}(\lambda,\omega)+\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\psi_O(\omega)\}
&=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_R\{\psi_{R,j}(\lambda,\omega)\}}{n_R^{\mathrm{tune}}}+\frac{\operatorname{Var}_O\{\psi_O(\omega)\}}{n_O^{\mathrm{tune}}}.
\label{eq:sieve-score-variance}
\end{align}
\item (Leading risk.) If the linearization remainder $\widehat\beta_j(\lambda,\omega)-\beta_p-\Gamma_R^{-1}\{\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\psi_{R,j}(\lambda,\omega)+\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\psi_O(\omega)\}$ has $\Gamma_R$-weighted mean square of order $o(1/n_R^{\mathrm{tune}}+1/n_O^{\mathrm{tune}})$, then
\begin{align}
\mathbb E\{\mathcal R(\widehat\zeta_j)\}
&=a_p^2+\mathcal J_j(\lambda,\omega)+o(1/n_R^{\mathrm{tune}}+1/n_O^{\mathrm{tune}}).
\label{eq:sieve-leading-risk}
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Item 2 is the sieve version of Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-variance}: the fitted coefficients are centered on the projection for every fixed $(\lambda,\omega)$, and the two tuning samples contribute independent variances. Item 3 keeps the linearization remainder as an explicit condition; it is the usual fixed-$p$ expansion and is not proved here.

\begin{theoremcorollary}[Oracle sieve coefficients]
\label{cor:sieve-oracle}
Under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:sieve-risk}, define
\begin{align}
A_p&\triangleq\operatorname{tr}\{\Gamma_R^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_R(w_jb\,\widetilde\Delta_j)\},
\notag\\
B_p&\triangleq\operatorname{tr}[\Gamma_R^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_R\{w_jb(\widehat g-\zeta_p)\}]
+\frac{n_R^{\mathrm{tune}}}{n_O^{\mathrm{tune}}}\operatorname{tr}[\Gamma_R^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_O\{r_0b(\widehat g-\zeta_p)\}],
\notag\\
C_p&\triangleq\operatorname{tr}[\Gamma_R^{-1}\operatorname{Cov}_R\{w_jb(\widetilde Z_{j,0}-\zeta_p),\,w_jb\,\widetilde\Delta_j\}],
\notag\\
D_p&\triangleq\operatorname{tr}[\Gamma_R^{-1}\operatorname{Cov}_R\{w_jb(\widetilde Z_{j,0}-\zeta_p),\,w_jb(\widehat g-\zeta_p)\}].
\label{eq:sieve-moments}
\end{align}
For DRF,
\begin{align}
n_R^{\mathrm{tune}}\{\mathcal J_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\lambda,\omega)-\mathcal J_{\mathrm{DRF}}(0,0)\}
&=2C_p\lambda-2D_p\omega+A_p\lambda^2+B_p\omega^2,
\label{eq:sieve-quadratic}
\end{align}
so if $A_p,B_p>0$ the first-order risk over $\mathbb R^2$ is minimized at $\lambda_p^\star=-C_p/A_p$ and $\omega_p^\star=D_p/B_p$, the minimizer over $[0,1]\times\Omega$ is its coordinatewise projection, and the first-order improvement over the RCT-only candidate is
\begin{align}
\mathcal J_{\mathrm{DRF}}(0,0)-\mathcal J_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\lambda_p^\star,\omega_p^\star)
&=\frac{1}{n_R^{\mathrm{tune}}}\left(\frac{C_p^2}{A_p}+\frac{D_p^2}{B_p}\right)\geq0.
\label{eq:sieve-oracle-gain}
\end{align}
For RF, Equation~\eqref{eq:sieve-quadratic} gains the cross term $-2\lambda\omega\operatorname{tr}[\Gamma_R^{-1}\operatorname{Cov}_R\{\chi b\,\widetilde\Delta_{\mathrm{RF}},\chi b(\widehat g-\zeta_p)\}]$, which vanishes when $e\equiv1/2$, and the minimizer solves the corresponding two-by-two linear system.
\end{theoremcorollary}
Equations~\eqref{eq:sieve-quadratic} and~\eqref{eq:sieve-oracle-gain} are the sieve versions of Theorem~\ref{thm:joint-oracle} and Corollary~\ref{cor:joint-oracle-variance}: the four ATE moments are replaced by basis-weighted covariances whitened by $\Gamma_R^{-1}$, $\lambda$ trades the noise of the pseudo-outcome against its correlation with $\Delta$, and $\omega$ trades the OBS variance, which enters through $n_R^{\mathrm{tune}}/n_O^{\mathrm{tune}}$, against the correlation of the RCT residual with $\widehat g$. A larger OBS tuning sample lowers $B_p$ and raises $\omega_p^\star$. The honest validation of Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-finite-validation} finds these coefficients on a grid without estimating $A_p$ to $D_p$; the corollary explains what it finds.

Three remarks close the subsection. First, with $K$-dimensional splines for an additive $\tau$ of smoothness $s$, $a_K^2=O(K^{-2s})$ while the traces in Equation~\eqref{eq:sieve-first-order-risk} grow in proportion to $K$, so the risk behaves like $K^{-2s}$ plus $K$ times a weighted sum of $1/n_R^{\mathrm{tune}}$ and $1/n_O^{\mathrm{tune}}$ whose weights depend on the learner and on $(\lambda,\omega)$; the two sample sizes do not collapse into one effective sample size. Second, with an estimated ratio $\widehat r$ in place of $r_0$, the mean of the score in item 2 of Theorem~\ref{thm:sieve-risk} becomes $\omega\,\mathbb E_O[\{\widehat r(X)-r_0(X)\}b\{\widehat g(X)-\zeta_p(X)\}]$ and $\mathbb E\{\widehat H_j(\omega)\}$ shifts by $\omega\,\mathbb E_O[\{\widehat r(X)-r_0(X)\}bb^\top]$, the sieve form of item 3 of Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-orthogonality-audit}. Third, kernel ridge regression is a sieve with a data-dependent basis and $p$ equal to the sample size: items 1 and 2 of Theorem~\ref{thm:sieve-risk} apply verbatim, but item 3 requires effective-dimension arguments in place of a fixed $p$ and is not pursued here.

\subsection{Implementation boundaries and open theory}
\label{sec:cate-scope}

 Estimated-ratio guarantees require uniform score, Hessian, and
validation-loss control. Cross-fitting can recover data efficiency, but
overlapping outer training sets do not justify a naive sum of fold variances.
Neither target preservation nor candidate inclusion gives universal
finite-sample improvement for estimated $(\lambda,\omega)$.
