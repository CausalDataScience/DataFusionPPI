\section{Prediction-Powered Fusion Learning for CATE}
\label{sec:cate}

\subsection{Prediction-powered loss, coefficient roles, and the ATE relation}
\label{sec:cate-loss}

Throughout this subsection, nuisances and ratios are fit on block 1. Candidate
fitting and honest validation use prespecified disjoint portions of block 2.
Every selected object is then held fixed before final evaluation on the untouched
block 0. Population expectations and moments use the three-block convention
from Section~\ref{sec:rct-obs-setting}.

For a fixed candidate CATE function \(t\), define the density-ratio-weighted prediction-powered squared loss
\begin{align}
\widehat L_{\mathrm{DR}}(t;\lambda,\omega)
&=
\mathbb P_{R,n}\{Z_\lambda-t(X)\}^2
\\
&\quad+
\omega\left[
\mathbb P_{O,N}\{r(X)(g(X)-t(X))^2\}
-
\mathbb P_{R,n}\{(g(X)-t(X))^2\}
\right].
\label{eq:dr-fusion-loss}
\end{align}
For every fixed candidate considered below, assume
\(Z_\lambda,t\in L^2(P_R)\), and let
\(G_t(X)=\{g(X)-t(X)\}^2\) satisfy
\(G_t\in L^1(P_R)\) and \(rG_t\in L^1(P_O)\).

\begin{proposition}[Exact target of the prediction-powered CATE loss]
\label{prop:cate-target-active}
\label{prop:cate-target}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:trial} and \ref{ass:exact-transport}, together with the three-block convention of Section~\ref{sec:rct-obs-setting},
\begin{align}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{DR}}(t;\lambda,\omega)\}
&=
Q(\lambda)+\mathbb E_R\{t(X)-\tau(X)\}^2,
\\
Q(\lambda)
&\triangleq
\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\tau(X)\}^2.
\label{eq:cate-target}
\end{align}
The common-marginal case sets \(r=1\). In both cases, the expected loss is flat in \(\omega\).
\end{proposition}

Because \(Z_\lambda=Z_0+\lambda\Delta\) and
\(\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)=0\),
\begin{align*}
Q(\lambda)
&=Q(0)+2C\lambda+A\lambda^2.
\end{align*}
When \(A>0\), define the integrated pseudo-outcome-noise oracle
\begin{align}
\lambda_Q^\star
&=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}Q(\lambda)
=-C/A.
\label{eq:lambda-q-oracle}
\end{align}
Write \(\lambda_{\mathrm{ATE}}^\star:=\lambda^\star\) and \(\omega_{\mathrm{ATE}}^\star:=\omega^\star\) for the two coordinates of the population-orthogonal ATE variance oracle in Equation~\eqref{eq:axis-oracle}. Then \(\lambda_Q^\star=\lambda_{\mathrm{ATE}}^\star\). When \(A=0\), \(\Delta=0\) almost surely and \(Q(\lambda)\) is flat in \(\lambda\). The ATE variance coefficient \(\omega_{\mathrm{ATE}}^\star=D/B\) is a possible training default, but Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-target} does not identify it as a CATE-risk optimizer.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed measurable and pointwise noise coefficients]
\label{prop:cate-pointwise-lambda-active}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:trial} and the three-block convention of Section~\ref{sec:rct-obs-setting}, let \(\lambda(\cdot)\) be a fixed measurable function such that \(Z_0+\lambda(X)\Delta\) is integrable. Then
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_R\{Z_0+\lambda(X)\Delta\mid X\}
&=\tau(X).
\end{align*}
For the pointwise statement, choose measurable versions of the displayed conditional covariance and variance and assume they are finite. For \(P_R^X\)-almost every \(x\) on which \(\operatorname{Var}_R(\Delta\mid X=x)>0\), the conditional-noise minimizer is
\begin{align*}
\lambda_Q^\star(x)
&=-
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_R(Z_0,\Delta\mid X=x)}
{\operatorname{Var}_R(\Delta\mid X=x)}.
\end{align*}
On the zero-denominator set the conditional criterion is flat, and any finite measurable value may be assigned. The ratio must be measurable on the positive-denominator set, and the resulting pseudo-outcome must remain integrable.
\end{proposition}

The global scalar \(\lambda_Q^\star\), the pointwise function \(\lambda_Q^\star(x)\), and the coefficient chosen by a restricted or penalized learner are different oracle objects.

The sampling variance of a fixed candidate loss identifies a distinct coefficient. Let
\(U_{t,\lambda}=\{Z_\lambda-t(X)\}^2\).
\begin{proposition}[Fixed-loss variance oracle]
\label{prop:cate-loss-variance-active}
Under the three-block convention of Section~\ref{sec:rct-obs-setting}, fix \(t\), \(\lambda\), \(g\), and \(r\) using blocks 1 and 2, and evaluate the displayed loss on the independent block-0 RCT and OBS samples. Suppose \(U_{t,\lambda}\in L^2(P_R)\), \(G_t\in L^2(P_R)\), and \(rG_t\in L^2(P_O)\). If the denominator below is positive, the unrestricted variance-minimizing coefficient is
\begin{align}
\omega_{\mathrm{LV}}^\star(t;\lambda)
&=
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_R(U_{t,\lambda},G_t)}
{\operatorname{Var}_R(G_t)+(n/N)\operatorname{Var}_O(rG_t)}.
\label{eq:loss-variance-omega}
\end{align}
Under the common covariate marginal this denominator is \((1+n/N)\operatorname{Var}_R(G_t)\). If the denominator is zero, the empirical correction is constant and its variance criterion is flat in \(\omega\).
\end{proposition}
This oracle minimizes the variance of one fixed empirical loss. It does not minimize the final learned CATE risk. The square-integrability conditions are fourth-moment-type requirements on the underlying residuals.

\paragraph{Final learner-risk oracle.}
Let \(\mathfrak C\) be a prespecified finite candidate class. An index
\begin{align}
c
&=
(\lambda_c,\omega_c,p_c,\rho_c)\in\mathfrak C
\end{align}
specifies the two fusion coefficients, the sieve dimension, and the penalty level. All other components of the learning rule, including the allocation of blocks 1 and 2, nuisance algorithms, basis construction, and optimization convention, are also prespecified. For realized blocks 1 and 2, let \(\widehat t_c\) denote the resulting fixed fitted function and define its sample-specific target risk and its algorithm-induced expected risk by
\begin{align}
\mathcal R_{12}(c)
&=
\mathbb E_R
\left[
\{\widehat t_c(X)-\tau(X)\}^2
\right],
\label{eq:sample-cate-risk}
\\
\mathfrak R(c)
&=
\mathbb E_{12}
\{\mathcal R_{12}(c)\}.
\label{eq:algorithm-cate-risk}
\end{align}
Here \(\mathbb E_{12}\) averages over the joint law of blocks 1 and 2 and all
prespecified algorithmic randomness used before block 0.
When the minimum is attained, define the sample-specific final learner-risk oracle
\begin{align*}
c_{\mathrm{risk}}^\star(12)
&\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{c\in\mathfrak C}
\mathcal R_{12}(c).
\end{align*}
This oracle chooses among fitted learning algorithms. It is distinct from both \(\lambda_Q^\star\) and \(\omega_{\mathrm{LV}}^\star(t;\lambda)\).

To make the fallback comparison exact, let
\begin{align}
\mathfrak C_R
&=
\{(0,0,p,\rho):(p,\rho)\in\mathcal H_R\}
\subseteq
\mathfrak C
\label{eq:rct-candidate-class}
\end{align}
contain the identical RCT-only learners over the prespecified sieve and penalty grid \(\mathcal H_R\). Identical means that the RCT observations, folds, RCT nuisance fits, basis, penalty scaling, and optimization code agree with the corresponding fusion candidates; only the two OBS fusion channels are set to zero.

\begin{proposition}[Candidate-class oracle dominance]
\label{prop:cate-candidate-dominance}
For every realized pair of blocks 1 and 2 for which the displayed infima are finite,
\begin{align}
\inf_{c\in\mathfrak C}
\mathcal R_{12}(c)
&\leq
\inf_{c\in\mathfrak C_R}
\mathcal R_{12}(c).
\label{eq:sample-oracle-dominance}
\end{align}
Likewise,
\begin{align}
\inf_{c\in\mathfrak C}\mathfrak R(c)
&\leq
\inf_{c\in\mathfrak C_R}\mathfrak R(c).
\label{eq:expected-oracle-dominance}
\end{align}
If the infima are attained, they define the sample-specific and expected-training final CATE-risk oracles. Either inequality is strict only when at least one fusion candidate has risk strictly below the optimally tuned RCT-only class under the corresponding comparison.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-candidate-dominance} is an infeasible oracle statement because its risks use the unknown \(\tau\). It does not say that a data-adaptive selector dominates the RCT-only learner on every finite sample.

\paragraph{Common-reference honest validation.}
Hold the block-1 and block-2-fitting candidates fixed and construct one shared
RCT-valid pseudo-outcome \(Z_{\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}}\) on the untouched RCT
validation portion of \(D_R^2\) using a fixed reference coefficient. Let
\(\mathbb E_2\) denote expectation only over the disjoint RCT and OBS validation
portions of block 2, and let \(\mathbb P_{R,2}\) and \(\mathbb P_{O,2}\) denote
their empirical averages. For candidate \(c\), set
\begin{align}
G_c(X)
&=
\{g(X)-\widehat t_c(X)\}^2.
\end{align}
Let \(r^\dagger=1\) under the common covariate marginal, \(r^\dagger=r\) under exact covariate transport, and \(r^\dagger=\widehat r\) in the estimated-ratio regime. Define the shared-reference validation score
\begin{align}
\widehat{\mathcal R}^{\mathrm{ref}}_c
&=
\mathbb P_{R,2}
\left[
\{Z_{\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}}-\widehat t_c(X)\}^2
\right]
\nonumber\\
&\quad+
\mathbb P_{O,2}
\{r^\dagger(X)G_c(X)\}
-
\mathbb P_{R,2}G_c(X).
\label{eq:common-reference-validation}
\end{align}
All candidates must use the same reference pseudo-outcome, \(g\), ratio rule, and block-2 validation observations. Under the common marginal or exact \(r\), known RCT propensity, and the three-block convention,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_2
\{\widehat{\mathcal R}^{\mathrm{ref}}_c\}
&=
Q(\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}})
+
\mathcal R_{12}(c).
\label{eq:common-reference-identity}
\end{align}
Thus candidate comparisons remove the same additive reference noise. The OBS correction is not required for validity of this identity, but it can reduce the sampling variability of validation when the prediction is useful.

For the approximate regime, define the fixed-candidate reference bias
\begin{align}
b_{\mathrm{ref}}(X)
&=
\mathbb E_R
\{Z_{\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}}-\tau(X)
\mid X\},
\end{align}
the ratio and nuisance drifts
\begin{align}
\delta_{r,c}
&=
\mathbb E_O[\{r^\dagger(X)-r(X)\}G_c(X)],
\\
\delta_{\mathrm{nuis},c}
&=
2\mathbb E_R
\left[
b_{\mathrm{ref}}(X)
\{\tau(X)-\widehat t_c(X)\}
\right],
\end{align}
and their uniform magnitudes
\begin{align}
\Delta_r
&=
\sup_{c\in\mathfrak C}|\delta_{r,c}|,
&
\Delta_{\mathrm{nuis}}
&=
\sup_{c\in\mathfrak C}|\delta_{\mathrm{nuis},c}|.
\label{eq:validation-drift-bounds}
\end{align}
Known trial propensity and an honest reference nuisance give \(b_{\mathrm{ref}}=0\) for every outcome-regression fit. Exact transport gives \(\Delta_r=0\). Estimated ratios or estimated propensities must instead be controlled through the displayed drifts or a stronger problem-specific expansion. Adaptive reuse of validation observations violates the held-out product law and measurability used here; it is outside Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-selector-inequality} unless a separate selective-inference or stability theorem is supplied.

\begin{proposition}[Validation-selector oracle inequality]
\label{prop:cate-selector-inequality}
Let
\begin{align}
\widehat c
&\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{c\in\mathfrak C}
\widehat{\mathcal R}^{\mathrm{ref}}_c
\end{align}
and define the validation fluctuation
\begin{align}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{val}}
&=
\sup_{c\in\mathfrak C}
\left|
\widehat{\mathcal R}^{\mathrm{ref}}_c
-
\mathbb E_2
\{\widehat{\mathcal R}^{\mathrm{ref}}_c\}
\right|.
\end{align}
Then
\begin{align}
\mathcal R_{12}(\widehat c)
&\leq
\inf_{c\in\mathfrak C}
\mathcal R_{12}(c)
+2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{val}}
+2\Delta_r
+2\Delta_{\mathrm{nuis}}
\label{eq:cate-selector-inequality}
\end{align}
and therefore the same upper bound holds with the infimum over \(\mathfrak C\) replaced by the larger infimum over \(\mathfrak C_R\).
\end{proposition}

If \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{val}}+\Delta_r+\Delta_{\mathrm{nuis}}=o_p(1)\), Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-selector-inequality} gives asymptotic weak noninferiority relative to the identical optimally tuned RCT-only class. Passing from this fixed-block statement to expected-training risk additionally requires uniform integrability. Strict asymptotic improvement requires a positive oracle gap that dominates the validation and drift remainders. No part of this argument gives universal finite-sample or realized-sample dominance.

\paragraph{Why \(\lambda_Q^\star\) need not optimize sieve risk.}
Consider an unpenalized linear sieve fitted on RCT pseudo-outcomes with \(\omega=0\), basis \(b_p\), population Gram matrix \(G_p=\mathbb E_R\{b_p(X)b_p(X)^\top\}\), projection \(t_p\) of \(\tau\), and leverage \(h_p(X)=b_p(X)^\top G_p^{-1}b_p(X)\). Under the usual fixed-sieve least-squares expansion, the \(\lambda\)-dependent part of the leading integrated estimation variance is
\begin{align}
\frac1n
\mathbb E_R
\left[
h_p(X)\operatorname{Var}_R(Z_\lambda\mid X)
\right].
\label{eq:leverage-weighted-noise}
\end{align}
The oracle \(\lambda_Q^\star\) minimizes the same expression without \(h_p(X)\). The two coefficients coincide when the leverage is constant, or under another condition that makes the two weighted quadratics share a minimizer, but they need not coincide in general. Penalties, \(\omega\), and data-adaptive basis selection create further differences between the integrated-noise oracle and the final learner-risk oracle.

For \(\omega\neq1\), define \(\widetilde Z_{\lambda,\omega}=(Z_\lambda-\omega g)/(1-\omega)\).
\begin{proposition}[Exact square completion]
\label{prop:cate-square-completion-active}
For every \(\omega\neq1\),
\begin{align*}
\{Z_\lambda-t(X)\}^2
-\omega\{g(X)-t(X)\}^2
&=
(1-\omega)\{t(X)-\widetilde Z_{\lambda,\omega}\}^2
-\frac{\omega}{1-\omega}\{Z_\lambda-g(X)\}^2.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{align}
\widehat L_{\mathrm{DR}}(t;\lambda,\omega)
&=
(1-\omega)\mathbb P_{R,n}\{t(X)-\widetilde Z_{\lambda,\omega}\}^2
\\
&\quad+
\omega\mathbb P_{O,N}\{r(X)(g(X)-t(X))^2\}
+\left[-\frac{\omega}{1-\omega}
\mathbb P_{R,n}\{Z_\lambda-g(X)\}^2\right].
\label{eq:completed-loss}
\end{align}
The bracketed term is constant in \(t\).
\end{proposition}
For \(0\leq\omega<1\), the two \(t\)-dependent terms form a nonnegative mixture of squared losses. At \(\omega=1\), the transformed outcome is undefined and the original objective must be used. For \(0\leq\omega\leq1\), the original empirical quadratic is positive semidefinite in unrestricted fitted values and in the coefficients of a linear sieve because its Hessian is a nonnegative weighted sum of Gram matrices. Outside this interval, or for nonlinear parameterizations, convexity is not guaranteed. Under a penalty or parameter constraint, rescaling the completed loss changes the relative penalty scale, so the original quadratic and its stated penalty must define the estimator.

\subsection{Implementable F-Learner and prospective R-loss extension}
\label{sec:cate-implementable}

We use \emph{F-Learner} as a provisional working label only for CATE estimators that minimize Equation~\eqref{eq:dr-fusion-loss}, which is built from the RCT-valid AIPW pseudo-outcome \(Z_\lambda\). The label does not claim name novelty and does not denote a generic unification of doubly robust and R losses.

For a linear sieve \(t_\beta(x)=b(x)^\top\beta\), with \(b(x)\in\mathbb R^p\), add the penalty \(\rho\beta^\top P\beta\), where \(\rho\geq0\) and \(P=P^\top\succeq0\) belongs to \(\mathbb R^{p\times p}\). Define
\begin{align}
G_R&=\mathbb P_{R,n}\{b(X)b(X)^\top\},
&
G_{O,r}&=\mathbb P_{O,N}\{r(X)b(X)b(X)^\top\},
\\
h_\lambda&=\mathbb P_{R,n}\{b(X)Z_\lambda\},
&
d_g&=\mathbb P_{O,N}\{r(X)b(X)g(X)\}-\mathbb P_{R,n}\{b(X)g(X)\}.
\end{align}
Whenever the matrix is invertible, the penalized F-Learner solution is
\begin{align}
\widehat\beta_{\lambda,\omega}
&=
\left\{(1-\omega)G_R+\omega G_{O,r}+\rho P\right\}^{-1}
\left\{h_\lambda+\omega d_g\right\}.
\label{eq:linear-sieve}
\end{align}
Because \(h_\lambda\) is affine in \(\lambda\), a fixed-\(\omega\) candidate path can be computed efficiently. A frozen neural basis replaces \(b(x)\) by a representation learned without the candidate-training or validation observations and inherits Equation~\eqref{eq:linear-sieve} conditional on that representation. End-to-end neural fitting is nonconvex, and the present linear-sieve guarantees do not cover it.

\paragraph{Out-of-fold nuisance and candidate construction.}
As an alternative within the block-1 and block-2 fitting stages, partition the
RCT and OBS indices into prespecified folds. For each held-out fold \(k\), fit
\(\widehat\mu_R^{(-k)}\) and \(\widehat\mu_O^{(-k)}\) without that fold, use the same
\begin{align*}
g^{(-k)}(x)
&=\widehat\mu_{O,1}^{(-k)}(x)-\widehat\mu_{O,0}^{(-k)}(x)
\end{align*}
on held-out RCT and OBS covariates, and construct \(Z_{0,i}^{(-k)}\), \(Z_{1,i}^{(-k)}\), and \(Z_{\lambda,i}^{(-k)}=Z_{0,i}^{(-k)}+\lambda\{Z_{1,i}^{(-k)}-Z_{0,i}^{(-k)}\}\) only on the held-out RCT fold. This prevents an observation's own outcome from entering its nuisance prediction, but estimates from different folds remain dependent.

For each \(c\in\mathfrak C\), candidate fitting, basis construction, penalty
selection, and tuning-dependent preprocessing must exclude the validation
portion of block 2 and all of block 0. The
common-reference validation score then ranks candidates already fitted on
block 1 and the fitting portion of block 2. Reusing block-2 validation outcomes
to fit a candidate or its preprocessing
violates the held-out product law and lies outside
Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-selector-inequality}.

A prospective R-loss extension can be implemented as an empirical candidate, but it requires a separate theorem before it belongs to the F-Learner class. Let \(m\) be a generic fixed, cross-fitted marginal-outcome regression trained independently of the observations on which the following loss is evaluated. One candidate objective is
\begin{align}
\widehat L_R(t;\omega)
&=
\mathbb P_{R,n}
\left[
\{Y-m(X)\}-\{A-e(X)\}t(X)
\right]^2
\\
&\quad+
\omega\left[
\mathbb P_{O,N}\{r(X)(g(X)-t(X))^2\}
-
\mathbb P_{R,n}\{(g(X)-t(X))^2\}
\right],
\label{eq:r-fusion-loss}
\end{align}
Randomized treatment implies
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_R[(A-e(X))\{Y-m(X)\}\mid X]
&=e(X)\{1-e(X)\}\tau(X)
\end{align}
for every fixed \(m\). This identity motivates the residualized objective. No \(\lambda\) channel has yet been derived for this R-loss candidate. A complete result must still derive the precise target under penalties and restricted learners, a principled role for \(\lambda\), an honest validation score, and estimated-nuisance rates. We therefore present Equation~\eqref{eq:r-fusion-loss} as a planned extension rather than a proved contribution.

Under covariate shift, an exact ratio can be recovered from domain-classification odds. If \(S=1\) denotes RCT membership, \(\pi=P(S=1)\), and \(s(x)=P(S=1\mid X=x)\), then
\begin{align}
r(x)
&=\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}\frac{s(x)}{1-s(x)}.
\end{align}
An estimated ratio \(\widehat r\) replaces exact target preservation with an explicit drift. Clipping changes the exact transported target and must be reported.

A loss-targeted alternative chooses a nonnegative, out-of-sample-evaluable weight function \(w\) to balance
\begin{align}
\mathcal G(\mathcal T;g)
&=\{(g-t)^2:t\in\mathcal T\}.
\end{align}
If \(\mathbb E_O\{w(g-t)^2\}=\mathbb E_R\{(g-t)^2\}\) for every \(t\in\mathcal T\), Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-target} remains exact with \(w\) in place of \(r\). For a linear sieve, balancing \(bg\) and \(bb^\top\) preserves the minimizer; balancing \(g^2\) is additionally required only to preserve the absolute loss value. Exact balancing on the same observations used to fit \(t\) makes the correction zero or constant in the sieve coefficient. Weight learning and candidate fitting must therefore use different honest blocks or appropriate outer folds.

For approximate weights, define
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\mathcal T}(w)
&=
\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
\left|
\mathbb E_O\{w(X)(g(X)-t(X))^2\}
-
\mathbb E_R\{(g(X)-t(X))^2\}
\right|.
\end{align}
The uniform population-loss drift is bounded by \(|\omega|\Delta_{\mathcal T}(w)\), and the excess target risk of the weighted population minimizer is at most \(2|\omega|\Delta_{\mathcal T}(w)\) when both minimizers exist.

\paragraph{Current scope and open theory.}
The proved CATE statements cover the AIPW-pseudo-outcome squared loss with fixed honest nuisances and either a common covariate marginal or exact transport. The linear-sieve solution and frozen-feature extension are conditional results. Estimated-ratio rates, adaptive loss-targeted balancing, end-to-end neural optimization, the R-loss extension, and cross-fitted inference remain open theory. Honest splitting prevents direct reuse but does not by itself close these gaps.

A reproducible analysis should report the candidate class \(\mathfrak C\), RCT-only subclass \(\mathfrak C_R\), folds, basis dimension, penalty and scaling, ratio method and overlap diagnostics, denominator fallbacks, selected candidate, and validation score of the identical RCT-only comparator. Values of \(\omega\) outside \([0,1]\) require an explicit curvature check of the original empirical objective.
