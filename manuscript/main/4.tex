\section{Data Fusion for CATE Estimation}
\label{sec:cate}

In this section, we develop two methods that combine an RCT with a potentially
confounded OBS to estimate the CATE in the RCT population. The RCT identifies
the causal target. The OBS outcome regressions and their treatment-effect
prediction may improve statistical efficiency, but they need not be causally
valid. The first method is the Doubly Robust Fusion Learner, based on an AIPW
pseudo-outcome. The second is the R-Fusion Learner, based on an R-loss scaled
by the known trial propensity variance.

\subsection{CATE targets and RCT-valid learning losses}
\label{sec:cate-loss}

\begin{definition}[CATE target, candidate class, and ideal risk]
The target is
\(\tau(x)\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x\}\). Let
\(\mathcal Z\subseteq L^2(P_R^X)\) be a prespecified nonempty class of
candidate CATE functions, such as a fixed linear sieve, and let
\(\zeta\in\mathcal Z\). Its ideal risk is
\begin{align}
\mathcal R(\zeta)
&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{\zeta(X)-\tau(X)\}^2.
\label{eq:cate-ideal-risk}
\end{align}
Throughout each conditional calculation, the nuisance fits are held fixed.
\end{definition}

The first tuning coefficient blends the RCT and OBS outcome-regression pairs
inside an RCT-valid score.
\begin{definition}[Fused outcome nuisance and pseudo-outcome]
For a fixed \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), define
\begin{align*}
q_R
&\triangleq\widehat\mu_R,\\
q_O
&\triangleq\widehat\mu_O,\\
q_\lambda
&\triangleq(1-\lambda)q_R+\lambda q_O,\\
Z_\lambda
&\triangleq\varphi(V;q_\lambda).
\end{align*}
The AIPW score \(\varphi\) is defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:aipw-score}.
\end{definition}

Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:trial} and \ref{ass:honest-separation},
Equation~\eqref{eq:pseudo-valid} gives, for every fixed \(\lambda\),
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda\mid X)&=\tau(X).
\end{align*}
Thus the known RCT propensity makes the fused pseudo-outcome conditionally
valid without requiring either fitted outcome regression to be correct.

\begin{proposition}[RCT DR loss and target]
\label{prop:cate-rct-risk}
Under the same assumptions, for fixed \(\lambda\in[0,1]\),
\(Z_\lambda\in L^2(P_R)\), and \(\zeta\in\mathcal Z\), define
\begin{align*}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda)
&\triangleq
\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\zeta(X)\}^2,\\
Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)
&\triangleq
\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\tau(X)\}^2.
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{align*}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda)
&=Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)+\mathcal R(\zeta).
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda)
&=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}
\mathcal R(\zeta).
\end{align*}
Thus minimizing the population RCT DR loss over \(\mathcal Z\) returns the
best CATE approximation in \(\mathcal Z\) under the ideal risk in
Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-ideal-risk}.
The term \(Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)\) is a \(\zeta\)-independent noise floor,
not the final risk of a fitted learner.
\end{proposition}

For the R-learner-style construction, define the known RCT propensity and
the corresponding marginal-outcome nuisances using the same \(\lambda\):
\begin{align*}
e_0(x)&\triangleq P_R(A=1\mid X=x),\\
m_R(x)&\triangleq e_0(x)q_{R,1}(x)+\{1-e_0(x)\}q_{R,0}(x),\\
m_O(x)&\triangleq e_0(x)q_{O,1}(x)+\{1-e_0(x)\}q_{O,0}(x),\\
m_\lambda(x)&\triangleq(1-\lambda)m_R(x)+\lambda m_O(x).
\end{align*}

\begin{proposition}[RCT R loss and target]
\label{prop:cate-rf-risk}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:trial} and \ref{ass:honest-separation}, let \(m\)
be a fixed admissible measurable function, suppose
\(Y-m(X)\in L^2(P_R)\), and let \(\zeta\in\mathcal Z\). Define
\begin{align*}
\mathcal L_R(\zeta;m)
&\triangleq
\mathbb E_R\left[
\frac{[\,Y-m(X)-\{A-e_0(X)\}\zeta(X)\,]^2}
{e_0(X)\{1-e_0(X)\}}
\right],\\
Q_R(m)
&\triangleq
\mathbb E_R\left[
\frac{[\,Y-m(X)-\{A-e_0(X)\}\tau(X)\,]^2}
{e_0(X)\{1-e_0(X)\}}
\right].
\end{align*}
Suppose the displayed losses are integrable. Then
\begin{align*}
\mathcal L_R(\zeta;m)
&=Q_R(m)+\mathcal R(\zeta).
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}
\mathcal L_R(\zeta;m)
&=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\zeta\in\mathcal Z}
\mathcal R(\zeta).
\end{align*}
Thus minimizing the population RCT R loss over \(\mathcal Z\) returns the
same best CATE approximation under the ideal risk in
Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-ideal-risk}. In particular, \(Q_R(m_\lambda)\) is the
noise floor associated with the fused marginal-outcome nuisance. The two
terms \(Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)\) and \(Q_R(m_\lambda)\) are different noise
floors and need not select the same \(\lambda\); neither is the fitted CATE
risk.
\end{proposition}

Both RCT-only routes identify the desired projection, but neither directly
averages the large OBS covariate sample. Let \(g\) denote the fitted OBS
treatment-effect prediction in Equation~\eqref{eq:obs-effect-prediction} and
define \(G_\zeta(X)\triangleq\{g(X)-\zeta(X)\}^2\). The following full-fusion
objects are retained here so that the subsequent orthogonality and
finite-sample results remain self-contained.

The primary \emph{Doubly Robust Fusion (DRF) Learner} extends
\(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}\) to use OBS covariates through the RCT-valid AIPW
score. Here ``doubly robust'' names that score construction under known
\(e_0\); it does not by itself assert numerical-loss orthogonality or
robustness to ratio error.
\begin{definition}[Doubly Robust Fusion loss]
For fixed \(\zeta\), define
\begin{align}
\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r)
&\triangleq \mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{fit}}\{Z_\lambda-\zeta(X)\}^2
+\omega\left[
\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{fit}}\{r(X)G_\zeta(X)\}
-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{fit}}\{G_\zeta(X)\}
\right].
\label{eq:dr-fusion-loss}
\end{align}
Assume \(Z_\lambda\in L^2(P_R)\), \(\zeta\in L^2(P_R^X)\),
\(G_\zeta\in L^1(P_R)\), and
\(r_0G_\zeta\in L^1(P_O)\). The coefficient \(\omega\) scales the
OBS loss correction and is not part of \(G_\zeta\).
\end{definition}

The first term in Equation~\eqref{eq:dr-fusion-loss} is the sample counterpart
of \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda)\). The coefficient \(\omega\)
multiplies the transported OBS loss correction, whose mean is zero when
\(r=r_0\).

\begin{proposition}[Exact DRF target]
\label{prop:cate-target}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:trial} and \ref{ass:honest-separation}, suppose
either Assumption~\ref{ass:common-marginal} holds and \(r_0=1\), or
Assumption~\ref{ass:exact-transport} holds and
\(r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X\). Use \(r=r_0\). Then
\begin{align}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}
&=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda)
\nonumber\\
&=Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)+\mathcal R(\zeta).
\label{eq:cate-target}
\end{align}
Thus the population minimizers over any fixed class \(\mathcal Z\) are the
\(L_2(P_R^X)\) projections of \(\tau\). Under the common covariate marginal,
\(r_0=1\). The expected loss is flat in \(\omega\).
\end{proposition}

To leverage OBS covariates through the R route, the secondary
\emph{R-Fusion (RF) Learner} extends \(\mathcal L_R(\zeta;m)\) with a
transported loss correction.
\begin{definition}[R-Fusion loss]
For a fixed marginal-outcome function \(m\), define
\begin{align*}
\kappa(A,X)
&\triangleq
\frac{\{A-e_0(X)\}^2}{e_0(X)\{1-e_0(X)\}}.
\end{align*}
Define
\begin{align}
\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;m,\omega,r)
&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{fit}}\left[
\frac{[\,Y-m(X)-\{A-e_0(X)\}\zeta(X)\,]^2}
{e_0(X)\{1-e_0(X)\}}
\right]\notag\\
&\quad+\omega\left[
\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{fit}}\{r(X)G_\zeta(X)\}
-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{fit}}\{\kappa(A,X)G_\zeta(X)\}
\right].
\label{eq:rf-fusion-loss}
\end{align}
Assume \(\tau\in L^2(P_R)\) and
the displayed RCT loss is integrable.
\end{definition}

The first term in Equation~\eqref{eq:rf-fusion-loss} is the sample counterpart
of \(\mathcal L_R(\zeta;m)\). The factor \(\kappa(A,X)\) is the RCT curvature
weight induced by the scaled squared treatment residual, so the RCT
subtraction matches the R-loss curvature. The correction has mean zero under
exact transport.
\begin{theorem}[Exact RF target]
\label{thm:rf-target}
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target},
\begin{align}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;m,\omega,r_0)\}
&=\mathcal L_R(\zeta;m)
\nonumber\\
&=Q_R(m)+\mathcal R(\zeta).
\label{eq:rf-target}
\end{align}
In particular, setting \(m=m_\lambda\) gives the noise floor
\(Q_R(m_\lambda)\).
Hence the population minimizers over a fixed class are the same unweighted
\(L_2(P_R^X)\) projections of \(\tau\).
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Orthogonality of learning equations and nuisance sensitivity}
\label{sec:cate-orthogonality}

Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target} and Theorem~\ref{thm:rf-target} establish
the exact population targets. We now ask a different question: how does each
fitted object enter the first-order equation that learns \(\zeta\)? Following
\citet[Assumption~1]{foster2023orthogonal}, loss orthogonality means that the
mixed derivative \(D_\eta D_\zeta L\) vanishes at the population target and
nuisance truth. Equivalently, up to scaling, it means
\(D_\eta\Psi=0\) for \(\Psi\triangleq-D_\zeta L/2\). This definition alone
does not establish an oracle convergence rate.

\begin{definition}[Derivative directions and learning roles]
For a functional \(F\), an object \(\eta\), and an admissible direction \(h\),
define the Gateaux derivative
\begin{align*}
D_\eta F(\eta)[h]
&\triangleq
\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}F(\eta+\varepsilon h)
\right|_{\varepsilon=0}.
\end{align*}
The target argument is \(\zeta\), and \(v\) denotes an admissible target
direction such that \(\zeta+\varepsilon v\) remains in the ambient loss domain
for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\). The learned objects \(q,m,g,r\)
are nuisance directions. The coefficients \(\lambda\) and \(\omega\) are held fixed during
this audit: \(\lambda\) selects \(q_\lambda\) or \(m_\lambda\), while
\(\omega\) scales the OBS correction. The trial propensity \(e_0\) is known
by design.
\end{definition}

Write \(Z_q\triangleq\varphi(V;q)\),
\(s_0(X)\triangleq e_0(X)\{1-e_0(X)\}\), and
\(\kappa_0(A,X)\triangleq\kappa(A,X)
=\{A-e_0(X)\}^2/s_0(X)\).
\begin{definition}[Full fusion learning equations]
For fixed \((\lambda,\omega)\), define the DRF and RF population learning
equations by
\begin{align}
\Psi_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;q,g,r)[v]
&\triangleq
-\frac12D_\zeta\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}\}[v]
\nonumber\\
&=\mathbb E_R[v(X)\{Z_q-\zeta(X)\}]
\nonumber\\
&\quad+\omega\left[
\mathbb E_O\{r(X)v(X)[g(X)-\zeta(X)]\}
-\mathbb E_R\{v(X)[g(X)-\zeta(X)]\}
\right],
\label{eq:drf-learning-equation}\\
\Psi_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;m,g,r)[v]
&\triangleq
-\frac12D_\zeta\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}\}[v]
\nonumber\\
&=\mathbb E_R\left[
v(X)\frac{A-e_0(X)}{s_0(X)}
\{Y-m(X)-[A-e_0(X)]\zeta(X)\}\right]
\nonumber\\
&\quad+\omega\left[
\mathbb E_O\{r(X)v(X)[g(X)-\zeta(X)]\}
-\mathbb E_R\{\kappa_0(A,X)v(X)[g(X)-\zeta(X)]\}
\right].
\label{eq:rf-learning-equation}
\end{align}
All derivatives hold the remaining displayed arguments fixed.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact-transport learning equations]
\label{prop:cate-learning-equations}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known propensity
\(e_0\) satisfying strict overlap, and nuisance functions fitted honestly on
data independent of the loss-evaluation observations.  Suppose either that
the RCT and OBS have a common covariate marginal, in which case \(r_0=1\),
or that exact covariate transport holds with
\(r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X\).  Use \(r=r_0\), and suppose all displayed
expectations below are finite.
For every integrable function \(f(X)\), exact transport gives
\(\mathbb E_O(r_0f)=\mathbb E_R(f)\), and randomization gives
\(\mathbb E_R(\kappa_0\mid X)=1\). Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\Psi_{\mathrm{DRF}}(\zeta;q,g,r_0)[v]
&=\mathbb E_R[v(X)\{Z_q-\zeta(X)\}],\\
\Psi_{\mathrm{RF}}(\zeta;m,g,r_0)[v]
&=\mathbb E_R\left[
v(X)\frac{A-e_0(X)}{s_0(X)}
\{Y-m(X)-[A-e_0(X)]\zeta(X)\}\right].
\end{align*}
Thus exact transport removes the OBS correction from the population learning
equation without removing it from the empirical optimization problem.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Direction-by-direction learning-equation orthogonality]
\label{prop:cate-orthogonality-audit}
Assume honest fitting, strict trial overlap, the integrability needed to
interchange differentiation and expectation, and exact fixed \(r_0\) where
stated. Then the following partial-path statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For an outcome-regression direction \(h=(h_0,h_1)\),
\begin{align*}
\dot Z_q[h]
&\triangleq
-\{A-e_0(X)\}\left\{
\frac{h_1(X)}{e_0(X)}+\frac{h_0(X)}{1-e_0(X)}\right\},\\
D_q\Psi_{\mathrm{DRF}}[h,v]
&=\mathbb E_R\{v(X)\dot Z_q[h]\}=0.
\end{align*}
This exact score orthogonality is the main protection supplied by the known
trial propensity.

\item For a marginal-outcome direction \(h\),
\begin{align*}
D_m\Psi_{\mathrm{RF}}[h,v]
&=-\mathbb E_R\left[
v(X)\frac{A-e_0(X)}{s_0(X)}h(X)\right]=0.
\end{align*}

\item For a prediction direction \(u\) and either
\(j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}\),
\begin{align}
D_g\Psi_j[u,v]
&=\omega\mathbb E_O[\{r(X)-r_0(X)\}u(X)v(X)],
\label{eq:cate-g-score-derivative}\\
D_g\mathbb E(\widehat L_j)[u]
&=2\omega\mathbb E_O[\{r(X)-r_0(X)\}
\{g(X)-\zeta(X)\}u(X)].
\label{eq:cate-g-loss-derivative}
\end{align}
Both derivatives vanish when \(r=r_0\), but not generally for an approximate
ratio.

\item For a ratio direction \(h_r\),
\begin{align}
D_r\Psi_j[h_r,v]
&=\omega\mathbb E_O[h_r(X)v(X)\{g(X)-\zeta(X)\}],
\label{eq:cate-ratio-score-derivative}\\
D_r\mathbb E\{\widehat L_j\}[h_r]
&=\omega\mathbb E_O[h_r(X)\{g(X)-\zeta(X)\}^2].
\label{eq:drf-ratio-derivative}
\end{align}
These derivatives are generally nonzero.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The normalization constraint on a density ratio restricts its regular tangent
directions. Define
\begin{align*}
\mathcal T_r
&\triangleq L_0^2(P_O^X)
=\{h\in L^2(P_O^X):\mathbb E_O(h)=0\}.
\end{align*}
This linear space describes first-order normalization; admissible paths must
also respect support and nonnegativity, and a ratio on the positivity boundary
may have only a tangent cone.

\begin{proposition}[Density-ratio nonorthogonality]
\label{prop:cate-ratio-nonorthogonality}
Suppose \(\omega\neq0\) and
\(v(g-\zeta)\in L^2(P_O^X)\).
Over the full normalized tangent space \(\mathcal T_r\), the ratio-score
derivative in Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-ratio-score-derivative} vanishes for
every \(h_r\in\mathcal T_r\) if and only if
\(v(X)\{g(X)-\zeta(X)\}\) is constant \(P_O^X\)-almost surely. Therefore,
the learning equations are generally not orthogonal to the density ratio.
Balancing selected empirical moments does not generally produce orthogonality
over the full normalized tangent space.
\end{proposition}

As a direct chain-rule consequence of items 1--3 in
Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-orthogonality-audit}, if the same OBS outcome
regression generates \((q_O,g)\) for DRF or \((m_O,g)\) for RF, then its
total learning-equation derivative is zero when \(e_0\) is known and exact
\(r_0\) is held fixed. This conclusion does not cover paths that change
\(r\), or the use of an approximate ratio.

These results audit nuisance sensitivity; they do not select the target
function or the tuning coefficients. The learner optimizes over \(\zeta\),
while \(\lambda\) changes the fused nuisance and \(\omega\) changes the
transported correction. The next subsection therefore selects
\((\lambda,\omega)\) by the risk of the fitted CATE function.

\subsection{Choosing the fusion coefficients}
\label{sec:cate-risk-selection}

Let \(j\in\{\mathrm{DRF},\mathrm{RF}\}\) label the Doubly Robust Fusion
Learner or the R-Fusion Learner.  Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R\) be a
prespecified compact set containing zero.  For fixed
\((\lambda,\omega)\in[0,1]\times\Omega\), define
\(\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal Z\) as the output of a
prespecified empirical minimization rule, with deterministic tie-breaking,
applied to the empirical DRF loss in
Equation~\eqref{eq:dr-fusion-loss} or the empirical RF loss in
Equation~\eqref{eq:rf-fusion-loss}, respectively, on the fitting sample.
In DRF, \(\lambda\) changes \(Z_\lambda\).  In RF, it changes
\(m_\lambda\).  In both methods, \(\omega\) changes the empirical transported
OBS correction.  Hence the fitted function depends on both coefficients,
even though exact transport cancels the correction in expectation for each
fixed candidate function.

Recall from Equation~\eqref{eq:cate-ideal-risk} that the ideal CATE risk of a
function \(f\) is
\begin{align*}
\mathcal R(f)&=\mathbb E_R[\{f(X)-\tau(X)\}^2].
\end{align*}
Throughout this section, an efficiency gain means a reduction in the
integrated squared CATE error \(\mathcal R\) of the fitted learner.  This
operational learning criterion is distinct from attaining a semiparametric
efficiency bound.  OBS information may reduce this error without changing
the RCT-population target.  Conditional on the nuisance and fitting samples
and all fitting-stage algorithmic randomness, whenever the minimum is
attained, define the infeasible final-risk oracle by
\begin{align}
(\lambda_j^\star,\omega_j^\star)
&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\lambda\in[0,1],\,\omega\in\Omega}
\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)\}.
\label{eq:final-risk-oracle}
\end{align}
This oracle is infeasible because \(\tau\) is unknown, and its value changes
across fitting-sample realizations.  For both methods,
\((\lambda,\omega)=(0,0)\) gives the corresponding RCT-only learner.  Since
this pair is feasible, the oracle obeys
the conditional comparison
\begin{align}
\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda_j^\star,\omega_j^\star)\}
&\leq\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;0,0)\}.
\label{eq:conditional-rct-oracle-comparison}
\end{align}
This is an oracle comparison, not a guarantee for estimated coefficients.
There is no universal closed form for Equation~\eqref{eq:final-risk-oracle}.

Two simpler closed forms minimize the base-loss noise floors rather than the
fitted risk.  Recall that \(Z_0=\varphi(V;q_R)\) and
\(Z_1=\varphi(V;q_O)\), while \(m_R\) and \(m_O\) are the corresponding
RCT and OBS marginal-outcome nuisances.  Propositions~\ref{prop:cate-rct-risk}
and \ref{prop:cate-rf-risk} define \(Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)\) and
\(Q_R(m_\lambda)\), respectively.  These terms do not equal the ideal risk
of a fitted CATE function.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-form noise-floor coefficients]
\label{prop:cate-noise-floor-lambda}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known propensity
\(e_0\) satisfying strict overlap, and nuisance functions fitted honestly on
data independent of the observations used below.  Condition on these fixed
nuisance fits and suppose the displayed expectations are finite.  Let
\(q_R=(q_{R,0},q_{R,1})\) and \(q_O=(q_{O,0},q_{O,1})\) be the fixed RCT and
OBS outcome-regression pairs, and define
\begin{align*}
Z_0&\triangleq\varphi(V;q_R),
&Z_1&\triangleq\varphi(V;q_O),\\
m_R(X)&\triangleq e_0(X)q_{R,1}(X)+\{1-e_0(X)\}q_{R,0}(X),\\
m_O(X)&\triangleq e_0(X)q_{O,1}(X)+\{1-e_0(X)\}q_{O,0}(X),\\
\Delta&\triangleq Z_1-Z_0,
&d_m(X)&\triangleq m_O(X)-m_R(X).
\end{align*}
For \(Z_\lambda\triangleq Z_0+\lambda\Delta\) and
\(m_\lambda\triangleq m_R+\lambda d_m\), define
\begin{align*}
Q_{\mathrm{DR}}(\lambda)
&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{Z_\lambda-\tau(X)\}^2,\\
Q_R(m_\lambda)
&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\left[
\frac{[Y-m_\lambda(X)-\{A-e_0(X)\}\tau(X)]^2}
{e_0(X)\{1-e_0(X)\}}\right].
\end{align*}
For an interval, the projection used below is
\(\operatorname{Proj}_{[0,1]}(u)\triangleq\min\{1,\max(0,u)\}\).
If \(\mathbb E_R(\Delta^2)>0\), the constrained DR noise-floor minimizer is
\begin{align}
\lambda_{\mathrm{DR},Q}^{\star}
&\triangleq\operatorname{Proj}_{[0,1]}
\left\{-\frac{\mathbb E_R(Z_0\Delta)}{\mathbb E_R(\Delta^2)}\right\}.
\label{eq:cate-dr-noise-lambda}
\end{align}
If
\(\mathbb E_R[d_m(X)^2/\{e_0(X)(1-e_0(X))\}]>0\), the constrained R
noise-floor minimizer is
\begin{align}
\lambda_{R,Q}^{\star}
&\triangleq\operatorname{Proj}_{[0,1]}\left[
\frac{
\mathbb E_R[\{Y-m_R(X)\}d_m(X)/\{e_0(X)(1-e_0(X))\}]
}{
\mathbb E_R[d_m(X)^2/\{e_0(X)(1-e_0(X))\}]
}\right].
\label{eq:cate-r-noise-lambda}
\end{align}
If either denominator is zero, its corresponding noise floor is flat in
\(\lambda\), so every \(\lambda\in[0,1]\) is a minimizer.  The displayed
equivalent moments do not involve \(\tau\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Population-loss flatness in the fusion scale]
\label{prop:cate-omega-flatness}
Assume RCT consistency and conditional randomization, a known trial
propensity \(e_0\) satisfying strict overlap, and nuisance functions fitted
honestly on data independent of the loss-evaluation observations.  Suppose
either that the RCT and OBS have a common covariate marginal with \(r_0=1\),
or that exact covariate transport holds with
\(r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X\).  Use \(r=r_0\), let
\(\Omega\subset\mathbb R\) be nonempty and compact, and fix
\(\zeta\in\mathcal Z\) and \(\lambda\in[0,1]\).  Condition on the fitted
nuisances and suppose the expectations below are finite.  For every
\(\omega\in\Omega\), the method-specific corrections satisfy
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_O\{r_0(X)G_\zeta(X)\}-\mathbb E_R\{G_\zeta(X)\}
&=0,\\
\mathbb E_O\{r_0(X)G_\zeta(X)\}
-\mathbb E_R\{\kappa(A,X)G_\zeta(X)\}
&=0.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}
(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}
&=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DR}}(\zeta;\lambda),\\
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}
(\zeta;m_\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}
&=\mathcal L_R(\zeta;m_\lambda),
\end{align*}
and both right-hand sides are constant in \(\omega\).  Therefore,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\omega\in\Omega}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{DRF}}
(\zeta;\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}
&=\Omega,\\
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\omega\in\Omega}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{\mathrm{RF}}
(\zeta;m_\lambda,\omega,r_0)\}
&=\Omega.
\end{align*}
Thus population loss at a fixed function and fixed \(\lambda\) cannot select
a unique fusion scale.
\end{proposition}

This fixed-function result does not imply that
\(\mathcal R\{\widehat\tau_j(\cdot;\lambda,\omega)\}\) is flat in
\(\omega\), because the empirical fitting problem, and hence its fitted
output, depends on \(\omega\).  To address this issue, we instead approximate
the infeasible oracle in Equation~\eqref{eq:final-risk-oracle} by honest
validation.  Honest validation uses validation observations only to compare
fully fixed candidates, never to fit nuisances, candidates, or validation
coefficients.

Let \(M\geq1\), and let
\(\widehat\tau_0,\ldots,\widehat\tau_{M-1}\) be the finite collection of
fitted CATE functions produced by prespecified candidate specifications and
fixed before validation.  Each specification encodes its method, fitting
coefficients, basis, and penalty.
Candidate \(0\) is the identical RCT-only learner.  Let
\(Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\) be a common RCT-valid pseudo-outcome, fixed before
validation, with
\(\mathbb E_R(Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\mid X)=\tau(X)\).  Let \(g\) be the fitted
OBS treatment-effect prediction from
Equation~\eqref{eq:obs-effect-prediction}.  Define the validation ratio by
\begin{align*}
r_{\mathrm{val}}&\triangleq
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{under the common covariate marginal},\\
r_0, & \text{under exact covariate transport}.
\end{cases}
\end{align*}
This exact \(r_{\mathrm{val}}\) is supplied to the validation identity and
theorem below; it is not fitted within Algorithm~\ref{alg:cate-fusion-selection}.
The feasible evaluation-independent estimate \(\widehat r_{\mathrm{val}}\)
and its resulting drift are treated after the theorem.
Let \(\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{val}}\) and
\(\mathbb P_{O,\mathrm{val}}\) denote the empirical averages over the RCT
and OBS validation samples.  For each \(k\), fix a validation
control-variate coefficient \(\nu_k\) before observing validation outcomes
and define
\begin{align*}
G_k(X)&\triangleq\{g(X)-\widehat\tau_k(X)\}^2.
\end{align*}
Under exact \(r_{\mathrm{val}}\), the coefficient \(\nu_k\) affects only
validation-score variability and need not equal the \(\omega\) used to fit
candidate \(k\).  Define
\begin{align}
\widehat C_k
&\triangleq\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{val}}
\{Z_{\mathrm{ref}}-\widehat\tau_k(X)\}^2
+\nu_k\left[
\mathbb P_{O,\mathrm{val}}\{r_{\mathrm{val}}G_k\}
-\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{val}}G_k\right].
\label{eq:common-reference-validation}
\end{align}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Honest selection of CATE fusion coefficients}
\label{alg:cate-fusion-selection}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{Independent nuisance, fitting, and validation samples; \(M\) prespecified
candidate specifications; a shared
\(Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\); the prespecified exact validation ratio
\(r_{\mathrm{val}}\); and validation-independent coefficients
\(\nu_0,\ldots,\nu_{M-1}\).}
Fit the nuisances on the nuisance samples.  A candidate specification may
also fit its own prespecified training ratio on these samples.\;
Fit every \(\widehat\tau_k\) on the fitting samples using its prespecified
method and tuning values.\;
Compute \(\widehat C_k\) in Equation~\eqref{eq:common-reference-validation}
for every \(k\) on the common validation samples.\;
Set \(\widehat k\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{0\leq k<M}\widehat C_k\).\;
\KwOut{The selected learner \(\widehat\tau_{\widehat k}\).}
\end{algorithm}

The simple choices \(\nu_k=0\), \(\nu_k=1\), or \(\nu_k=\omega_k\), where
\(\omega_k\) is candidate \(k\)'s fitting coefficient, are valid when
prespecified.  A variance-oriented \(\nu_k\) may instead be learned on an
additional sample,
but optimizing it on the same validation outcomes is not covered below.

Under the exact common-marginal or transport regime just defined,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{val}}(\widehat C_k)
&=Q_{\mathrm{ref}}+\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_k),
\label{eq:common-reference-identity}
\end{align}
where \(Q_{\mathrm{ref}}\triangleq
\mathbb E_R\{Z_{\mathrm{ref}}-\tau(X)\}^2\).
Define the best realized candidate by
\begin{align*}
k^\star&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{0\leq k<M}
\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_k).
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-class honest validation]
\label{thm:cate-finite-validation}
Let the RCT and OBS validation samples be mutually independent and independent
of the fitted candidates, and condition on candidates and coefficients fixed
before validation.  Let the common reference score be RCT-valid,
\(\mathbb E_R(Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\mid X)=\tau(X)\), and independent of the
validation observations.  Use exact transport
\(r_{\mathrm{val}}=r_0=dP_R^X/dP_O^X\), including the common-marginal case
\(r_{\mathrm{val}}=r_0=1\).  Let
\(\widehat\tau_0,\ldots,\widehat\tau_{M-1}\) be finite and fixed before
validation, with candidate \(0\) the identical RCT-only learner.  Suppose
\(\nu_0,\ldots,\nu_{M-1}\) are fixed before validation.  For each \(k\),
let \(g\) be the fixed OBS treatment-effect prediction and define
\begin{align*}
G_k(X)&\triangleq\{g(X)-\widehat\tau_k(X)\}^2,\\
\widehat C_k
&\triangleq\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{val}}
\{Z_{\mathrm{ref}}-\widehat\tau_k(X)\}^2
+\nu_k\left[
\mathbb P_{O,\mathrm{val}}\{r_{\mathrm{val}}G_k\}
-\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{val}}G_k\right].
\end{align*}
Here, \(\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{val}}\) and
\(\mathbb P_{O,\mathrm{val}}\) are the corresponding empirical averages.
Let \(n_v\) and \(N_v\) be the RCT and OBS validation sample sizes, define
\(\mathcal R(f)\triangleq\mathbb E_R[\{f(X)-\tau(X)\}^2]\), and let
\(k^\star\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{0\leq k<M}
\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_k)\).  Suppose
the centered RCT and OBS per-observation scores, including their prespecified
\(\nu_k\) multipliers, have uniform range lengths at most \(B_R\) and
\(B_O\). Define
\begin{align*}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{val}}(\delta)&\triangleq
B_R\sqrt{\frac{\log(4M/\delta)}{2n_v}}
+B_O\sqrt{\frac{\log(4M/\delta)}{2N_v}}.
\end{align*}
Here, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{val}}(\delta)\) is a uniform validation
estimation-error radius across the \(M\) candidates; it decreases with the
validation sizes \(n_v,N_v\), and increases with \(M\) and confidence level
\(1-\delta\).  If
\(\widehat k\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{0\leq k<M}\widehat C_k\), then,
with probability at least \(1-\delta\),
\begin{align}
\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_{\widehat k})
&\leq\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_{k^\star})
+2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{val}}(\delta)
\leq\mathcal R(\widehat\tau_0)
+2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{val}}(\delta).
\label{eq:cate-selector-inequality}
\end{align}
This is not realized-sample universal dominance.
\end{theorem}

For an evaluation-independent approximate ratio, define
\(\widehat r_{\mathrm{val}}\) and replace \(r_{\mathrm{val}}\) in
Equation~\eqref{eq:common-reference-validation}.  If the reference
conditional bias is
\(b_{\mathrm{ref}}(X)\triangleq
\mathbb E_R(Z_{\mathrm{ref}}-\tau(X)\mid X)\), define
the two uniform drifts directly by
\begin{align}
\Delta_r
&\triangleq\max_{0\leq k<M}
\left|\nu_k\mathbb E_O[(\widehat r_{\mathrm{val}}-r_0)G_k]\right|,\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
&\triangleq\max_{0\leq k<M}
\left|2\mathbb E_R[b_{\mathrm{ref}}
\{\tau-\widehat\tau_k\}]\right|.
\label{eq:validation-drift-bounds}
\end{align}
Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-finite-validation} then adds
\(2\Delta_r+2\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\), provided its
concentration envelope holds for the actual weighted scores.

\subsection{Linear-sieve estimation and profiled coefficient selection}
\label{sec:cate-sieve}

In general, the empirical inverse Hessian and score depend on the same fitting
sample, final-risk coefficients have no universal closed form, and ratio error
perturbs both score and curvature. We therefore begin with a fixed linear
sieve. Let \(b(x)\in\mathbb R^p\) be fixed or honestly trained and define
\(t_\beta(x)\triangleq b(x)^\top\beta\). Let \(P\succeq0\) and \(\rho\geq0\).
For DRF, define
\begin{align*}
G_R&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{fit}}(bb^\top),
&G_{O,r}&\triangleq\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{fit}}(r_0bb^\top),\\
h_\lambda&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{fit}}(bZ_\lambda),
&d_g&\triangleq\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{fit}}(r_0bg)
-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{fit}}(bg).
\end{align*}
Whenever the matrix is invertible,
\begin{align}
\widehat\beta_{\lambda,\omega}^{\mathrm{DRF}}
&=\{(1-\omega)G_R+\omega G_{O,r}+\rho P\}^{-1}
\{h_\lambda+\omega d_g\}.
\label{eq:linear-sieve}
\end{align}
For RF, define
\begin{align*}
s_{m_\lambda}&\triangleq
\frac{(A-e_0)(Y-m_\lambda)}{e_0(1-e_0)},
&G_{R,\kappa}&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{fit}}(\kappa bb^\top),\\
h_{m_\lambda}&\triangleq\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{fit}}(bs_{m_\lambda}),
&d_{g,\kappa}&\triangleq\mathbb P_O^{\mathrm{fit}}(r_0bg)
-\mathbb P_R^{\mathrm{fit}}(\kappa bg).
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{align}
\widehat\beta_{\lambda,\omega}^{\mathrm{RF}}
&=\{(1-\omega)G_{R,\kappa}+\omega G_{O,r}+\rho P\}^{-1}
\{h_{m_\lambda}+\omega d_{g,\kappa}\}.
\label{eq:linear-sieve-rf}
\end{align}
For \(0\leq\omega\leq1\), nonnegative \(r_0\), and \(P\succeq0\), both
Hessians are positive semidefinite. A feasible DRF or RF fit using
\(\widehat r\) requires \(\widehat r\geq0\), a direct Hessian check, or an
explicit projection onto the positive-semidefinite cone.

For either learner, define
\begin{align*}
G&\triangleq\mathbb E_R(bb^\top),
&\gamma&\triangleq\mathbb E_R(b\tau),\\
\beta_p&\triangleq G^{-1}\gamma,
&t_p&\triangleq b^\top\beta_p,
&a_p^2&\triangleq\mathbb E_R(\tau-t_p)^2.
\end{align*}
Assume \(G\succ0\).
Define the unpenalized Hessian and right-hand-side objects by
\begin{align*}
\widehat H_{\mathrm{DRF}}
&\triangleq(1-\omega)G_R+\omega G_{O,r},
&
\widehat h_{\mathrm{DRF}}
&\triangleq h_\lambda+\omega d_g,\\
\widehat H_{\mathrm{RF}}
&\triangleq(1-\omega)G_{R,\kappa}+\omega G_{O,r},
&
\widehat h_{\mathrm{RF}}
&\triangleq h_{m_\lambda}+\omega d_{g,\kappa}.
\end{align*}
For either \(j\), the penalized normal equation is
\begin{align*}
(\widehat H_j+\rho P)\widehat\beta_j&=\widehat h_j.
\end{align*}
These expressions profile the sieve coefficient, not the final-risk fusion
coefficients.  More precisely, for fixed \(\omega\), write the now-defined
right-hand side as
\begin{align}
\widehat h_j(\lambda,\omega)
&\triangleq\widehat h_{j,0}(\omega)
+\lambda\widehat h_{j,1}(\omega).
\end{align}
It follows that
\begin{align}
\widehat\beta^j_{\lambda,\omega}
&=\widehat\beta^j_{0,\omega}
+\lambda\dot\beta^j_{\omega},
&
\dot\beta^j_{\omega}
&\triangleq
\{\widehat H_j(\omega)+\rho P\}^{-1}
\widehat h_{j,1}(\omega).
\label{eq:linear-sieve-lambda-profile}
\end{align}
Let
\(\widehat t_{0,\omega}^j\triangleq b^\top\widehat\beta^j_{0,\omega}\),
\(\dot t_\omega^j\triangleq b^\top\dot\beta_\omega^j\), and suppose the
validation coefficient \(\nu_\omega\) is fixed across the profiled values of
\(\lambda\).  Expanding Equation~\eqref{eq:common-reference-validation}
gives
\begin{align}
\widehat C_j(\lambda,\omega)
&=c_0(\omega)+2c_1(\omega)\lambda+c_2(\omega)\lambda^2,
\label{eq:linear-sieve-validation-profile}\\
c_1(\omega)
&\triangleq-\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{val}}
[\{Z_{\mathrm{ref}}-\widehat t^j_{0,\omega}\}\dot t_\omega^j]
\nonumber\\
&\quad-\nu_\omega\left[
\mathbb P_{O,\mathrm{val}}
\{r_{\mathrm{val}}(g-\widehat t^j_{0,\omega})\dot t_\omega^j\}
-\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{val}}
\{(g-\widehat t^j_{0,\omega})\dot t_\omega^j\}
\right],\nonumber\\
c_2(\omega)
&\triangleq\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{val}}(\dot t_\omega^j)^2
+\nu_\omega\left[
\mathbb P_{O,\mathrm{val}}\{r_{\mathrm{val}}(\dot t_\omega^j)^2\}
-\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{val}}(\dot t_\omega^j)^2
\right].\nonumber
\end{align}
For each \(\omega\) in a prespecified finite grid, this quadratic can be
minimized on \([0,1]\) by checking its interior stationary point, when it is
a minimum, and the two endpoints.  This reduces computation to a finite
one-dimensional search over \(\omega\).  If the profiling is performed on an
additional tuning sample, its finitely many resulting candidates are fixed
before validation and Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-finite-validation} applies.  A
prespecified finite \((\lambda,\omega)\) grid is another directly covered
option.  Profiling \(\lambda\) on the same validation sample, or searching
continuously over \(\omega\), requires a separate uniform-convergence
argument and is not covered by the finite-class theorem.

Define
\begin{align*}
\widehat S_j&\triangleq\widehat h_j-\widehat H_j\beta_p.
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}[Exact sieve excess-risk identity]
\label{thm:cate-sieve-identity}
If \(\widehat H_j+\rho P\) is invertible, then
\begin{align}
\widehat\beta_j-\beta_p
&=(\widehat H_j+\rho P)^{-1}
\{\widehat S_j-\rho P\beta_p\},
\label{eq:sieve-coefficient-identity}\\
\mathbb E_R(\widehat t_j-\tau)^2
&=a_p^2+(\widehat\beta_j-\beta_p)^\top
G(\widehat\beta_j-\beta_p).
\label{eq:sieve-risk-identity}
\end{align}
No same-sample bias-variance split follows from
\(\mathbb E(\widehat S_j)=0\), because the inverse Hessian and score are
dependent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-candidate finite-sample bound]
\label{thm:cate-fixed-candidate-bound}
Assume the exact-target conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:cate-target} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:rf-target}, known \(e_0\), exact \(r_0\), and honestly fixed
nuisances. Let fitting sizes be \(n_f,N_f\). Let \(\psi_{R,j}\) and \(\psi_{O,j}\)
denote the raw RCT and OBS vector contributions to \(\widehat S_j\), including
their signs and \(\omega\)-multipliers. Define
\begin{align*}
\xi_{R,j}&\triangleq G^{-1/2}\{\psi_{R,j}-\mathbb E_R\psi_{R,j}\},
&
\xi_{O,j}&\triangleq G^{-1/2}\{\psi_{O,j}-\mathbb E_O\psi_{O,j}\}.
\end{align*}
The source means obey the exact cancellation
\(\mathbb E_R\psi_{R,j}+\mathbb E_O\psi_{O,j}=0\), equivalently
\(\mathbb E(\widehat S_j)=0\).
Suppose every one-dimensional projection of these centered whitened scores is
sub-Gaussian with norm at most \(\sigma_{R,j}\) and \(\sigma_{O,j}\),
respectively. On the Gram
event
\begin{align*}
\mathcal E_H&\triangleq
\{\|G^{-1/2}(\widehat H_j-G)G^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\eta\},
\qquad 0\leq\eta<1,
\end{align*}
the following holds with probability at least
\(1-\delta_H-\delta_S\), where \(\Pr(\mathcal E_H^c)\leq\delta_H\):
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_R(\widehat t_j-\tau)^2
&\leq a_p^2+
\frac{2C_{\mathrm{sg}}^2}{(1-\eta)^2}
\left[\sigma_{R,j}\sqrt{\frac{p+\log(4/\delta_S)}{n_f}}
+\sigma_{O,j}\sqrt{\frac{p+\log(4/\delta_S)}{N_f}}\right]^2
\nonumber\\
&\quad+\frac{2\rho^2}{(1-\eta)^2}
\|G^{-1/2}P\beta_p\|_2^2.
\label{eq:finite-sieve-bound}
\end{align}
Here \(C_{\mathrm{sg}}>0\) is a universal constant from Euclidean-norm
concentration for means of independent sub-Gaussian vectors.
The order \(a_p^2+O_p(p/n_f+p/N_f)\) additionally requires bounded score
envelopes, a Gram event bounded away from singularity with probability tending
to one, and ridge bias no larger than that order.
\end{theorem}

With an honest estimated ratio, the conditional score and Hessian drifts are
\begin{align}
d_{r,j}&\triangleq\omega\mathbb E_O[(\widehat r-r_0)b(g-t_p)],
\label{eq:cate-ratio-score-drift}\\
H_{r,j}&\triangleq\omega\mathbb E_O[(\widehat r-r_0)bb^\top].
\label{eq:cate-ratio-hessian-drift}
\end{align}
Normalization alone controls neither object.

\begin{corollary}[Operational additive-spline rate]
\label{cor:cate-spline-rate}
Suppose \(\tau(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^d\tau_\ell(x_\ell)\), with fixed \(d\) and
smoothness \(s>0\). Consider a deterministic \(K_n\), or a prespecified finite
grid selected by Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-finite-validation}. Let
\(p_n\asymp K_n\), \(a_{p_n}^2=O(K_n^{-2s})\), and
\(\delta_n\downarrow0\). Assume exact \(r_0\), known \(e_0\), and uniformly
along the sequence: (i) the eigenvalues of \(G_{p_n}\) are bounded above and
away from zero; (ii) the centered whitened scores and weighted designs are
sub-Gaussian with bounded norms, while \(r_0\) and \(\kappa\) are nonnegative
and bounded; (iii)
\(K_n+\log(1/\delta_n)=o\{\min(n_f,N_f)\}\); and (iv)
\begin{align*}
\rho_n^2\|G_{p_n}^{-1/2}P\beta_{p_n}\|_2^2
&=O\left(K_n^{-2s}+\frac{K_n}{n_f}+\frac{K_n}{N_f}\right).
\end{align*}
If a finite grid is used, also require the validation remainder in
Theorem~\ref{thm:cate-finite-validation} to be no larger than this target rate.
Then
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_R(\widehat t_j-\tau)^2
&=O_p\left(K_n^{-2s}+\frac{K_n}{n_f}+\frac{K_n}{N_f}\right).
\label{eq:cate-spline-rate}
\end{align}
With \(m_{\mathrm{eff}}\triangleq(1/n_f+1/N_f)^{-1}\) and
\(K_n\asymp m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/(2s+1)}\), this becomes
\(O_p\{m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-2s/(2s+1)}\}\), provided the same conditions hold.
If \(N_f\geq c n_f\), the RCT size determines the generic exponent.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Implementation boundaries and open theory}
\label{sec:cate-scope}

A fixed or honestly trained frozen neural representation can replace \(b\),
conditional on that representation. End-to-end neural fitting is nonconvex and
is not covered. Estimated-ratio guarantees require uniform score, Hessian, and
validation-loss control. Cross-fitting can recover data efficiency, but
overlapping outer training sets do not justify a naive sum of fold variances.
Neither target preservation nor candidate inclusion gives universal
finite-sample improvement for estimated \((\lambda,\omega)\).
