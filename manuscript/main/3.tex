\section{Prediction-Powered Fusion for the ATE}
\label{sec:ate}

\subsection{Estimator and exact variance}

For fixed \((\lambda,\omega)\), define
\begin{align}
\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)
&=
\mathbb P_{R,n}Z_\lambda
+
\omega\left\{
\mathbb P_{O,N}\{r(X)g(X)\}
-
\mathbb P_{R,n}g(X)
\right\}.
\label{eq:ate-estimator}
\end{align}
The first term averages an RCT-valid pseudo-outcome. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:exact-transport}, the expression in braces is a mean-zero control variate; under Assumption~\ref{ass:common-marginal}, this is the special case \(r=1\). Thus, the RCT identifies the target, \(\lambda\) changes within-covariate score noise, and \(\omega\) uses the large OBS covariate sample to remove predictable between-covariate variation.

\begin{theorem}[Exact conditional mean and variance]
\label{thm:ate-variance}
\label{thm:ate-exact-r}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:trial}, \ref{ass:exact-transport}, and \ref{ass:honest-evaluation}, for positive \(n,N\) and square-integrable displayed scores, every fixed \((\lambda,\omega)\) satisfies
\begin{align}
\mathbb E\{\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}
&=\theta,
\\
\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}
&=
\frac1n\operatorname{Var}_R\{Z_\lambda-\omega g(X)\}
+
\frac{\omega^2}{N}\operatorname{Var}_O\{r(X)g(X)\}.
\label{eq:ate-variance}
\end{align}
Equivalently, the law of total variance gives the within-/between-covariate decomposition
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}
&=
\frac1n\mathbb E_R\{\operatorname{Var}_R(Z_\lambda\mid X)\}
+\frac1n\operatorname{Var}_R\{\tau(X)-\omega g(X)\}
+\frac{\omega^2}{N}\operatorname{Var}_O\{r(X)g(X)\}.
\label{eq:ate-within-between}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

The mean follows from \(\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda)=\theta\) and \(\mathbb E_O(rg)=\mathbb E_R(g)\). The variance follows after rewriting Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-estimator} as the sum of independent RCT and OBS empirical means.

\begin{corollary}[Common-marginal specialization]
\label{cor:ate-common-active}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:trial}, \ref{ass:common-marginal}, and \ref{ass:honest-evaluation}, set \(r=1\) in Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-variance}. Then Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-estimator} is exactly conditionally unbiased and its OBS variance term is \(\omega^2\operatorname{Var}_R\{g(X)\}/N\).
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Joint and population-orthogonal oracles}

Throughout this subsection, all population moments are conditional on \(\mathcal F\), the sample sizes \(n\) and \(N\) are positive, and the displayed random variables have finite second moments.
Recall that \(\mathcal F\) is the sigma-field generated before final evaluation by the prespecified split assignments, the fitted nuisance and ratio functions, and the coefficient choice; conditional on \(\mathcal F\), all of these objects are fixed while the final RCT and OBS observations retain their evaluation product law.

Define the five conditional population moments
\begin{align}
A&=\operatorname{Var}_R(\Delta),
&
B&=\operatorname{Var}_R(g)+\frac nN\operatorname{Var}_O(rg),
\\
C&=\operatorname{Cov}_R(Z_0,\Delta),
&
D&=\operatorname{Cov}_R(Z_0,g),
&
E&=\operatorname{Cov}_R(\Delta,g).
\label{eq:ate-moments}
\end{align}
With
\begin{align}
\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}
&=
\begin{pmatrix}A&-E\\-E&B\end{pmatrix},
&
\boldsymbol\ell
&=
\begin{pmatrix}C\\-D\end{pmatrix},
&
\beta
&=
\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\\\omega\end{pmatrix},
\end{align}
the compact variance in Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-variance} becomes
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\beta)\mid\mathcal F\}
&=
\frac1n\left\{
\operatorname{Var}_R(Z_0)+2\boldsymbol\ell^\top\beta+\beta^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\beta
\right\}.
\label{eq:ate-quadratic}
\end{align}
The coefficient vector \(\beta=(\lambda,\omega)^\top\) collects the two fusion choices. The vector \(\boldsymbol\ell=(C,-D)^\top\) is the linear slope of the variance objective at the RCT-only origin \(\beta=0\), while \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) records its quadratic curvature.

The matrix \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is positive semidefinite because
\begin{align}
\begin{pmatrix}u&v\end{pmatrix}
\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}
\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix}
&=
\operatorname{Var}_R(u\Delta-vg)
+\frac nN v^2\operatorname{Var}_O(rg)
\geq0.
\end{align}
Positive semidefiniteness means that the variance objective cannot curve downward along any direction \((u,v)\). For this two-dimensional matrix, the stronger condition \(AB-E^2>0\), together with positive semidefiniteness, makes \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) positive definite and invertible. It is therefore the condition that turns a possibly flat quadratic into one with a unique unrestricted minimizer.

\begin{theorem}[Joint population oracle]
\label{thm:joint-oracle}
If \(AB-E^2>0\), the unique unrestricted minimizer of Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-quadratic} is
\begin{align}
\lambda_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star
&=\frac{ED-BC}{AB-E^2},
&
\omega_{\mathrm{joint}}^\star
&=\frac{AD-EC}{AB-E^2}.
\label{eq:joint-oracle}
\end{align}
If \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is singular, the minimizer set is \(-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^+\boldsymbol\ell+\operatorname{Null}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})\). If \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is positive definite and the feasible set is nonempty, closed, and convex, the constrained minimizer is unique and equals the \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\)-metric projection of the unrestricted minimizer. For a positive-semidefinite \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) and a prespecified compact feasible set, a constrained minimizer exists but need not be unique.
\end{theorem}

The theorem records the two-dimensional quadratic solution before using the model-implied restriction \(E=0\). Under the exact RCT-valid pseudo-outcome model, the population solution therefore specializes to the axis formulas below; retaining a nonzero interaction remains relevant for the realized empirical tuning quadratic.

Here \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is singular when \(\det(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})=AB-E^2=0\), in which case the ordinary inverse \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\) does not exist. Its null space is \(\operatorname{Null}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})=\{v\in\mathbb R^2:\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}v=0\}\), and \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^+\) denotes the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse.

The unrestricted singular formula is well-defined because a variance quadratic is bounded below. This boundedness forces the compatibility condition \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\operatorname{Range}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})\). The term \(-\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^+\boldsymbol\ell\) is one minimum-norm minimizer, and adding any vector in \(\operatorname{Null}(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}})\) moves along a direction in which the variance is flat. A nonempty prespecified compact feasible set guarantees that the continuous quadratic attains a constrained minimum even when this minimum is not unique.
A prespecified compact feasible set is a nonempty, closed, and bounded subset of \(\mathbb R^2\) fixed before viewing the tuning outcomes; the implementation below uses the rectangular coefficient box \(\mathcal B_\beta=[L_\lambda,U_\lambda]\times[L_\omega,U_\omega]\).

\begin{corollary}[Joint-oracle variance reduction]
\label{cor:joint-oracle-variance}
Let \(\sigma_0^2=\operatorname{Var}_R(Z_0)\) and \(\mathcal V_0=\sigma_0^2/n\) be the conditional variance at \((\lambda,\omega)=(0,0)\). If \(AB-E^2>0\), the unrestricted joint oracle has
\begin{align*}
V_{\min}
&=
\mathcal V_0-
\frac1n
\frac{BC^2-2ECD+AD^2}{AB-E^2},
\\
\mathcal V_0-V_{\min}
&=
\frac1n\boldsymbol\ell^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell.
\end{align*}
The reduction is nonnegative and is strictly positive exactly when \(\boldsymbol\ell\neq0\), equivalently when \((C,D)\neq(0,0)\). Under population orthogonality \(E=0\) and \(A,B>0\), this specializes to
\begin{align*}
V_{\min}
&=
\mathcal V_0-
\frac1n\left(\frac{C^2}{A}+\frac{D^2}{B}\right).
\end{align*}
If \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is singular, bounded-below compatibility gives
\begin{align*}
V_{\min}
&=
\mathcal V_0-\frac1n\boldsymbol\ell^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}^+\boldsymbol\ell,
\end{align*}
with a strict reduction exactly when \(\boldsymbol\ell\neq0\). For a nonempty compact feasible set \(\mathcal B_\beta\) containing the origin, the constrained oracle is weakly no worse than \(\mathcal V_0\); the reduction is strict precisely when some \(\beta\in\mathcal B_\beta\) satisfies \(2\boldsymbol\ell^\top\beta+\beta^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\beta<0\).
\end{corollary}

For \(\rho>0\), a ridge-stabilized population coefficient is
\begin{align}
\beta_\rho^\star
&=-(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}+\rho I_2)^{-1}\boldsymbol\ell.
\end{align}
It minimizes the penalized quadratic
\(2\boldsymbol\ell^\top\beta+\beta^\top\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\beta+\rho\lVert\beta\rVert_2^2\).
This is a penalized stability oracle, not the oracle for the unpenalized variance in
Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-quadratic}.
Here \(I_2\) is the \(2\times2\) identity matrix. Adding \(\rho I_2\) makes every curvature direction strictly positive, so \(\beta_\rho^\star\) is unique even when \(\mathbf H_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) is singular. The coefficient minimizes the variance quadratic plus the penalty \(\rho\lVert\beta\rVert_2^2\); it is therefore a penalized oracle rather than another expression for the unpenalized oracle.

The conditional identity \(\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)=0\) gives the exact population orthogonality
\begin{align}
E
&=\operatorname{Cov}_R\{\Delta,g(X)\}=0.
\label{eq:ate-population-orthogonality}
\end{align}
The identity \(\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)=0\) says that changing the outcome-regression blend through \(\lambda\) changes pseudo-outcome noise but not its conditional CATE target. Since \(g(X)\) is measurable with respect to \(X\), averaging the conditional mean-zero difference against \(g(X)\) yields \(E=0\). Thus the exact population variance has no \(\lambda\omega\) interaction, which motivates separate population-axis optimization.
It also gives \(D=\operatorname{Cov}_R\{\tau(X),g(X)\}\). When \(A,B>0\), the population oracle therefore separates:
\begin{align}
\lambda^\star
&=-\frac CA,
&
\omega^\star
&=\frac DB.
\label{eq:axis-oracle}
\end{align}
If \(A=0\), conditional mean zero forces \(\Delta=0\) almost surely and the \(\lambda\) direction is flat. If \(B=0\), both \(g(X)\) and \(r(X)g(X)\) are constant on their relevant supports, so the prediction-powered correction is samplewise zero and the \(\omega\) direction is flat. Under the common covariate marginal,
\begin{align*}
B
&=\left(1+\frac nN\right)\operatorname{Var}_R\{g(X)\}.
\end{align*}
For the rectangular box \(\mathcal B_\beta\) and positive \(A,B\), population orthogonality gives the coordinatewise projection
\begin{align*}
\lambda_{\mathcal B_\beta}^\star
&=\Pi_{[L_\lambda,U_\lambda]}(-C/A),
&
\omega_{\mathcal B_\beta}^\star
&=\Pi_{[L_\omega,U_\omega]}(D/B).
\end{align*}
For a coupled constraint or a nonzero interaction, the original quadratic must be minimized directly. The population oracle weakly improves the variance objective whenever \((0,0)\) is feasible. Estimated coefficients have no universal finite-sample no-harm guarantee.

\subsection{Estimable honest algorithm}

More explicitly, let the tuning-block sizes be \(m_R,m_O\geq2\). For \(S\in\{R,O\}\) and \(a\in\{1,2\}\), define
\begin{align*}
\overline h_{a,S,\mathrm{tu}}
&=
\frac1{m_S}\sum_{k=1}^{m_S}h_a(V_{S,k}),
\\
s_{S,\mathrm{tu}}(h_1,h_2)
&=
\frac1{m_S-1}
\sum_{k=1}^{m_S}
\{h_1(V_{S,k})-\overline h_{1,S,\mathrm{tu}}\}
\{h_2(V_{S,k})-\overline h_{2,S,\mathrm{tu}}\}.
\end{align*}
The factor \(n/N\) below refers to the final evaluation sizes whose variance is being optimized.

Let \(s_R(h_1,h_2)\) and \(s_O(h_1,h_2)\) denote unbiased sample covariances on independent tuning blocks, with \(s_S^2(h)=s_S(h,h)\). Compute
\begin{align}
\widehat A&=s_R^2(\Delta),
&
\widehat B&=s_R^2(g)+\frac nN s_O^2(rg),
\\
\widehat C&=s_R(Z_0,\Delta),
&
\widehat D&=s_R(Z_0,g),
&
\widehat E&=s_R(\Delta,g).
\end{align}
Define
\begin{align*}
\widehat{\mathbf H}_{\mathrm{ATE}}
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
\widehat A&-\widehat E\\
-\widehat E&\widehat B
\end{pmatrix},
&
\widehat{\boldsymbol\ell}
&=
\begin{pmatrix}\widehat C\\-\widehat D\end{pmatrix},
&
\widehat d
&=
\widehat A\widehat B-\widehat E^2.
\end{align*}
Choose positive thresholds \(t_A,t_B,t_{\det}\) before observing the tuning outcomes. The population-orthogonality axis rule is
\begin{align*}
\widehat\lambda_{\mathrm{axis}}
&=
\begin{cases}
-\widehat C/\widehat A,&\widehat A>t_A,\\
0,&\widehat A\leq t_A,
\end{cases}
&
\widehat\omega_{\mathrm{axis}}
&=
\begin{cases}
\widehat D/\widehat B,&\widehat B>t_B,\\
0,&\widehat B\leq t_B.
\end{cases}
\end{align*}
When \(\widehat A>t_A\), \(\widehat B>t_B\), and \(\widehat d>t_{\det}\), the unrestricted empirical joint rule is
\begin{align*}
\widehat\lambda_{\mathrm{joint}}
&=
\frac{\widehat E\widehat D-\widehat B\widehat C}{\widehat d},
&
\widehat\omega_{\mathrm{joint}}
&=
\frac{\widehat A\widehat D-\widehat E\widehat C}{\widehat d}.
\end{align*}
For the prespecified nonempty compact box \(\mathcal B_\beta=[L_\lambda,U_\lambda]\times[L_\omega,U_\omega]\) containing the origin, the boundary-correct empirical choice is
\begin{align*}
\widehat\beta_{\mathcal B_\beta}
&\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\beta\in\mathcal B_\beta}
\left\{
2\widehat{\boldsymbol\ell}^\top\beta
+\beta^\top\widehat{\mathbf H}_{\mathrm{ATE}}\beta
\right\}.
\end{align*}
If the unrestricted joint rule lies outside \(\mathcal B_\beta\), this quadratic program must be solved rather than clipping its coordinates separately; rectangular coordinate-wise clipping is exact only after imposing \(\widehat E=0\). For the joint rule, if a required threshold fails or the quadratic solver is numerically unstable, the prespecified fallback is \((0,0)\); the axis rule uses its coordinate-specific threshold definitions above.

The population-orthogonality plug-in imposes \(\widehat E=0\) and uses \((-\widehat C/\widehat A,\widehat D/\widehat B)\). The empirical joint minimizer retains \(\widehat E\) and applies Equation~\eqref{eq:joint-oracle}. The two rules differ because sample orthogonality is not exact.

Each sample covariance is unbiased for its corresponding fixed-score population moment, but the ratios defining \(\widehat\lambda\) and \(\widehat\omega\) are nonlinear and are not generally unbiased for the population oracle coefficients. The thresholds and feasible box control instability; they do not turn these selected coefficients into finite-sample oracle estimates.

The displayed analytic ratios apply only when their corresponding denominator thresholds pass. Imposing \(\widehat E=0\) does not assert that the realized tuning covariance vanishes. It applies the proven population restriction \(E=0\) before solving the sample problem. Retaining \(\widehat E=s_R(\Delta,g)\) instead minimizes the realized empirical quadratic, whose interaction term is generally nonzero because of tuning-sample noise.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:honest-ate} states the exact three-way implementation.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Three-way honest ATE estimation}
\label{alg:honest-ate}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{Independent RCT and OBS samples; a prespecified feasible box and denominator thresholds.}
\textbf{Split:} Divide each source into nuisance-training, coefficient-tuning, and final-evaluation blocks.\;
\textbf{Fit:} Estimate \(\widehat\mu_R\), \(\widehat\mu_O\), \(g\), and, if required, a transport model without using any tuning or evaluation observations. The exact theorem uses the true ratio \(r\); replacing it by \(\widehat r\) defines the approximate regime described below.\;
\textbf{Tune:} On the independent tuning blocks, compute the five moments, apply the prespecified denominator thresholds, and solve the box-constrained quadratic. Use \((0,0)\) as the declared fallback when the moments are unstable.\;
\textbf{Evaluate:} Hold every fitted object fixed and evaluate Equation~\eqref{eq:ate-estimator} on the untouched final blocks.\;
\textbf{Infer:} Set \(\psi_R=Z_{\widehat\lambda}-\widehat\omega g\) and \(\psi_O=\widehat\omega rg\), and report
\(\widehat V=s_R^2(\psi_R)/n+s_O^2(\psi_O)/N\). Under conditional Lindeberg conditions, bounded conditional \((2+\delta)\) moments, and a nondegenerate variance limit, use
\(\widehat\theta_r\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat V}\).\;
\KwOut{\(\widehat\theta_r\), \(\widehat V\), and the corresponding Wald interval.}
\end{algorithm}

For explicit evaluation notation, define
\begin{align*}
\psi_{R,i}
&=
Z_{\widehat\lambda}(V_i^R)-\widehat\omega g(X_i^R),
&
\psi_{O,j}
&=
\widehat\omega r(X_j^O)g(X_j^O),
\end{align*}
and let \(s_{R,\mathrm{ev}}^2\) and \(s_{O,\mathrm{ev}}^2\) be the usual sample variances with denominators \(n-1\) and \(N-1\), respectively. For \(n,N\geq2\), the fixed-selected-coefficient variance estimator and Wald interval are
\begin{align*}
\widehat V
&=
\frac{s_{R,\mathrm{ev}}^2(\psi_R)}{n}
+
\frac{s_{O,\mathrm{ev}}^2(\psi_O)}{N},
\\
\widehat\theta_r
&\mathbin{\pm}
z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat V}.
\end{align*}
Conditional unbiasedness of \(\widehat V\) follows from evaluation-block independence. Wald coverage additionally requires the stated conditional Lindeberg, moment, and nondegeneracy conditions; it is not a finite-sample coverage statement.
Here \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) is the \((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile of the standard normal distribution; for \(\alpha=0.05\), it is approximately \(1.96\).

\begin{proposition}[Exact variance estimation after honest selection]
\label{prop:ate-honest-variance-active}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:ate-variance}, let \((\widehat\lambda,\widehat\omega)\) be \(\mathcal F\)-measurable and suppose \(n,N\geq2\). Conditional on \(\mathcal F\), the selected estimator is unbiased and
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E(\widehat V\mid\mathcal F)
&=
\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\widehat\lambda,\widehat\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional central limit theorem and Wald inference]
\label{thm:ate-honest-clt-active}
In addition to Proposition~\ref{prop:ate-honest-variance-active}, suppose \(n,N\to\infty\), \(n/N\to\kappa\in[0,\infty)\), and for some \(\delta>0\) the centered RCT and OBS final-score arrays have uniformly bounded conditional \((2+\delta)\) moments. This moment assumption supplies the required conditional Lindeberg condition. Assume also that
\begin{align*}
n\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\widehat\lambda,\widehat\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}
&\to\sigma^2\in(0,\infty)
\end{align*}
in probability. Then
\begin{align*}
\frac{\widehat\theta_r-\theta}{\sqrt{\widehat V}}
&\rightsquigarrow N(0,1),
\end{align*}
and the displayed Wald interval has asymptotic coverage \(1-\alpha\). Variance unbiasedness alone does not imply this coverage statement.
\end{theorem}

With an estimated ratio \(\widehat r\), define
\begin{align}
h_n(X)
&=\{\widehat r(X)-r(X)\}g(X).
\end{align}
The exact conditional drift is
\begin{align}
\omega b_{\mathcal F}
&=
\omega\mathbb E_O\{h_n(X)\}.
\end{align}
Honest splitting prevents evaluation leakage but does not remove this drift. A direct
sufficient negligible-error regime is
\begin{align}
\sqrt n\,\omega\,\mathbb E_O\{h_n(X)\}
&=o_p(1),
&
|\omega|\sqrt{\frac nN}
\left\|h_n-\mathbb E_O(h_n)\right\|_{L^2(P_O)}
&=o_p(1).
\end{align}
Evaluation-only variance is first-order adequate under these conditions and the
preceding CLT assumptions. Otherwise the ratio learner's influence-function
contribution, the OBS empirical-process term, and their covariances must be included.

\paragraph{Estimating the density ratio.}
All three constructions below use only a ratio-training block and are held fixed before coefficient tuning and final evaluation. Let \(\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{rat}}\) and \(\mathbb P_{O,\mathrm{rat}}\) denote empirical averages on its RCT and OBS parts. First, pool the ratio-training covariates and let \(S=1\) indicate an RCT observation and \(S=0\) an OBS observation. With \(\pi=P(S=1)\) and \(s(x)=P(S=1\mid X=x)\), Bayes' rule gives
\begin{align*}
r(x)
&=
\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}\frac{s(x)}{1-s(x)},
&
\widehat r_{\mathrm{cls}}(x)
&=
\frac{1-\widehat\pi}{\widehat\pi}
\frac{\widehat s(x)}{1-\widehat s(x)}.
\end{align*}
For an unweighted pooled classifier with ratio-training sizes \(m_R^{\mathrm{rat}}\) and \(m_O^{\mathrm{rat}}\), \(\widehat\pi=m_R^{\mathrm{rat}}/(m_R^{\mathrm{rat}}+m_O^{\mathrm{rat}})\). Class weighting or resampling requires replacing this factor by the effective training prior. The odds representation also requires overlap, \(0<s(X)<1\), on the RCT covariate support. Probability clipping stabilizes the estimate but changes it from the exact density ratio.

A Riesz construction estimates the transport functional directly \citep{chernozhukov2021riesz,chernozhukov2022autodml}. Let \(\phi:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^p\) be a fixed feature map and define
\begin{align*}
\widehat M_O
&=
\mathbb P_{O,\mathrm{rat}}\{\phi(X)\phi(X)^\top\},
&
\widehat b_R
&=
\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{rat}}\{\phi(X)\}.
\end{align*}
For \(\rho>0\), the empirical Riesz quadratic and its closed-form solution are
\begin{align*}
\widehat\eta_\rho
&=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\eta\in\mathbb R^p}
\left[
\eta^\top\widehat M_O\eta
-2\widehat b_R^\top\eta
+\rho\lVert\eta\rVert_2^2
\right]
=
(\widehat M_O+\rho I_p)^{-1}\widehat b_R,
\\
\widehat r_{\mathrm{Riesz}}(x)
&=
\phi(x)^\top\widehat\eta_\rho.
\end{align*}
At \(\rho=0\), a compatible singular system uses \(\widehat M_O^+\widehat b_R\). A restricted feature map estimates a projected representer, and the resulting weights need not be nonnegative.

A nonnegative automatic-balancing alternative starts with an out-of-sample-evaluable family \(w_\eta:\mathcal X\to[0,\infty)\). For a fixed feature map \(\phi\), a positive semidefinite matrix \(W\), and \(\gamma>0\), define \(\lVert v\rVert_W^2=v^\top Wv\) and solve
\begin{align*}
\widehat\eta_{\mathrm{bal}}
&\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\eta:\,
\mathbb P_{O,\mathrm{rat}}w_\eta=1}
\left[
\left\|
\mathbb P_{O,\mathrm{rat}}\{w_\eta(X)\phi(X)\}
-
\mathbb P_{R,\mathrm{rat}}\{\phi(X)\}
\right\|_W^2
+
\gamma\mathbb P_{O,\mathrm{rat}}\{w_\eta(X)^2\}
\right],
\\
\widehat r_{\mathrm{bal}}(x)
&=
w_{\widehat\eta_{\mathrm{bal}}}(x).
\end{align*}
Including \(g\) among the coordinates of \(\phi\) directly targets the transported moment used by the ATE correction. The dispersion penalty controls unstable weights but generally prevents exact empirical balance.

These constructions do not automatically inherit the exact-r theorem. For any fixed evaluation-independent weight function \(w\), replacing \(r\) by \(w\) gives
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E\{\widehat\theta_w(\lambda,\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}
&=
\theta
+
\omega\{P_O(wg)-P_R(g)\}
\\
&=
\theta
+
\omega P_O\{(w-r)g\}.
\end{align*}
Thus exact conditional unbiasedness holds for \(w=r\), or more generally for a fixed \(w\) satisfying the exact population balance condition \(P_O(wg)=P_R(g)\). Classifier, restricted Riesz, and automatic-balancing estimates are otherwise in the estimated-ratio regime governed by the drift and rate conditions above. The Auto-DML citation motivates the Riesz learning criterion; it does not by itself make the present score Neyman-orthogonal to ratio error or supply the ratio learner's first-order variance contribution.

\paragraph{Nested outer cross-fitting.}
To reuse observations without scoring any observation with a nuisance fit trained on its own outcome, prespecify paired outer folds in both sources, or randomize the folds independently and include that randomness in the conditioning field. Within each outer complement, use inner splits to fit nuisances and select \((\widehat\lambda_k,\widehat\omega_k)\); then score only the untouched paired folds. With fixed nonnegative weights \(a_k\) summing to one, define
\begin{align*}
\widehat\theta_k
&=
\mathbb P_{R,k}Z_{\widehat\lambda_k}^{(-k)}
+\widehat\omega_k
\left\{
\mathbb P_{O,k}(r g^{(-k)})
-\mathbb P_{R,k}g^{(-k)}
\right\},
&
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{outer}}
&=\sum_k a_k\widehat\theta_k.
\end{align*}

\begin{proposition}[What outer-fold honesty proves]
\label{prop:ate-outer-unbiased-active}
Under the common covariate marginal or exact \(r\), each \(\widehat\theta_k\) is conditionally unbiased given its own outer complement, and \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{outer}}\) is unconditionally unbiased. Overlapping outer training complements make the fold estimators dependent, so these facts do not justify adding foldwise variances or using a naive pooled Wald interval.
\end{proposition}

A pooled cross-fitted interval requires an additional stability or influence-function argument, for example convergence of fold-specific RCT and OBS scores to common deterministic limits in their respective \(L_2\) norms, uniform \((2+\delta)\) moments, and a nondegenerate two-sample limit. The present theory does not assert a general cross-fitted variance theorem without such conditions.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.}
An implementation should report split assignments, propensity bounds, candidate boxes, all denominator and determinant thresholds, coefficient fallbacks, ratio overlap and clipping, and the selected RCT-only comparator. Near-zero \(\widehat A\), \(\widehat B\), or \(\widehat d\) must trigger the prespecified rule rather than an unstable analytic ratio.
