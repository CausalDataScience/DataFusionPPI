\section{Background and Problem Setting}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Prediction-powered inference and PPI++}
\label{sec:ppi-background}

Let \((X_i,Y_i)\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\), be labeled pairs and let \(\widetilde X_j\), \(j=1,\ldots,N\), be unlabeled covariates. Suppose a fixed predictor supplies \(f(X_i)\) and \(f(\widetilde X_j)\). For \(\omega\in\mathbb R\), the PPI++ estimator \citep{angelopoulos2024ppipp} of the labeled-population mean is
\begin{align}
\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{PPI++}}(\omega)
&\triangleq
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nY_i
+\omega\left\{
\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N f(\widetilde X_j)
-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n f(X_i)
\right\}.
\end{align}
When labeled and unlabeled covariates share a marginal law and the two samples are independent, the expression in braces has mean zero for every fixed predictor \(f\). PPI++ selects \(\omega\) to control how strongly this mean-zero prediction difference enters the estimator. Our construction retains this control-variate role and introduces a separate outcome-regression blending coefficient only after defining the RCT-valid score.

\subsection{RCT--OBS problem setting and notation}
\label{sec:rct-obs-setting}

Let \(\mathcal K\triangleq\{\mathrm{eval},\mathrm{nuis},\mathrm{tune}\}\). For
each source \(S\in\{R,O\}\) and role \(k\in\mathcal K\), observe
\begin{align}
D_S^k
&\triangleq
\{V_{S,i}^k=(X_{S,i}^k,A_{S,i}^k,Y_{S,i}^k):i=1,\ldots,n_S^k\}
\sim P_S^{\otimes n_S^k}.
\label{eq:role-indexed-samples}
\end{align}
Here \(X\) takes values in the covariate space \(\mathcal X\),
\(A\in\{0,1\}\) is treatment, and \(Y\) is the observed outcome. For each
\(S\in\{R,O\}\), the datasets \(D_S^{\mathrm{eval}}\),
\(D_S^{\mathrm{nuis}}\), and \(D_S^{\mathrm{tune}}\) are used for final
evaluation, nuisance and density-ratio fitting, and coefficient or candidate
tuning, respectively. Set \(n\triangleq n_R^{\mathrm{eval}}\) and
\(N\triangleq n_O^{\mathrm{eval}}\), and define
\(\mathbb P_{R,n}h\triangleq n^{-1}\sum_i h(V_{R,i}^{\mathrm{eval}})\) and
\(\mathbb P_{O,N}h\triangleq N^{-1}\sum_j h(V_{O,j}^{\mathrm{eval}})\).
Every displayed moment is finite; square-integrability and stronger moment
conditions are stated locally when variance estimation or normal-approximation inference
requires them.

Let \(Y(1)\) and \(Y(0)\) denote potential outcomes. The RCT-population conditional and average treatment effects are
\begin{align}
\tau(x)
&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x\},
&
\theta
&\triangleq\mathbb E_R\{\tau(X)\}.
\end{align}

\begin{assumption}[Trial identification]
\label{ass:trial}
Consistency holds, \(\{Y(0),Y(1)\}\perp A\mid X\) under \(P_R\), and the known trial propensity \(e(x)\triangleq P_R(A=1\mid X=x)\) satisfies \(\epsilon<e(X)<1-\epsilon\) almost surely for a constant \(\epsilon>0\).
\end{assumption}

We study both the common-covariate regime \(P_R^X=P_O^X\), formalized in Assumption~\ref{ass:common-marginal}, and the more general covariate-shift regime \(P_R^X\neq P_O^X\), formalized in Assumption~\ref{ass:exact-transport}.

\begin{definition}[Target-to-source density ratio]
\label{def:true-density-ratio}
Whenever \(P_R^X\ll P_O^X\), define the target-to-source density ratio by
\begin{align*}
r_0(x)
&\triangleq
\frac{dP_R^X}{dP_O^X}(x).
\end{align*}
It is the Radon--Nikodym derivative that transports an OBS covariate expectation
to the RCT covariate population.
\end{definition}

\enlargethispage{\baselineskip}
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\begin{assumption}[Common covariate marginal]
\label{ass:common-marginal}
\label{ass:covariate}
The trial and observational covariates have the same marginal law, \(P_R^X=P_O^X\). This assumption does not equate the treatment or outcome mechanisms across sources, and the observational treatment contrast may remain confounded.
In this regime, Definition~\ref{def:true-density-ratio} gives
\(r_0(x)=1\) for \(P_O^X\)-almost every \(x\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Covariate shift and support overlap]
\label{ass:exact-transport}
Under covariate shift, \(P_R^X\ll P_O^X\), so the RCT covariate population has
no support outside the OBS covariate population. Definition~\ref{def:true-density-ratio}
therefore defines \(r_0\). This assumption establishes existence of the ratio;
it does not assert that \(r_0\) is known or exactly estimated.
\end{assumption}

Assumptions~\ref{ass:common-marginal} and \ref{ass:exact-transport} describe alternative covariate regimes, not cumulative restrictions. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:common-marginal}, no transport weighting is needed and we set \(r\triangleq1\). When the covariate marginals differ, Assumption~\ref{ass:exact-transport} is needed to convert OBS covariate expectations into expectations under the RCT population. Neither regime assumes that the OBS treatment contrast is causal.
Exact-oracle results below use \(r\triangleq r_0\). Feasible results may replace
\(r_0\) by an evaluation-independent estimate \(\widehat r\) under the
conditions in Section~\ref{sec:ratio-estimation}.

\begin{assumption}[Honest sample separation]
\label{ass:honest-separation}
The nuisance samples \(D_R^{\mathrm{nuis}},D_O^{\mathrm{nuis}}\), the tuning
samples \(D_R^{\mathrm{tune}},D_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\), and the evaluation samples
\(D_R^{\mathrm{eval}},D_O^{\mathrm{eval}}\) are mutually independent. Nuisance
and density-ratio estimators use only
\(D_R^{\mathrm{nuis}},D_O^{\mathrm{nuis}}\). Coefficients and candidate rules
use only \(D_R^{\mathrm{tune}},D_O^{\mathrm{tune}}\) after the nuisance objects
are fixed. If candidate fitting and validation both use tuning data, their
assignments are prespecified and disjoint. Final point estimation, variance
estimation, and normal-approximation inference use only
\(D_R^{\mathrm{eval}},D_O^{\mathrm{eval}}\), after every learned object and
split assignment has been fixed.
\end{assumption}

Throughout, every \(P_R\)- or \(P_O\)-expectation, covariance, variance, and
exact variance result that involves a learned object is conditional on the
realized nuisance and tuning datasets, split assignments, and pre-evaluation
algorithmic randomness. These objects are held fixed, and only the evaluation
observations remain random. We suppress this conditioning in the notation.

For \(S\in\{R,O\}\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define the population outcome-regression target \(\mu_{S,a}:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\) by
\begin{align*}
\mu_{S,a}(x)
&\triangleq
\mathbb E_S[Y\mid X=x,A=a].
\end{align*}
For each \(S\in\{R,O\}\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\), let
\(\widehat\mu_{S,a}:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\) denote a
fixed pre-evaluation predictor of the corresponding
population regression \(\mu_{S,a}\).  The RCT predictor is trained only on
\(D_R^{\mathrm{nuis}}\), and the OBS predictor is trained only on
\(D_O^{\mathrm{nuis}}\). No correctness assumption is imposed on either fitted
predictor. Define
\begin{align*}
\widehat\mu_R
&\triangleq
(\widehat\mu_{R,0},\widehat\mu_{R,1}),
&
\widehat\mu_O
&\triangleq
(\widehat\mu_{O,0},\widehat\mu_{O,1}).
\end{align*}
Then define the observational treatment-effect prediction
\begin{align}
g(x)
&\triangleq\widehat\mu_{O,1}(x)-\widehat\mu_{O,0}(x),
\label{eq:obs-effect-prediction}
\end{align}

\begin{definition}[RCT-valid AIPW score]
\label{def:rct-aipw-score}
Let \(q\triangleq(q_0,q_1)\) be a fixed pair of measurable functions from
\(\mathcal X\) to \(\mathbb R\). Define
\begin{align}
\varphi(V;q)
&\triangleq q_1(X)-q_0(X)
+\frac{A}{e(X)}\{Y-q_1(X)\}
-\frac{1-A}{1-e(X)}\{Y-q_0(X)\}.
\label{eq:aipw-score}
\end{align}
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}[RCT-validity for an arbitrary outcome regression]
\label{prop:pseudo-validity-active}
\label{prop:pseudo-validity}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:trial} and \ref{ass:honest-separation}, every fixed
\(q\) with finite first moments satisfies
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_R\{\varphi(V;q)\mid X\}
&=\tau(X).
\label{eq:pseudo-valid}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{definition}[Fusion score]
\label{def:fusion-score}
For \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), define
\begin{align}
\widehat\mu_\lambda
&\triangleq(1-\lambda)\widehat\mu_R+\lambda\widehat\mu_O,
\notag\\
Z_\lambda
&\triangleq\varphi(V;\widehat\mu_\lambda)
=Z_0+\lambda\Delta,
\notag\\
\Delta
&\triangleq Z_1-Z_0.
\end{align}
\end{definition}
Equation~\eqref{eq:pseudo-valid} implies
\(\mathbb E_R(Z_\lambda\mid X)=\tau(X)\) and
\(\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X)=0\). The observational contrast \(g\)
is used only as a prediction and need not equal \(\tau\).

\begin{definition}[RCT-only AIPW estimator]
\label{def:rct-only-aipw}
Let \(\mu_R\triangleq(\mu_{R,0},\mu_{R,1})\). Setting \(q=\mu_R\) in
Definition~\ref{def:rct-aipw-score} gives the oracle RCT AIPW score. Its
nuisance-sample fitted counterpart is \(Z_0=\varphi(V;\widehat\mu_R)\). Define
the RCT-only AIPW estimator on the evaluation sample by
\begin{align*}
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{AIPW},R}
&\triangleq
\mathbb P_{R,n}\varphi(V;\widehat\mu_R)
=\mathbb P_{R,n}Z_0.
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[RCT-only AIPW recovery]
\label{cor:rct-only-aipw}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:trial} and \ref{ass:honest-separation},
\(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{AIPW},R}\) is unbiased for \(\theta\) over
\(D_R^{\mathrm{eval}}\).
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Existing data-fusion estimators}
\label{sec:existing-fusion}

Three existing strategies provide the nearest comparisons. First, shrinkage methods combine an unbiased experimental estimate with a potentially biased observational estimate and trade variance against observational bias \citep{rosenman2020shrinkage}. Second, adaptive RCT--OBS estimators estimate source-specific conditional effects and downweight the observational estimator when its discrepancy from the trial is large \citep{cheng2021adaptive}. Third, direct multi-source learners construct weighted pseudo-outcomes for heterogeneous effects across sources \citep{li2022directfusion}. Our estimator differs at the level of the fusion object: \(\lambda\) changes the nuisance regression inside a trial-valid score, while \(\omega\) centers a fixed prediction across study covariate samples. Neither channel treats the observational treatment contrast as identified causal truth.

Within the adaptive and integrative line, the elastic procedure of \citet{yang2023elastic} tests whether real-world data are sufficiently compatible with the randomized trial before pooling information for prespecified effect modifiers. The confounding-function approach of \citet{yang2025confounding} instead introduces and jointly estimates an HTE function and an observational confounding function under explicit cross-source identification conditions. These methods use observational outcomes through a poolability decision or an identified confounding-function model; our two fusion channels retain the RCT-valid score as the causal anchor.
