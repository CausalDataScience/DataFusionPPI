# Prediction-Powered Data Fusion for CATE Estimation

## Setup

We observe two independent set of samples. The randomized controlled trial (RCT) sample is

$$
D_R\triangleq\{V_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i):i=1,\ldots,n\} \sim P_R,
$$

where $P_R$ denotes the sampling distribution for the RCT samples. 

The observational study (OBS) sample is
$$
D_O\triangleq\{V_j=(X_j,A_j,Y_j):j=1,\ldots,N\} \sim P_O,
$$

where $P_O$ denotes the sampling distribution for the OBS samples. Here, $X$ denotes covariates, $A\in\{0,1\}$ denotes treatment, and $Y$ denotes the observed outcome. For any function $h$, define
$$
\mathbb E_n[h(V)]
\triangleq
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n h(V_i),
\qquad
\mathbb E_N[h(V)]
\triangleq
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^N h(V_j).
$$

Let $Y(1)$ and $Y(0)$ denote the potential outcomes under treatment and control. Our target is the conditional average treatment effect (CATE) in the RCT population,

$$
\tau(x)
\triangleq
\mathbb E_R[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x], \quad \text{and} \quad \theta \triangleq \mathbb{E}_R[\tau(X)]. 
$$

We assume the followings: 
$$
\begin{align*}
\text{(Consistency)} &\qquad Y = AY(1) + (1-A)Y(0), \\ 
\text{(Conditional Randomization on RCT)} &\qquad \{Y(0),Y(1)\}\perp A\mid X \quad \text{ on RCT $P_R$}, \\ 
\text{(Known and strictly positive RCT propensity)} &\qquad e(X) \triangleq P_R(A =1 \mid X) \in (\epsilon, 1-\epsilon) \text{ a.s for some } \epsilon > 0, \\ 
\text{Baseline common covariate marginal} &\qquad P_R^X = P_O^X.
\end{align*}
$$

Here, $P_R^X$ and $P_O^X$ denote the covariate marginals of the two studies. The common-marginal condition is the baseline theory. We also consider a covariate-shift extension that replaces it with
$$
P_R^X \ll P_O^X,
\qquad
r(x)
\triangleq
\frac{dP_R^X}{dP_O^X}(x).
$$
Because $r$ is the Radon--Nikodym derivative of the RCT covariate law with respect to the OBS covariate law,
$$
\mathbb E_O[r(X)]=1,
\qquad
\mathbb E_O[r(X)h(X)]=\mathbb E_R[h(X)]
$$
for every integrable function $h$. The baseline condition is the special case $r(X)=1$ almost surely.

Let

$$
\widehat\mu_R
\triangleq
(\widehat\mu_{R,0},\widehat\mu_{R,1}), \quad \text{ where } \quad \widehat{\mu}_{R,a}(X) \triangleq \widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{R}[Y \mid A=a, X], \quad \forall a \in \{0,1\}.
$$

denote outcome models trained on RCT data. 

Let 
$$
\widehat\mu_O
=
(\widehat\mu_{O,0},\widehat\mu_{O,1}), \quad \text{ where }\quad  \widehat{\mu}_{O,a}(X) \triangleq \widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{O}[Y \mid A=a, X], \quad \forall a \in \{0,1\}.
$$

denote outcome models trained on OBS data.

Define the OBS treatment-effect prediction
$$
\widehat g_O(X)
\triangleq
\widehat\mu_{O,1}(X)-\widehat\mu_{O,0}(X).
$$

For any pair of functions $q(X)=(q_0(X),q_1(X))$, define the pseudo-outcome as 

$$
\varphi(V;q)
\triangleq 
q_1(X)-q_0(X)
+
\frac{A}{e(X)}\{Y-q_1(X)\}
-
\frac{1-A}{1-e(X)}\{Y-q_0(X)\}.
$$

For any fixed pair $q$,

$$
\mathbb E_R[\varphi(V;q)\mid X=x]
=
\tau(x) = \mathbb E_R[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x].
$$

Thus, $q$ can be chosen to minimize the conditional variance of the pseudo-outcome while preserving its conditional mean.

We combine the RCT and OBS outcome models through

$$
\widehat\mu_\lambda
=
(1-\lambda)\widehat\mu_R+\lambda\widehat\mu_O.
$$

Define
$$
\widehat{Z}_{\lambda} \triangleq \varphi(V; \widehat{\mu}_{\lambda}); \quad \widehat{Z}_{0} \triangleq \varphi(V; \widehat{\mu}_{R}); \quad \widehat{Z}_{1} \triangleq \varphi(V; \widehat{\mu}_{O}).
$$
Note that 
$$
\widehat Z_\lambda
=
\widehat Z_0
+
\lambda(\widehat Z_1-\widehat Z_0).
$$
The exact finite-sample statements below use honest conditioning. Let $\mathcal F$ be the sigma-field generated by all nuisance-training data and all independent coefficient-tuning data used to fit $\widehat\mu_R$, $\widehat\mu_O$, $\widehat g_O$, a density-ratio model, or $(\widehat\lambda,\widehat\omega)$. Conditional on $\mathcal F$, the RCT and OBS evaluation samples are independent and have laws $P_R^n$ and $P_O^N$, respectively. Every fitted function and selected coefficient is therefore treated as $\mathcal F$-measurable. We suppress this conditioning in the notation. For the variance statements, assume
$$
\widehat Z_0,
\quad
\widehat Z_1-\widehat Z_0,
\quad
\widehat g_O(X)
\in L_2(P_R),
\qquad
r(X)\widehat g_O(X)\in L_2(P_O).
$$

Under the honest conditioning stated above,
$$
\mathbb E_R[\widehat Z_\lambda\mid X=x,\mathcal F]
=
\tau(x),
$$
for every fixed $\lambda$. Therefore, $\lambda$ controls the noise of the RCT-valid pseudo-outcome without changing its CATE target.

Averaging the conditional identity at $\lambda=0$ gives
$$
\mathbb E_R(\widehat Z_0\mid\mathcal F)
=
\theta.
$$
Consequently, the RCT-only AIPW estimator
$$
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{AIPW}}
\triangleq
\mathbb E_n[\widehat Z_0]
$$
is conditionally unbiased:
$$
\mathbb E
\left(
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{AIPW}}
\mid
\mathcal F
\right)
=
\theta.
$$

Ordinary overlapping cross-fitting is a practical approximation to this honest-split analysis, but it does not by itself produce one global conditioning sigma-field under which all foldwise evaluation terms are independent. The exact identities below therefore apply directly to independent evaluation samples and fixed coefficients; additional arguments are required for adaptively tuned or overlapping cross-fitted estimators.

## ATE estimation

The RCT-only AIPW estimator above is the $(\lambda,\omega)=(0,0)$ baseline. To use the large OBS covariate sample under either the baseline common-marginal condition or the density-ratio extension, write
$$
g(X)\triangleq\widehat g_O(X),
\qquad
\Delta\triangleq\widehat Z_1-\widehat Z_0,
\qquad
\widehat Z_\lambda=\widehat Z_0+\lambda\Delta,
$$
and define
$$
\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)
\triangleq
\mathbb E_n[\widehat Z_\lambda]
+
\omega
\left\{
\mathbb E_N[r(X)g(X)]
-
\mathbb E_n[g(X)]
\right\}.
$$
The OBS average estimates the RCT-population mean of the prediction through importance weighting, while the RCT subtraction prevents the prediction from being treated as causal truth. Conditional on $\mathcal F$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\{\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}
&=
\mathbb E_R(\widehat Z_\lambda)
+
\omega
\{\mathbb E_O(rg)-\mathbb E_R(g)\}
\\
&=
\theta
\end{aligned}
$$
for every fixed $(\lambda,\omega)$. Under $P_R^X=P_O^X$, $r=1$ and this estimator reduces to the original prediction-powered difference of unweighted covariate averages.

### Exact conditional variance

The two evaluation samples are conditionally independent given $\mathcal F$. Hence the compact variance formula is
$$
\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}
=
\frac{1}{n}
\operatorname{Var}_R\{\widehat Z_\lambda-\omega g(X)\}
+
\frac{\omega^2}{N}
\operatorname{Var}_O\{r(X)g(X)\}.
$$
Expanding $\widehat Z_\lambda=\widehat Z_0+\lambda\Delta$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}
={}&
\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{Var}_R(\widehat Z_0)
+
\frac{2\lambda}{n}\operatorname{Cov}_R(\widehat Z_0,\Delta)
+
\frac{\lambda^2}{n}\operatorname{Var}_R(\Delta)
\\
&-
\frac{2\omega}{n}\operatorname{Cov}_R(\widehat Z_0,g)
-
\frac{2\lambda\omega}{n}\operatorname{Cov}_R(\Delta,g)
+
\frac{\omega^2}{n}\operatorname{Var}_R(g)
\\
&+
\frac{\omega^2}{N}\operatorname{Var}_O(rg).
\end{aligned}
$$

Before imposing the RCT orthogonality, define the five conditional population moments
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&\triangleq\operatorname{Var}_R(\Delta),
&
B&\triangleq
\operatorname{Var}_R(g)
+
\frac{n}{N}\operatorname{Var}_O(rg),
\\
C&\triangleq\operatorname{Cov}_R(\widehat Z_0,\Delta),
&
D&\triangleq\operatorname{Cov}_R(\widehat Z_0,g),
\\
E&\triangleq\operatorname{Cov}_R(\Delta,g).
\end{aligned}
$$
For $\beta=(\lambda,\omega)^\top$, set
$$
\mathbf H
\triangleq
\begin{pmatrix}
A&-E\\
-E&B
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\ell
\triangleq
\begin{pmatrix}
C\\-D
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The expanded variance is the joint quadratic
$$
\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\beta)\mid\mathcal F\}
=
\frac{1}{n}
\left{
\operatorname{Var}_R(\widehat Z_0)
+2\ell^\top\beta
+\beta^\top\mathbf H\beta
\right\}.
$$
The matrix $\mathbf H$ is positive semidefinite because it is the covariance Hessian contributed by the RCT score plus the nonnegative OBS variance term. If
$$
\det(\mathbf H)=AB-E^2>0,
$$
then the unique unrestricted joint oracle is
$$
\boxed{
\lambda_{\mathrm{joint}}^*
=
\frac{ED-BC}{AB-E^2},
\qquad
\omega_{\mathrm{joint}}^*
=
\frac{AD-EC}{AB-E^2}
}.
$$
Its minimum variance is
$$
\frac{1}{n}
\left{
\operatorname{Var}_R(\widehat Z_0)
-
\frac{BC^2-2ECD+AD^2}{AB-E^2}
\right}.
$$

The exact RCT pseudo-outcome validity now supplies the simplifying orthogonality:
$$
\mathbb E_R(\Delta\mid X,\mathcal F)=0.
$$
Because $g$ is a fixed measurable function of $X$ given $\mathcal F$,
$$
E
=
\operatorname{Cov}_R(\Delta,g)
=0,
\qquad
D
=
\operatorname{Cov}_R(\widehat Z_0,g)
=
\operatorname{Cov}_R\{\tau(X),g(X)\}.
$$
Thus $\mathbf H=\operatorname{diag}(A,B)$ and the population oracle separates into two axes:
$$
\boxed{
\lambda^*=-\frac{C}{A},
\qquad
\omega^*=\frac{D}{B}
}
$$
when $A>0$ and $B>0$. Equivalently,
$$
\lambda^*
=
-
\frac{
\operatorname{Cov}_R(\widehat Z_0,\widehat Z_1-\widehat Z_0)
}{
\operatorname{Var}_R(\widehat Z_1-\widehat Z_0)
},
$$
and
$$
\omega^*
=
\frac{
\operatorname{Cov}_R\{\tau(X),g(X)\}
}{
\operatorname{Var}_R\{g(X)\}
+
(n/N)\operatorname{Var}_O\{r(X)g(X)\}
}.
$$
The numerator of $\omega^*$ may be written as $\operatorname{Cov}_R(\widehat Z_\lambda,g)$ for any fixed $\lambda$. This observable representation motivates moment estimation, but it is not an observational causal covariance.

If $\mathbf H$ is singular, let $\mathbf H^+$ denote its Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse. Because the displayed objective is a variance, $\ell$ belongs to the range of $\mathbf H$. The complete unrestricted minimizer set is
$$
-\mathbf H^+\ell
+
\operatorname{Null}(\mathbf H),
$$
and the minimum variance is
$$
\frac{1}{n}
\left{
\operatorname{Var}_R(\widehat Z_0)
-
\ell^\top\mathbf H^+\ell
\right}.
$$
After orthogonality, the singular cases become explicit flat directions. If $A=0$, then $\Delta=0$ almost surely and $C=0$, so every $\lambda$ is equivalent. If $B=0$, then both $g$ under $P_R$ and $rg$ under $P_O$ are almost-sure constants with their means matched by transport, so the correction is samplewise zero and $D=0$; every $\omega$ is equivalent. If both vanish, every pair is equivalent.

For a nonempty feasible set $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathbb R^2$, the constrained population oracle minimizes the original joint quadratic over $\mathcal C$. If $\mathbf H$ is positive definite and $\mathcal C$ is closed and convex, the minimizer exists uniquely and is the $\mathbf H$-metric projection of the unrestricted oracle. If $\mathbf H$ is only positive semidefinite, compactness of $\mathcal C$ guarantees attainment, although the minimizer can be nonunique. After population orthogonality, for a rectangle $[L_\lambda,U_\lambda]\times[L_\omega,U_\omega]$ with positive $A$ and $B$, the solution is
$$
\lambda_{\mathcal C}^*
=
\Pi_{[L_\lambda,U_\lambda]}(-C/A),
\qquad
\omega_{\mathcal C}^*
=
\Pi_{[L_\omega,U_\omega]}(D/B).
$$
Coupled constraints require solving the original two-dimensional quadratic program. If $(0,0)\in\mathcal C$, the constrained population objective is weakly no larger than the AIPW objective. This is an oracle comparison, not a finite-sample no-harm guarantee for estimated coefficients.

Under $r=1$ and $P_R^X=P_O^X$,
$$
B
=
(1+n/N)\operatorname{Var}_R(g),
$$
which recovers the common-marginal formula. Under unequal covariate marginals, the same formulas hold with the exact density ratio and
$$
B
=
\operatorname{Var}_R(g)
+
(n/N)\operatorname{Var}_O(rg).
$$

### Fixed-coefficient variance estimation

For $n,N\geq2$, let $V_i^R$ and $V_j^O$ denote the RCT and OBS evaluation observations, respectively, and define
$$
\overline h_R
\triangleq
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n h(V_i^R),
\qquad
\overline h_O
\triangleq
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^N h(V_j^O).
$$
The unbiased sample variances are
$$
s_R^2(h)
\triangleq
\frac{1}{n-1}
\sum_{i=1}^n
\{h(V_i^R)-\overline h_R\}^2,
$$
and
$$
s_O^2(h)
\triangleq
\frac{1}{N-1}
\sum_{j=1}^N
\{h(V_j^O)-\overline h_O\}^2.
$$
For fixed $(\lambda,\omega)$ and functions fitted independently of the evaluation samples,
$$
\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}
\{\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)\}
\triangleq
\frac{s_R^2(\widehat Z_\lambda-\omega g)}{n}
+
\frac{\omega^2s_O^2(rg)}{N}
$$
is conditionally unbiased for the exact conditional variance given $\mathcal F$.

Variance unbiasedness does not by itself give confidence-interval coverage. Consider a triangular array with $n,N\to\infty$ and $n/N\to\kappa\in[0,\infty)$. Suppose, conditional on $\mathcal F$, that the selected fixed scores have uniformly bounded $(2+\delta)$ moments for some $\delta>0$ and that
$$
n\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}
\to
\sigma^2
\in(0,\infty)
$$
in probability. The conditional Lindeberg--Feller theorem and consistency of the two sample variances then give
$$
\frac{
\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)-\theta
}{
\sqrt{
s_R^2(\widehat Z_\lambda-\omega g)/n
+
\omega^2s_O^2(rg)/N
}
}
\rightsquigarrow
N(0,1).
$$
Thus an asymptotic $(1-\alpha)$ Wald interval is
$$
\widehat\theta_r(\lambda,\omega)
\pm
z_{1-\alpha/2}
\left{
\frac{s_R^2(\widehat Z_\lambda-\omega g)}{n}
+
\frac{\omega^2s_O^2(rg)}{N}
\right}^{1/2}.
$$
The interval is not finite-sample exact, and the statement requires the functions and coefficients to be selected independently of the final evaluation observations.

### Estimated density ratio

Now let $\widehat r$ be trained independently of the evaluation samples and included in $\mathcal F$, and assume $\widehat r(X)g(X)\in L_2(P_O)$. Replacing $r$ by $\widehat r$ gives
$$
\widehat\theta_{\widehat r}(\lambda,\omega)
\triangleq
\mathbb E_n[\widehat Z_\lambda]
+
\omega
\left\{
\mathbb E_N[\widehat r(X)g(X)]
-
\mathbb E_n[g(X)]
\right\}.
$$
Define the exact conditional drift
$$
b_{\mathcal F}
\triangleq
\mathbb E_O
\left[
\{\widehat r(X)-r(X)\}g(X)
\right].
$$
Then
$$
\mathbb E
\{\widehat\theta_{\widehat r}(\lambda,\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}
=
\theta+\omega b_{\mathcal F},
$$
so exact conditional unbiasedness holds only when $\omega b_{\mathcal F}=0$. For a nonzero correction coefficient, this requires the exact weighted-moment condition $b_{\mathcal F}=0$; consistency of $\widehat r$ alone does not provide that finite-sample identity. The conditional variance remains
$$
V_{\mathcal F}
\triangleq
\operatorname{Var}
\{\widehat\theta_{\widehat r}(\lambda,\omega)\mid\mathcal F\}
=
\frac{1}{n}
\operatorname{Var}_R(\widehat Z_\lambda-\omega g)
+
\frac{\omega^2}{N}
\operatorname{Var}_O(\widehat r g).
$$
Consequently, its conditional mean squared error for $\theta$ is
$$
V_{\mathcal F}
+
\omega^2b_{\mathcal F}^2.
$$
Without conditioning, the three distinct quantities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Bias}
\{\widehat\theta_{\widehat r}(\lambda,\omega)\}
&=
\mathbb E(\omega b_{\mathcal F}),
\\
\operatorname{Var}
\{\widehat\theta_{\widehat r}(\lambda,\omega)\}
&=
\mathbb E(V_{\mathcal F})
+
\operatorname{Var}(\omega b_{\mathcal F}),
\\
\operatorname{MSE}
\{\widehat\theta_{\widehat r}(\lambda,\omega)\}
&=
\mathbb E(V_{\mathcal F})
+
\mathbb E\{(\omega b_{\mathcal F})^2\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The estimated-ratio error has the exact decomposition
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\theta_{\widehat r}(\lambda,\omega)-\theta
={}&
(\mathbb E_n-\mathbb E_R)
(\widehat Z_\lambda-\omega g)
+
\omega(\mathbb E_N-\mathbb E_O)(rg)
\\
&+
\omega b_{\mathcal F}
+
\omega(\mathbb E_N-\mathbb E_O)
\{(\widehat r-r)g\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Let $h_n=(\widehat r-r)g$. At the $\sqrt n$ scale, a sufficient pair of conditions for the two additional ratio-estimation terms to be negligible is
$$
\sqrt n\,\omega\mathbb E_O(h_n)=o_p(1),
\qquad
|\omega|\sqrt{\frac nN}
\|h_n-\mathbb E_O(h_n)\|_{L_2(P_O)}
=o_p(1).
$$
Indeed, conditional Chebyshev gives
$$
\sqrt n\,\omega(\mathbb E_N-\mathbb E_O)(h_n)
=
O_p
\left(
|\omega|\sqrt{\frac{n}{N}}
\|h_n-\mathbb E_O(h_n)\|_{L_2(P_O)}
\right)
=o_p(1).
$$
If $n/N=O(1)$, the empirical-remainder condition follows from $|\omega|\|h_n\|_{L_2(P_O)}=o_p(1)$. For a fixed finite $\omega$, this provides a convenient sufficient condition, but it does not imply the separate root-$n$ drift condition. Convergence of $\widehat r-r$ alone in $L_2(P_O)$ is not enough when $g$ is unbounded. Sufficient alternatives include bounded $g$, or Hölder-compatible moments satisfying $1/2=1/p+1/q$ together with $\|\widehat r-r\|_{L_p(P_O)}=o_p(1)$ and $\|g\|_{L_q(P_O)}=O_p(1)$.

A model-specific influence-function expansion is needed when the ratio-training drift contributes at first order. In this regime, continue to require the evaluation empirical remainder to be negligible:
$$
|\omega|\sqrt{\frac nN}
\|h_n-\mathbb E_O(h_n)\|_{L_2(P_O)}
=o_p(1).
$$
Let $T_1,\ldots,T_m$ be independent ratio-training observations, independent of both evaluation samples. Suppose that for a mean-zero function $\xi_g$ with finite variance,
$$
\mathbb E\{\xi_g(T)\}=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E\{\xi_g(T)^2\}<\infty,
$$
and
$$
\mathbb E_O(h_n)
=
\frac{1}{m}
\sum_{k=1}^m
\xi_g(T_k)
+
o_p(m^{-1/2}).
$$
Then the expansion of $\widehat\theta_{\widehat r}(\lambda,\omega)-\theta$ contains the additional first-order term
$$
\frac{\omega}{m}
\sum_{k=1}^m
\xi_g(T_k).
$$
If $n/m\to\kappa\in[0,\infty)$ and $\omega$ is fixed and finite, independence of the ratio-training and evaluation samples implies that the $\sqrt n$ asymptotic variance gains the additive component
$$
\kappa\omega^2
\operatorname{Var}\{\xi_g(T)\}.
$$
If the displayed empirical-remainder condition fails, that empirical-process term and its covariances must also be included. If ratio training and evaluation overlap, their first-order terms need not be independent, and the asymptotic variance must also include the corresponding covariance terms. Thus, an evaluation-only sample variance estimator is insufficient whenever density-ratio estimation contributes at first order.

Normalizing $\widehat r$ by its empirical mean does not restore exact finite-sample unbiasedness because it introduces a random ratio. Training $\widehat r$, selecting $(\lambda,\omega)$, and evaluating the selected estimator on the same observations also invalidates the honest conditional calculation. Cross-fitting can reduce direct dependence, but it does not remove the drift $\mathbb E_O\{(\widehat r-r)g\}$ and does not automatically recover the exact independent-split variance formula.

### Population versus empirical tuning

The separation above is a population statement conditional on $\mathcal F$. Although $\operatorname{Cov}_R(\Delta,g)=0$, its sample analogue need not vanish. This distinction produces three different coefficient objects.

1. The **population oracle** uses $(A,B,C,D,E)$ and satisfies $E=0$ exactly.
2. The **population-orthogonality plug-in** estimates $(A,B,C,D)$ but sets $\widehat E=0$ by the proven moment restriction. Its observable estimate of $D$ is the tuning-sample covariance between $Z_0$ and $g$.
3. The **empirical joint minimizer** also estimates $E$ by the tuning-sample covariance between $\Delta$ and $g$. It minimizes the full empirical quadratic and generally differs from the axis plug-in because $\widehat E$ is not exactly zero.

Let the independent tuning samples have sizes $m_R,m_O\geq2$. For tuning-sample functions $h_1,h_2$, let $s_{R,\mathrm{tu}}(h_1,h_2)$ and $s_{O,\mathrm{tu}}(h_1,h_2)$ denote unbiased sample covariances, with $s^2(h)=s(h,h)$. The plug-in moments for an evaluation estimator with sizes $(n,N)$ are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat A&=s_{R,\mathrm{tu}}^2(\Delta),
&
\widehat B&=s_{R,\mathrm{tu}}^2(g)+\frac nN s_{O,\mathrm{tu}}^2(rg),
\\
\widehat C&=s_{R,\mathrm{tu}}(Z_0,\Delta),
&
\widehat D&=s_{R,\mathrm{tu}}(Z_0,g),
\\
\widehat E&=s_{R,\mathrm{tu}}(\Delta,g).
\end{aligned}
$$
The population-orthogonality axis plug-in is
$$
\widehat\lambda_{\mathrm{axis}}
=
-\frac{\widehat C}{\widehat A},
\qquad
\widehat\omega_{\mathrm{axis}}
=
\frac{\widehat D}{\widehat B},
$$
when the denominators pass prespecified positive thresholds. The empirical joint minimizer uses
$$
\widehat{\det}
=
\widehat A\widehat B-\widehat E^2
$$
and, when $\widehat{\det}$ passes its threshold,
$$
\widehat\lambda_{\mathrm{joint}}
=
\frac{
\widehat E\widehat D-\widehat B\widehat C
}{
\widehat{\det}
},
\qquad
\widehat\omega_{\mathrm{joint}}
=
\frac{
\widehat A\widehat D-\widehat E\widehat C
}{
\widehat{\det}
}.
$$
The sample moment estimators are unbiased for their conditional population counterparts, but the nonlinear coefficient ratios are not unbiased estimators of the oracle coefficients.

For a prespecified compact coefficient set $\mathcal C$, both plug-in variants may instead solve
$$
\widehat\beta
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\beta\in\mathcal C}
\left{
2\widehat\ell^\top\beta
+
\beta^\top\widehat{\mathbf H}\beta
\right},
$$
where the joint version uses
$$
\widehat{\mathbf H}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\widehat A&-\widehat E\\
-\widehat E&\widehat B
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\widehat\ell
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\widehat C\\-\widehat D
\end{pmatrix},
$$
and the axis version replaces $\widehat E$ by zero. A reproducible implementation must prespecify thresholds $(t_A,t_B,t_{\det})$. If $\widehat A\leq t_A$, set $\widehat\lambda=0$; if $\widehat B\leq t_B$, set $\widehat\omega=0$; if the joint determinant is at most $t_{\det}$, use the constrained quadratic program or the declared axis fallback instead of dividing by the determinant. Rectangular clipping is valid in closed form only for the axis version.

### Exact three-way honest algorithm

The exact adaptive procedure uses three mutually independent blocks from each source.

1. **Nuisance training.** Fit $\widehat\mu_R$, $\widehat\mu_O$, $g$, and, under unequal covariate marginals, a candidate $\widehat r$ using only the training blocks. Exact finite-sample unbiasedness in the unequal-marginal theorem requires the true $r$, or the exact moment equality $\mathbb E_O(\widehat r g)=\mathbb E_R(g)$.
2. **Coefficient tuning.** Evaluate $Z_0,\Delta,g,$ and $rg$ on independent tuning blocks. Compute the five moments above, apply the prespecified thresholds, and select the axis or joint coefficient in the prespecified feasible set.
3. **Final evaluation.** Hold every fitted function and selected coefficient fixed. On independent final blocks, report
$$
\widehat\theta_r
=
\mathbb E_{n,\mathrm{ev}}
(
\widehat Z_{\widehat\lambda}-\widehat\omega g
)
+
\widehat\omega
\mathbb E_{N,\mathrm{ev}}(rg),
$$
the fixed-selected-coefficient variance estimate
$$
\widehat V_{\mathrm{ev}}
=
\frac{
s_{R,\mathrm{ev}}^2(
\widehat Z_{\widehat\lambda}-\widehat\omega g
)
}{n}
+
\frac{
\widehat\omega^2s_{O,\mathrm{ev}}^2(rg)
}{N},
$$
and, when the conditional Lindeberg and nondegeneracy assumptions hold, the Wald interval
$$
\widehat\theta_r
\pm
z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat V_{\mathrm{ev}}}.
$$
Conditional on the training and tuning blocks, the point estimator is exactly unbiased under the common-marginal model or the exact density ratio, and $\widehat V_{\mathrm{ev}}$ is exactly unbiased for its conditional variance. The Wald interval remains asymptotic.

### Nested outer cross-fitting

Nested outer cross-fitting reuses observations while keeping each scored observation out of its own nuisance and coefficient fits. Partition both sources into paired outer folds using prespecified, data-independent assignments. Alternatively, generate the assignments by independent randomization and include the split randomness in the conditioning sigma-field. For fold $k$, use only the two outer complements and inner cross-fitting within those complements to construct nuisance predictions, estimate the tuning moments, and choose $(\widehat\lambda_k,\widehat\omega_k)$. Refit nuisances on the outer complements, and compute on the untouched paired folds
$$
\widehat\theta_k
=
\mathbb E_{R,k}
(\widehat Z_{\widehat\lambda_k}^{(-k)})
+
\widehat\omega_k
\left{
\mathbb E_{O,k}(r g^{(-k)})
-
\mathbb E_{R,k}(g^{(-k)})
\right}.
$$
For fixed nonnegative fold weights $a_k$ summing to one, set
$$
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{outer}}
=
\sum_k a_k\widehat\theta_k.
$$
Under the common marginal or exact $r$, each fold statistic is conditionally unbiased given its outer complement, so the aggregate is unconditionally unbiased. This statement does not yield an exact variance formula for the aggregate. Outer training complements overlap, and the resulting fold estimators are dependent. In particular, the naive sum of foldwise conditional variance estimates omits cross-fold dependence.

A pooled out-of-fold variance estimator and Wald interval require an additional asymptotic stability theorem. One sufficient high-level route assumes that the fold-specific RCT and OBS scores converge in their respective $L_2$ norms to common deterministic limits, have uniformly bounded $(2+\delta)$ moments, and satisfy a nondegenerate two-sample central limit theorem. For estimated $r$, the negligible-drift conditions must also hold foldwise. Without these assumptions or a separate influence-function calculation, the nested procedure should report empirical replication variability rather than claim a general analytic CI.

### Why adaptive reuse is biased

Independent tuning is necessary even though every fixed coefficient is unbiased. Two score-level counterexamples isolate the problem.

For the $\lambda$ channel, let $X$ be degenerate, $\theta=0$, $Z_0=0$, and let $\Delta$ be a mean-zero Rademacher score. Every fixed $\lambda$ gives the unbiased estimator $\lambda\mathbb E_n(\Delta)$. If the same evaluation sample chooses
$$
\widehat\lambda
=
\operatorname{sign}\{\mathbb E_n(\Delta)\},
$$
then the reported estimate is
$$
\widehat\lambda\mathbb E_n(\Delta)
=
|\mathbb E_n(\Delta)|,
$$
which has positive expectation.

For the $\omega$ channel, let $P_R^X=P_O^X$, let $g(X)$ be a mean-zero Rademacher variable, and take $Z_\lambda=0$ and $\theta=0$. Every fixed $\omega$ gives an unbiased prediction-powered difference. If the same two evaluation samples choose
$$
\widehat\omega
=
\operatorname{sign}
\left[
\mathbb E_N\{g(X)\}
-
\mathbb E_n\{g(X)\}
\right],
$$
the reported correction is the absolute value of that difference and has positive expectation. These counterexamples satisfy the fixed-coefficient mean-zero identities and show that data reuse, rather than nuisance misspecification, creates the bias.

## CATE Estimation

For a fixed candidate CATE function $\widetilde\tau (X)$, define the prediction-powered loss

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\widehat L(\widetilde\tau;\lambda,\omega)
\triangleq{}&
\mathbb E_n
\left[
\{\widehat{Z}_\lambda-\widetilde\tau(X)\}^2
\right]
\\
&+
\omega
\left[
\mathbb E_N
\left[
\{\widehat g_O(X)-\widetilde\tau(X)\}^2
\right]
-
\mathbb E_n
\left[
\{\widehat g_O(X)-\widetilde\tau(X)\}^2
\right]
\right].
\end{aligned}
}
$$

Under $P_R(X)=P_O(X)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L(\widetilde\tau;\lambda,\omega)\}
&=
\mathbb E_R
\left[
\{\widehat{Z}_\lambda-\widetilde\tau(X)\}^2
\right]
\\
&=
\mathbb E_R
\left[
\{\widehat{Z}_\lambda-\tau(X)\}^2
\right]
+
\mathbb E_R
\left[
\{\tau(X)-\widetilde\tau(X)\}^2
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the first term does not depend on $\widetilde\tau$, this loss function doesn't change the population target. 

From
$$
\begin{align*}
& \mathbb E\{\widehat L(\widetilde\tau;\lambda,\omega)\} \\ 
&= \mathbb E_R
\left[
\{\widehat{Z}_\lambda-\tau(X)\}^2
\right]
+
\mathbb E_R
\left[
\{\tau(X)-\widetilde\tau(X)\}^2
\right]\\ 
&= \mathbb{E}_{R}[\{\widehat{Z}_0 + \lambda (\widehat{Z}_1 - \widehat{Z}_0)\} - \tau(X)\}^2] + \mathbb E_R
\left[
\{\tau(X)-\widetilde\tau(X)\}^2
\right] \\ 
&= \| \widehat{Z}_0 - \tau \|_R^2 + 2\lambda \mathbb{E}_{R}[\{\widehat{Z_0} - \tau(X)\}\times \{ \widehat{Z}_1 - \widehat{Z_0}  \}] + \lambda^2\|\widehat{Z}_1 - \widehat{Z}_0 \|_R^2 + \| \tau - \widetilde{\tau} \|^2_R,
\end{align*}
$$
we can have 
$$
\lambda^\star
\triangleq 
-
\frac{
\mathbb E_R[
\{\widehat{Z}_0-\tau(X)\}(\widehat{Z}_1-\widehat{Z}_0)
]
}{
\mathbb E_R[
(\widehat{Z}_1-\widehat{Z}_0)^2
]
} = - \frac{ \operatorname{Cov}_R(\widehat{Z}_0,\widehat{Z}_1-\widehat{Z}_0) }{ \operatorname{Var}_R(\widehat{Z}_1-\widehat{Z}_0) },
$$

which is the same $\lambda^{\star}$ used in the ATE estimation. 

Conditioning on $X=x$, we can have 
$$
\lambda^\star(X) \triangleq  - \frac{ \operatorname{Cov}_R(\widehat{Z}_0,\widehat{Z}_1-\widehat{Z}_0 \mid X) }{ \operatorname{Var}_R(\widehat{Z}_1-\widehat{Z}_0 \mid X) }.
$$




### Choice of $\omega$

Conditional on the fitted functions and under $P_R(X)=P_O(X)$, the expected CATE loss is flat in $\omega$ (i.e., the expected CATE loss is not a function of $\omega$):

$$
\mathbb E\{\widehat L(\widetilde\tau;\lambda,\omega)\}
=
\mathbb E_R
\left[
\{\widehat Z_\lambda-\widetilde\tau(X)\}^2
\right].
$$

Thus, the population CATE risk does not identify an optimal $\omega$. As a practical default that does not require a preliminary CATE estimator, we use the ATE-optimal coefficient
$$
\omega_{\mathrm{ATE}}^\star
=
\frac{
\operatorname{Cov}_R
\{\widehat Z_\lambda,\widehat g_O(X)\}
}{
(1+n/N)
\operatorname{Var}_R
\{\widehat g_O(X)\}
}.
$$

In oracle simulations, where $\tau(X)$ is known, we may also consider the fixed-loss-variance coefficient

$$
\omega_{\mathrm{LV}}^\star(\tau;\lambda)
=
\frac{
\operatorname{Cov}_R
\left(
\{\widehat Z_\lambda-\tau(X)\}^2,
\{\widehat g_O(X)-\tau(X)\}^2
\right)
}{
(1+n/N)
\operatorname{Var}_R
\left[
\{\widehat g_O(X)-\tau(X)\}^2
\right]
}.
$$

This coefficient minimizes $\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat L(\tau;\lambda,\omega)\}$ over fixed values of $\omega$, conditional on the fitted nuisance functions. Because it depends on the unknown $\tau$, it serves primarily as an oracle benchmark.

Alternatively, we can select $(\lambda,\omega)$ empirically using prediction-powered cross-validation. Specifically, we can define 
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat R(\lambda,\omega)
\triangleq{}&
\mathbb E_{n}
\left[
\{\widehat{Z}_{\lambda^{\star}_{\mathrm{ATE}}}-\widehat\tau_{\lambda,\omega}(X)\}^2
\right]\\
&+
\left[
\mathbb E_{N}
\left[
\{\widehat g_O(X)-\widehat\tau_{\lambda,\omega}(X)\}^2
\right]
-
\mathbb E_{n}
\left[
\{\widehat g_O(X)-\widehat\tau_{\lambda,\omega}(X)\}^2
\right]
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
Under $P_R(X)=P_O(X)$ and held-out evaluation,

$$
\mathbb E\{\widehat R(\lambda,\omega)\}
=
\mathbb E_R
\left[
\{\widehat\tau_{\lambda,\omega}(X)-\tau(X)\}^2
\right]
+
\mathbb E_R
\left[
\operatorname{Var}(Z_{\lambda^{\star}_{\mathrm{ATE}}}\mid X)
\right].
$$

Because the second term is common to all candidates, minimizing $\mathbb E\{\widehat R(\lambda,\omega)\}$ is equivalent to minimizing the MSE $\mathbb E_R
\left[
\{\widehat\tau_{\lambda,\omega}(X)-\tau(X)\}^2
\right]$. Therefore, we may work with
$$
(\widehat\lambda,\widehat\omega)
=
\arg\min_{(\lambda,\omega)\in\mathcal G}
\widehat R(\lambda,\omega).
$$

### CATE under covariate shift: loss-targeted transport

The preceding CATE loss uses the equality $P_R(X)=P_O(X)$. This subsection gives the corresponding construction when the covariate marginals differ. Write

$$
P_R^X \neq P_O^X,
\qquad
P_R^X \ll P_O^X,
\qquad
r(x)\triangleq \frac{dP_R^X}{dP_O^X}(x),
$$

and, conditional on the fitted functions, abbreviate

$$
g(X)\triangleq \widehat g_O(X),
\qquad
t(X)\triangleq \widetilde\tau(X),
\qquad
G_t(X)\triangleq \{g(X)-t(X)\}^2.
$$

All finite-sample expectation statements below are conditional on the fitted nuisance functions, the candidate class, and any fitted weight function. Thus, these objects must be fixed independently of the evaluation observations. We also assume the displayed random variables are measurable and integrable.

#### Density ratio from domain-classification odds

Pool RCT and OBS covariates and define a domain indicator $S$, with $S=1$ for RCT and $S=0$ for OBS. Let

$$
\pi\triangleq \mathbb P(S=1),
\qquad
s(x)\triangleq \mathbb P(S=1\mid X=x).
$$

Bayes' rule gives

$$
r(x)
=
\frac{dP_R^X}{dP_O^X}(x)
=
\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}
\frac{s(x)}{1-s(x)}.
$$

Therefore, a probabilistic domain classifier yields the plug-in ratio

$$
\widehat r_{\mathrm{cls}}(x)
=
\frac{1-\widehat\pi}{\widehat\pi}
\frac{\widehat s(x)}{1-\widehat s(x)}.
$$

For an unweighted pooled classifier trained on $m_R$ RCT covariates and $m_O$ OBS covariates,

$$
\widehat\pi=\frac{m_R}{m_R+m_O},
\qquad
\frac{1-\widehat\pi}{\widehat\pi}=\frac{m_O}{m_R}.
$$

If class weights or resampling alter the classifier's effective class prior, the prior-odds factor must be corrected accordingly. This representation also requires covariate overlap. Probability clipping can stabilize the estimated ratio, but it generally changes the exact transported target and must therefore be analyzed as an approximation.

#### Exact loss-targeted balance

For a fixed weight function $w$ and candidate $t$, define the weighted prediction-powered loss

$$
\widehat L_w(t;\lambda,\omega)
\triangleq
\mathbb E_n\left[\{\widehat Z_\lambda-t(X)\}^2\right]
+
\omega
\left\{
\mathbb E_N\left[w(X)G_t(X)\right]
-
\mathbb E_n\left[G_t(X)\right]
\right\}.
$$

Let $\mathcal T$ be a fixed class of measurable CATE candidates. The balance condition needed for this loss is not balance for every function of $X$. It is balance for the generated loss class

$$
\mathcal G(\mathcal T;g)
\triangleq
\left\{G_t=(g-t)^2:t\in\mathcal T\right\}.
$$

**Proposition 1 (exact loss-targeted transport).** Suppose that

$$
\widehat Z_\lambda\in L_2(P_R)
$$

and

$$
\mathbb E_R(\widehat Z_\lambda\mid X)=\tau(X)
$$

and that, for every $t\in\mathcal T$, the integrability conditions

$$
t\in L_2(P_R^X),
\qquad
G_t\in L_1(P_R^X),
\qquad
wG_t\in L_1(P_O^X),
$$

hold together with the balance condition

$$
\mathbb E_O\{w(X)G_t(X)\}
=
\mathbb E_R\{G_t(X)\}
\qquad
\text{for every }t\in\mathcal T.
$$

Then, for every fixed $t\in\mathcal T$,

$$
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_w(t;\lambda,\omega)\}
=
Q_\lambda
+
\mathbb E_R\left[\{t(X)-\tau(X)\}^2\right],
$$

where

$$
Q_\lambda
\triangleq
\mathbb E_R\left[
\operatorname{Var}_R(\widehat Z_\lambda\mid X)
\right]
$$

does not depend on $t$ or $\omega$. Consequently, whenever the relevant minimizers exist, the population minimizers of the weighted loss over $\mathcal T$ coincide with the $L_2(P_R^X)$ projections of $\tau$ onto $\mathcal T$.

**Proof.** The balance condition makes the expected correction equal to zero. Moreover, conditional pseudo-outcome validity gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_R\left[\{\widehat Z_\lambda-t(X)\}^2\right]
={}&
\mathbb E_R\left[
\operatorname{Var}_R(\widehat Z_\lambda\mid X)
\right]
+
\mathbb E_R\left[
\{\mathbb E_R(\widehat Z_\lambda\mid X)-t(X)\}^2
\right]
\\
={}&
Q_\lambda
+
\mathbb E_R\left[\{\tau(X)-t(X)\}^2\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Combining these two identities proves the claim. $\square$

The exact density ratio $w=r$ satisfies the balance condition for every integrable $G_t$, since

$$
\mathbb E_O\{r(X)G_t(X)\}=\mathbb E_R\{G_t(X)\}.
$$

The proposition is more general than exact density-ratio recovery: any $w$ that balances the particular loss class $\mathcal G(\mathcal T;g)$ preserves the same population minimizers.

#### Finite sufficient moments for a linear sieve

Consider the fixed linear sieve

$$
t_\beta(x)=b(x)^\top\beta,
\qquad
b(x)\in\mathbb R^p,
\qquad
\beta\in\mathcal B\subseteq\mathbb R^p.
$$

For this class,

$$
G_{t_\beta}(X)
=
g(X)^2
-
2\beta^\top b(X)g(X)
+
\beta^\top b(X)b(X)^\top\beta.
$$

Therefore, the two moment conditions

$$
\mathbb E_O\{w(X)b(X)g(X)\}
=
\mathbb E_R\{b(X)g(X)\},
$$

and

$$
\mathbb E_O\{w(X)b(X)b(X)^\top\}
=
\mathbb E_R\{b(X)b(X)^\top\}
$$

are sufficient to make

$$
\mathbb E_O\{w(X)G_{t_\beta}(X)\}
-
\mathbb E_R\{G_{t_\beta}(X)\}
$$

constant in $\beta$. Hence they preserve the population minimizer over $\mathcal B$. To preserve the absolute loss identity in Proposition 1, rather than only its minimizer, one additionally needs

$$
\mathbb E_O\{w(X)g(X)^2\}
=
\mathbb E_R\{g(X)^2\}.
$$

Thus, balance of $g^2$ is needed for the absolute loss value, but not for the minimizer. The normalization $\mathbb E_O\{w(X)\}=1$ is a separate weight convention and stability condition. It should not be silently substituted for any of the loss-targeted moments above.

A convenient finite feature vector is therefore

$$
\phi_g(X)
=
\left(
1,
g(X)^2,
b(X)g(X),
\operatorname{vech}\{b(X)b(X)^\top\}
\right).
$$

The $g^2$ coordinate may be omitted when the sole target is preservation of the sieve minimizer.

#### Approximate minimax balance and uniform drift

Exact balance may be infeasible or unstable. Define the population imbalance over the generated loss class by

$$
\Delta_{\mathcal T}(w)
\triangleq
\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
\left|
\mathbb E_O\{w(X)G_t(X)\}
-
\mathbb E_R\{G_t(X)\}
\right|.
$$

For every fixed $w$ and $\omega$,

$$
\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
\left|
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_w(t;\lambda,\omega)\}
-
Q_\lambda
-
\mathbb E_R\left[\{t(X)-\tau(X)\}^2\right]
\right|
\leq
|\omega|\Delta_{\mathcal T}(w).
$$

This is a deterministic consequence of the loss definition and does not require an asymptotic argument. If a weighted population minimizer $t_w^\star$ and a target-risk minimizer $t_{\mathcal T}^\star$ both exist, then

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_R\left[\{t_w^\star(X)-\tau(X)\}^2\right]
-
\mathbb E_R\left[\{t_{\mathcal T}^\star(X)-\tau(X)\}^2\right]
\\
&\qquad\leq
2|\omega|\Delta_{\mathcal T}(w).
\end{aligned}
$$

Indeed, one uniform-error term compares the weighted criterion with target risk at $t_w^\star$, a second compares them at $t_{\mathcal T}^\star$, and optimality of $t_w^\star$ makes the intervening weighted-risk difference nonpositive.

On an independent balance sample, a generic stabilized minimax construction is

$$
\widehat w
\in
\arg\min_{
w\in\mathcal W:\,
w(x)\geq 0,\,
\mathbb E_{N,\mathrm{bal}}(w)=1
}
\left[
\widehat\Delta_{\mathcal T}(w)^2
+
\gamma\mathbb E_{N,\mathrm{bal}}(w^2)
\right],
$$

where

$$
\widehat\Delta_{\mathcal T}(w)
\triangleq
\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}
\left|
\mathbb E_{N,\mathrm{bal}}\{w(X)G_t(X)\}
-
\mathbb E_{n,\mathrm{bal}}\{G_t(X)\}
\right|.
$$

Here, $\mathcal W$ is a fixed class of measurable weight functions whose fitted members can be evaluated out of sample, such as a parameterized exponential-tilting class. The optimization estimates a function in $\mathcal W$, not an unrelated free weight for each balance observation. The first term controls worst-case loss-target drift and the second controls weight dispersion. An upper bound $w(x)\leq w_{\max}$ may also be imposed. For the finite linear sieve, the supremum can be replaced by a norm of the discrepancies in the finite moments above. Exact finite balance may fail when the target moments lie outside the feasible weighted convex hull, in which case the approximate problem is the appropriate estimand-specific target.

#### Riesz formulation

The transport functional

$$
T(h)\triangleq \mathbb E_R\{h(X)\}
$$

has density ratio $r$ as its $L_2(P_O^X)$ Riesz representer when $r\in L_2(P_O^X)$, because

$$
\mathbb E_O\{r(X)h(X)\}=T(h).
$$

At the population level, $r$ minimizes

$$
\alpha
\mapsto
\mathbb E_O\{\alpha(X)^2\}
-
2\mathbb E_R\{\alpha(X)\}
$$

over any subset of $L_2(P_O^X)$ containing $r$. A restricted empirical Riesz estimator is

$$
\widehat\alpha
\in
\arg\min_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}
\left[
\mathbb E_{N,\mathrm{bal}}\{\alpha(X)^2\}
-
2\mathbb E_{n,\mathrm{bal}}\{\alpha(X)\}
\right].
$$

For $\alpha_\eta(X)=\phi(X)^\top\eta$, if the empirical target moment lies in the column space of the empirical Gram matrix, a minimum-norm solution is

$$
\widehat\eta
=
\left[
\mathbb E_{N,\mathrm{bal}}\{\phi(X)\phi(X)^\top\}
\right]^+
\mathbb E_{n,\mathrm{bal}}\{\phi(X)\},
$$

where $M^+$ denotes the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse. The first-order condition balances all directions represented by $\phi$. In finite samples, compatibility can fail if the empirical Gram matrix is singular while the empirical target moment has a component in its null space; ridge regularization then gives a well-defined approximate solution. Also, unrestricted Riesz weights may be negative. Nonnegative calibration or minimax weights are usually safer when the weights multiply a squared loss.

#### Why same-sample exact balance can erase the correction

**Proposition 2 (same-sample cancellation).** Suppose the same RCT and OBS observations are used both to fit weights and to optimize over $t_\beta$. If the fitted weights satisfy

$$
\mathbb E_N\{w(X)G_{t_\beta}(X)\}
=
\mathbb E_n\{G_{t_\beta}(X)\}
\qquad
\text{for every }\beta\in\mathcal B,
$$

then the empirical correction is identically zero for every candidate, so the fitted CATE function is independent of $\omega$. If only the two minimizer-preserving sieve moments are balanced exactly, the empirical correction is constant in $\beta$, and the fitted minimizer is again independent of $\omega$.

**Proof.** Under full balance, the term in braces in the definition of $\widehat L_w$ is zero pointwise in $\beta$. Under minimizer-preserving balance, expansion of $G_{t_\beta}$ shows that the coefficients of the linear and quadratic terms in $\beta$ cancel, leaving only the empirical discrepancy in $g^2$, which is constant in $\beta$. $\square$

This cancellation is an algebraic reuse effect, not evidence that covariate shift has disappeared. Weights should therefore be learned on a separate balance block, or outside the fold on which candidate losses are optimized and compared.

#### Honest and cross-fitted implementation

An exact honest implementation can use five roles for observations:

1. Fit $\widehat\mu_R$, $\widehat\mu_O$, $g$, and any fixed basis $b$ on a nuisance-training block.
2. Fit $\widehat r_{\mathrm{cls}}$, a Riesz weight function, or a calibrated/minimax weight function on a separate balance block, treating $g$ and $b$ as fixed, and retain the fitted function for out-of-sample evaluation on all later blocks.
3. Train the candidate functions $t_{\lambda,\omega}$ on a candidate-training block using the fixed weights.
4. Select $(\lambda,\omega)$ on a validation block using a common held-out prediction-powered score.
5. Report final risk or a final estimand on an untouched evaluation block.

Nested outer cross-fitting can recycle observations while preserving the same logic: for each outer held-out fold, every nuisance, balance weight, candidate, and tuning coefficient used on that fold must be trained without its observations. Fold assignments should be prespecified or generated independently, with the split randomness included in the conditioning sigma-field. Because outer training samples overlap, a naive sum of foldwise variance estimators is not automatically a valid variance estimator for the aggregated cross-fitted procedure. Any confidence interval requires a separate stability or influence-function argument.

When $\widehat w$ is estimated rather than equal to $r$, exact target preservation is replaced by the drift bound above. A consistency or inference theorem must therefore control the relevant loss-class imbalance, weight dispersion, candidate-class complexity, and the effects of nuisance and tuning estimation. Honest splitting prevents direct adaptive reuse, but it does not by itself make those errors negligible.

#### Auto-DML caveat

Calling a weight learner ``Auto-DML'' does not make the present weighted loss Neyman orthogonal. For a perturbation $w+\varepsilon\delta w$, the Gateaux derivative of the expected loss correction at $\varepsilon=0$ is

$$
\left.
\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}
\mathbb E\{\widehat L_{w+\varepsilon\delta w}(t;\lambda,\omega)\}
\right|_{\varepsilon=0}
=
\omega\mathbb E_O\{\delta w(X)G_t(X)\},
$$

which is generally nonzero. Therefore, a generic Riesz or Auto-DML learner does not automatically give double robustness, root-$n$ inference, or immunity to density-ratio error for this loss. Such conclusions would require a newly derived orthogonal score or explicit rate and influence-function conditions for the loss-targeted drift. Until then, the precise term is *loss-targeted Riesz balancing*, not an Auto-DML estimator.

#### Recommended hybrid estimator and theorem sequence

A practical primary estimator is a hybrid of domain classification and loss-targeted calibration. First obtain $\widehat r_{\mathrm{cls}}$. Then, on the independent balance block, tilt it toward the moments generated by the selected CATE class:

$$
w_\eta(x)
=
\frac{
\widehat r_{\mathrm{cls}}(x)
\exp\{\eta^\top\phi_g(x)\}
}{
\mathbb E_{N,\mathrm{bal}}
\left[
\widehat r_{\mathrm{cls}}(X)
\exp\{\eta^\top\phi_g(X)\}
\right]
}.
$$

The parameter $\eta$ can be chosen by exact calibration when feasible, or by the stabilized minimax objective otherwise. The classifier supplies global overlap information, while calibration targets the particular loss directions that determine the CATE learner. Experiments should separately compare exact-oracle $r$, classifier-only weights, balancing-only weights, and the hybrid.

The recommended theorem-development order is:

1. exact target preservation under loss-class balance;
2. finite sufficient moments and minimizer preservation for the linear sieve;
3. approximate uniform drift and excess-risk bounds;
4. an honest held-out selection result for $(\lambda,\omega)$ over a fixed or controlled candidate class;
5. a rate or influence-function result that includes estimated weights, nuisances, and tuning.

Domain-classification odds, generic covariate balancing, and Riesz estimation are established tools and are not claimed here as new. The potential project contribution is their loss-targeted specialization to the generated class $\{(g-t)^2:t\in\mathcal T\}$ and their coupling with prediction-powered CATE learning and held-out $(\lambda,\omega)$ selection. A final novelty claim requires an explicit literature comparison.
